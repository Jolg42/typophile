This lettering is a pastiche of styles. Note for example the median spurs -- found in many wild west fonts --and the foxtail terminals There are some fonts that catch some of the flavor of the lettering. For example, Exocet by Jonathan Barnbrook [http://www.emigre.com/EF.php?fid=95](http://web.archive.org/web/20150415001414/http:/www.emigre.com/EF.php?fid=95 "http://www.emigre.com/EF.php?fid=95") and the Dogma movie font, a fan effort as Avqest [http://www.fontspace.com/gemfonts/avqest](http://web.archive.org/web/20150415001414/http:/www.fontspace.com/gemfonts/avqest "http://www.fontspace.com/gemfonts/avqest"). Also some other fonts by Jonathan Barnbrook, including Mason [http://www.emigre.com/EF.php?fid=104](http://web.archive.org/web/20150415001414/http:/www.emigre.com/EF.php?fid=104 "http://www.emigre.com/EF.php?fid=104") and Jeremy Dooley's Newcomen [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/insigne/newcomen/](http://web.archive.org/web/20150415001414/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/insigne/newcomen/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/insigne/newcomen/") Don

Can't agree more with Don. Definitely custom (and by [St Bernardine](http://web.archive.org/web/20150415001414/http:/www.stbernadine.com/clients/evil-spirits-distillery)). Reminds me also on construction this [previous discussion](http://web.archive.org/web/20150415001414/http:/typophile.com/node/114413) too: initials /E and /S from one side, /V, /I, /L, /P, /R and /T (cross-shaped) from the other.
