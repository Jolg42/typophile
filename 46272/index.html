I think I found the answer. I looked in a German book of Schreibschriften and found a font called “Prima”, which my notes showed was also included in the Jaspert book. It was a Stempel typeface from 1957, designed by Martin Hermersdorf. The text says that this “pen-drawn type was used by the Moritz Diesterweg educational publishing house in reading primers”. I overlooked it before, because it was included in the scripts, and I thought it was an italic face. - Mike Yanega

Anonymous @ 14.Jun.2008 7.04pm
Nice job, Mike! Just confirming: [Global Type](http://web.archive.org/web/20080615181526/http:/www.global-type.org/) also credits [Karlgeorg Hoefer](http://web.archive.org/web/20080615181526/http:/www.typophile.com/wiki/Karlgeorg%252BHoefer) with being the designer of Prima. Martin Hermersdorf was a student of [Rudolf Koch](http://web.archive.org/web/20080615181526/http:/www.typophile.com/wiki/Rudolf%252BKoch). He reworked Koch’s [Offenbacher Schrift](http://web.archive.org/web/20080615181526/http:/de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offenbacher_Schrift), a German Kurrentschrift to be written with the broad-nipped pen. Bavarian schools used this ‘Koch-Hermersdorf-Schrift’ as a secondary script in the 1950s. Hermersdorf also is the author of several books and was a member of the Iserlohner Schreibkreis. As far as I know, he hasn’t designed any typefaces.
