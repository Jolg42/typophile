Unicase typeface (school project)

Hi,

I am designing a unicase typeface for my Typography class. It is called ‘Cutting Edge’ for now—hopefully I’ll come up with something less cheesy down the road.

I was hoping to get some critique and feedback; general comments regarding aesthetics, which letters work and which don’t, which of the two versions you prefer, and any other sage advice the type pros of this forum might have for me.

Version 1 has been built from two basic shapes, a square (denoting a “pixel”), and a triangle (“half a pixel”). Version 2 evolved from the first, with an additional rounded shape to smooth things out. Even though it shares certain characteristics with pixel fonts, they are both intended as display fonts (set at 24 points or larger). Mainly because there is no such thing as half a pixel but also because legibility suffers at small point sizes.

The spacing and kerning are arbitrary for now. Any comments are welcome.

Edit: Attached pixel-version.pdf (November 13)

Sëftos Parathenia

This is a font designed for an invented alphabet, so designing a font for it is a little tricky with no examples to follow. (If you’re interested, you can read a little about the fictional history of the script and find a sample manuscript here).

I didn’t know about OpenType when I designed this font, so the alternate letter forms are all just placed in different positions to be typed by the user. I’d like to design an OpenType font to make the replacement automatic. Anyway, it’s been a little while since I actually made this font, but I just recently found Typophile and thought I’d ask for opinions. I’ll explain the sample PDF (and GIF) so nobody will be confused. It’s contents are, from top to bottom:

  • Font Name (Sëftos Parathenia, in the Sëftos script)
  • Three lines containing all the letters, with alternates
  • Two lines with groups of similar letters
  • A sample sentence
  • Four columns containing a poem
  • And finally, figures 0-9 at the very bottom

Thanks in advance.
