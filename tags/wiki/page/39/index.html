Optima

Indices : Typefaces : Optima

Optima, one of Hermann Zapf’s most successful typefaces, was released by the D. Stempel AG in 1958.

In 1950, Zapf made his first sketches for his “serifless roman” while visiting the Santa Croce church in Florence. He sketched letters from grave plates that had been cut about 1530, and as he had no other paper with him at the time, the sketches were done on two 1000 lire bank notes. These letters from the floor of the church inspired Optima, a typeface that is classically roman in proportion and character, but without serifs. The letterforms were designed in the proportions of the Golden Ratio.

In 1952, after careful legibility testing, the first drawings were finished. The type was cut by Stempel’s renowned punchcutter, August Rosenberger. Optima was also produced in matrices for the Linotype Machine. With the clear, simple elegance of its sans serif forms and the warmly human touches of its tapering stems, this family has proved popular around the world.

Optima is the typeface used for the text on the Vietnam Veterans’ Memorial in Washington, D.C.

In 2002, Hermann Zapf and Akira Kobayashi developed Optima nova, an expansion and redesign of the Optima family. The Linotype website has complete overviews of the original Optima type family, as well as the Optima nova fonts.

ITC Kabel

In 1976, Victor Caruso revived Rudolf Koch’s 1927 Kabel for ITC. The design was a significant departure from the original, with a massive increase in x-height. See the Typowiki Kabel entry for more information.

Linotype Univers

Indicies : Typefaces : Linotype Univers

During the 1990s, Adrian Frutiger worked together with Linotype to expand and update his classic Univers family. The result, Linotype Univers, includes 63 different weights, each drawn carefully to ensure compatibility with all the others.

The original Univers type family can be found online here. Linotype Univers can be found on this overview page.

MetaDesign

Indices

Large international design studio started in Berlin by Erik Spiekermann in 1979.

The typeface Meta was originally designed for the German Post Office in 1985 and called PT 55, but never introduced. It was later redigitized for the studio’s own communications, then licensed to FontShop International and exploded into one of the best selling and most used typefaces of the 1990s. It is still the best-selling font in the FontFont library.

URL: http://www.metadesign.com/

Neue Helvetica

Indicies : Typefaces : Neue Helvetica

In 1983, Neue Helvetica was released as an updated version of Linotype’s Helvetica. It includes more weights and styles, making the classic type family more consistant.

Helvetica is still one of the most widley used typefaces of all time; although it is loved by many, it is hated by some. There is no shortage of commentary on the typeface, and there has even been a book published by Lars Muller Publishers entitled “Helvetica: Homage to a Typeface” dedicated to the famous type family.

Linotype’s website includes an overview of all of the original Helvetica font weights, and all of the Neue Helvetica font weights as well.
