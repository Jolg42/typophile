CheapProFonts is 1 year old!

CheapProFonts offers a wide selection of mainly display-fonts with a large character set supporting 65 languages requiring more that the basic A-Z.
(Check out the long list of supported languages here: http://www.cheapprofonts.com/Languages )

What I have done is to team up with several well-known freefont designers (like Ray Larabie, Nick Curtis, Brian Kent and Derek Vogelpohl), and use their fonts as a starting point to make updated professional quality multilingual Unicode fonts - and then offer them at a very low price and with very generous licensing terms.
(Check out the license here: http://www.cheapprofonts.com/License )

I started building the library one year ago, and have managed to rework and release one new font almost every week through 2008! The library is continually expanded with new releases, and new designers. All the reworking is done under agreement with the original designers, and they receive royalties from the sales.
(Check out a complete overview of the fonts here: http://www.cheapprofonts.com/ALL_Fonts )

I hope many “foreign language” users find CheapProFonts a valuable resource for cheap and cheerful display fonts supporting their specific language needs.

A Kewl Story and a sweet release

A Kewl Story was produced to illustrate the use of Kewl Script font, one of the 2 new Sudtipos releases. All illustrations were specially done by the argentinian artist Catnez . Grab it!

Kewl Script is the result of being caught in the afterimage of one design project while conceptualizing another one. Just before finishing the final tests on Mrs Blackfort, the first of what became a long series of Charles Bluemlein fonts, some of the letters began morphing differently in my mind. The idea was to go on the heavier and more playful side, but with a South American sign letterer’s twist, rather than just good handwriting. I did some sketching, took some notes, then got busy with other projects. Some of that stuff eventually seeped into Candy Script and, to a lesser extent, the Whomp font. But it was only a matter of time before I got back to the original concept and finished it. Kewl Script is ideal for food packaging, book and music covers, magazines, and window splashes.

///////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////
A sweet release - Sugar Pie
///////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////

I called it Sugar Pie because it has a sweet taste, quite similar to Candy Script, mostly because of its round-to-sharp terminal concept. This in turn echoes the concept of the clean brush scripts found in the different film type processes of late 1960s and early 1970s.

While Candy Script’s main visual appeal counts on the loops, swashes, and stroke extensions working within a concept of casual form variation, Sugar Pie is artistically a straight forward packaging typeface. Its many ligatures and alternates are just as visually effective as Candy Script’s, but in a subtler and less pronounced fashion. The alternates and ligatures features of Sugar Pie contain quite a few nice variations on the main character set. Use them to achieve the right degree of softness you desire for your design.

The Sugar Pie PDF specimen illustrates some possible uses of the font.

Newt Serif released with Veer

Hi! How’s it going! I’m excited. Because my first font is now on the market with Veer!

I developed Newt Serif with the help of the Typophile Critique forum ( here). After a long time, it’s finally come to fruition. The character set includes Basic Latin, Latin-1, and Latin Extended-A codepages, along with a few other characters here and there. There are four weights – Light, Regular, Demi, and Bold, each with an italic. Thanks for the help, everyone! I hope you like it.
