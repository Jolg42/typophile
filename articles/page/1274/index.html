First of all, hello everyone. I’ll come clean that this is my first post, but I have community aspirations… honest. I’ve had a long look around and I love what I see here on the boards.

Please have at me on this one. I’m quite close to the project, which has worked both for and against its success. So far as the type design has gone, I’d love the eyes of a master or two before it hits signage and letterpress plates.

I’d very much appreciate a technical focus, which is why I’ve kept the background/design brief ambiguous (for now). I think there’s plenty to discuss in the type itself. A few relevant details:

  • I drew the type from scratch in response to other identity elements. The proportions of the H and the 45-degree cuts were my starting points in common.
  • The application will include letterpress cards, glass etching, and metalwork.
  • Screen readability is very important in the final product. I’d very much like advice towards that goal.

I’m looking forward to your thoughts. Please, tease apart the nuances, chew on it, spit it back at me. Below the image is an attached version at a larger scale in b+w.

The NY Times: Oscar's Acceptance Speech Statistics

An interesting typographic chart on the front page of “The Arts” section in to-day’s NY Times (p. E1). In that chart “each word is scaled proportionally to the number of times it has been used in acceptance speeches at the last six Academy Award ceremonies.” The largest-size word is… “ thank ”. All words in the chart are set in Matthew Carter’s excellent Times Cheltenham. The chart was designed by Jonathan Corum of The New York Times.

The Process Type Foundry announces Maple

—
We’re pleased to announce the arrival of our latest typeface Maple.
A distinct departure from our recent releases, Maple is a warm grotesque
with modern sensibilities and historical influences from the 19th and
early 20th centuries. Suited for both text and display settings, the
complete family contains four weights, companion italics, rugged charm
and ostentatious flair.

Maple is available for license as a complete family, in packages
containing two weights each, or as single weights. Both Mac and Windows
purchasers will receive two formats: OpenType (which includes extended
language support) and traditional PostScript Type 1.

—
See more of Maple:
