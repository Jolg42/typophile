The Power of the Quote

The quote is a tricky, powerful “shape-shifter” of a character, with abilities way beyond its formal uses for marking verbal expressions, titles, etc. We’ve all seen it used to mean “not exactly”, or even “not really, but this way you can’t sue me”, when a product is advertised to do something it doesn’t. I’ve also seen it used to mean “not exactly but there’s something to it”: during a visit to the Cayman Islands I saw a car rental place with a large “JEEP ONLY” sign, in quotes; it actually had other vehicles for rent too, but/so wanted to succinctly state: “This is the place for renting a jeep, but we have other stuff too”*. And during this past spring break I saw the quote used thus:

_
Can you guess what it’s doing there?

The St. Bride Library needs your help

Apologies if you are all already aware of this; I wasn’t until a friend alerted me to it today, which is why I thought I’d post it here & now.

If you feel you can spare a donation (of any size) for a cause dear to most typophiles’ hearts, please visit the link below.

St Bride Library - The real story
It has been apparent for some time that St Bride Library’s facilities in Bride Lane are in need of major improvement. Since the library returned to the control of the governors of St Bride Foundation on 1 April 2004, plans have been developed to radically improve the accommodation and the services offered, and those plans have now received planning consent.
Recently there has been some publicity suggesting that the library might have to close (‘£7m funds needed to save library’ - BBC News 6.1.07), but the £7m sought is not simply ‘To save the library’ but is the cost of this major refurbishment of the building, including:
. Building of new exhibition and reading rooms within the present lightwell void with a four-fold increase in the number of books on open access to readers.
. Refurbishment of the existing reading room as a special collections area and return of the Blades Library to its pristine Victorian state as the heart of the collection.
. Creation of a working demonstration area for the artefact collection with glass-screened viewing zone, coupled with refurbishment of the raked Victorian classroom adjacent to the demonstration area.
. Installation of a passenger lift serving most levels of the building, augmented by stair lifts, and rationalization of the numerous floor levels to improve access for all.
. Provision of a dedicated book lift between stacks and reading room.
. Creation of a BS5454:2000 compliant archive storage area.
. Provision of a substantial ground-floor cafe / bar area and an additional meeting room.
. Improvements to the theatre area to provide a multi-use theatrical and conference facility.
. Imposing new entrance from Bride Lane.
After the improvements, the St Bride Foundation’s total business model is a healthy one and the future bright; without them the library is expensive to run and cannot continue indefinitely.
So, the question ‘Is the St Bride Library in danger of closing?’ is not straightforward. Firstly, it is intended that the Library will close for the period of the building works in any event; the real question is ‘Will it reopen afterwards?’. Exciting plans have been prepared to radically improve an old and quirky building, and those plans have been granted planning permission, with final approval given only last month. The money to carry out the works is now needed: application has been made to the Heritage Lottery Fund for half the sum required and there is a fundraising strategy in place for the remainder.
Fundraising is beginning to show results, but the pace needs to quicken so that the project can be assured of success. The building works are anticipated to begin in mid-2008. However the project still needs a considerable amount of money to be secured in the next few months, so that it can be assured of success.

Those wishing to assist should contact the library at their support page.

Thankyou!

Thoughts on Logo development companies

Hi all,

I’m new here and decided to post my question here as it seems like a rather mature forum. :)

Anyway, time’s tight for me I have a client who needs some logo work done. I had informed him that I am simply to busy, but that there be some options. Here’s what I was thinking:

The background:

  • The client has a very distinct and clear idea of what they want.
  • A limited budget.
  • They are a good past client.
  • This is a small side venture for them (hence the budget constraints).

The plan:

  • Possibly use a company like logoloft.
  • I have mentioned the idea to the client so they are aware, and beyond the cost they would pay me to be a go between and most likely to do final tweaks.

Why Logoloft:

  • It fits the budget constraints.
  • There is a clear vision to start with.
  • Even if they get the logo 70% of the way that’s time saved for me. I can then just come in a spice it all up and add finishing touches.

The downsides:

  • There are many people with a strong opinion about places like logoloft.

My general outlook is the same way I look at development, it’s business and you need to consider not only the quality but general feasibility in everything that you choose.

I’m hoping that perhaps some people here may have experience with logoloft (or other such companies) and may be able to point me in a reliable direction. Or in general just be able to recap their experiences with companies like logoloft. :)

Thanks for anytime you spend following up on this thread,

thoughts on retro design

I am a graphic design student, working on my final study about retro design in display fonts.

I chose this topic because there seems to be a lot of new fonts on the market that owe their form to a certain decade or style. Some are direct copies of old novelty faces and some have just a hint or feeling of some era. I’m focusing especially on display fonts done during the last ten years.

Here I have here some questions to which I would really appreciate some thoughts or opinions:

Why now? There’s no limitations in the forming of letters but still people are interested more reviving these old styles.
Is that just the point? Are designers just tired of the weird stuff that was made in the 90s?
Are the retro designers all amateurs who just make what they think will look nice?
What are the reasons for making fonts that look old?
What problems these retro fonts have?
What styles seem to be the most reproduced and what styles would you wish someone would digitalize?

I would really like to hear your further thoughts and opinions on the matter,
that would be very helpful.

Thanks!
