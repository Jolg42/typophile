Century Old Style vs. Bookman

Perhaps I just need new glasses or something.

To my way of thinking, the typeface Century Old Style so strongly resembles the Old Style typeface designed by Alexander Phemister as to be almost an imitation (although, apparently, it does have one change: drastically shortened descenders) - while the version of Bookman with which I am most familiar, the one drawn by Ed Benguiat, also might take some inspiration from it, but still seems to me to be an entirely new typeface, for which Ed Benguiat should get all the blame - and the credit.

Yet, a Google search shows Phemister mentioned most often in connection with Bookman. This may just be because Bookman is vastly more popular than Century Old Style. though, and not because the link is stronger.

Command "Append Glyphs" -- FontLab Studio 5.0.4 bug? Yes! And more.

Two fonts opened in FontLab, Alpha and Beta. The only difference between the two fonts is, that one glyph is missing in Alpha. And that glyph is the left and the right partner of kerning pairs in Beta. Select the glyph from Beta, copy it and append (context menu) it to Alpha. And now check the kerning. Only the kerning of those pairs is appended, in which the copied and appended glyph is the left partner. The other pairs are ignored.

If you also want to have the kerning of the pairs, in which the copied glyph is the right partner, you have to select the appended glyph in Alpha and command “Paste special” (Strg+Alt+V) and in the dialog the check boxes for pasting the kerning must be activated.

This is a bug, isn’t it? Is it already documented? I have learned to be careful talking about bugs. This time I checked it three times. Can anyone verify, that I am right?

Contextual Alternates: limit to massive use

I’m designing my first typeface, a contemporary serif family with four weights. I’d like to use massive contextual alternates (as in Kinescope or Vesper), but I have doubts about the limits to use this feature.

I plan to adopt contextual alternates through stylistic sets for these situations:

  1. beginnings and endings;
  2. glyphs collisions and undesired optical effects;
  3. typesetting special symbols as arrows and flourishes;
  4. Roman numerals;
  5. chess notation with figurines (unsure about this one);
  6. mathematical symbols and expressions.

Besides this, there would also be optional variations in some glyphs (typically, Q, R, f, a) which could also be set with stylistic sets.

So, is there any problem to set dozens or even hundreds of contextual alternates in OT tables? This may cause poor performance?

I understand some of the situations I listed could be addressed with GREP styles in InDesign (as Roman numerals and chess notation), but it would be nice if the font had all these features.
