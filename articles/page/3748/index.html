Ute Harder

Indices : Designers : Ute Harder

Ute Harder, aka Frau Jenson, moved to Hamburg, Germany for university studies after unsatisfying jobs with a pharmacy and then a paper manufacturer. Another year followed with a variety of jobs including the great experience as a scenery assistant at the ‘Ohnsorg Theater’ in Hamburg. In 1994 she studied Illustration and Communication design at the University of Applied Science in Hamburg. In the typography classes of Jovica Veljovic she ‘got infected by the typevirus’. In 1999 she digitized ’ Marathon’, a hot metal typeface originally designed by Rudolf Koch which received special mention at ‘Kurt Christians Förderpreis’-Contest. Marathon is licensed to the Linotype Library GmbH. Frau Jenson graduated with the ‘Diplom-Designer FH’ in 2002 and since she is working as a freelance graphic designer, collaborating on diverse projects for Linotype and developing her own fonts. Frau Jenson lives and works in Hamburg.

Type designs by Ute Harder: Marathon, Alpha Initials, Cilati, Frau Jenson People, Tulda, Bastyan, Alpha Roman

James Grieshaber

Indices : Designers : James Grieshaber

James Grieshaber attended Rochester Institute of Technology and earned a BFA in Graphic Design. Grieshaber was a staff designer at P22 Type Foundry and helped establish International House of Fonts. He has been honored with awards of Excellence in Type Design from Association Typographique International (ATypI) for Gothic Gothic and from TypeArt'05 for his Operina Cyrillic type designs. He has been involved with the Society of Typographic Aficionados and served as Chairman of SOTA from 2008—2010. Designer and Co-editor of the Indie Fonts book series, Grieshaber has taught typography at RIT and now runs Typeco, his own type design studio, in Chicago, IL.

Type designs by James Grieshaber include: Cezanne Pro, Operina Pro, Segoe Print, Reenie Beanie, Black Ops, Short Stack, Atomic Age, Supermercado, Metamorphous, Sarina, Posada, Woodtype, Art Deco Chic, Arts & Crafts Hunter, Arts & Crafts Tall, Glyphic Neue, Chunkfeeder, Trapper, Super Duty, Cypher, Gothic Gothic, Cusp, Numismatic, Yule, and Zaftig.

Fontlab blend

When creating a type family I start with the 2 extreme weights - Thin and Extra Bold.

I then carefully space and kern these 2 weights keeping them consistent. Once I am happy with the 2 weights I blend percentages between them - for example 20%, 40%, 60% and 80% to create a six weight family. Doing it this way ensures correct spacing and kerning throughout the family. I don’t use the same kerning for both weights.

This worked fine in Fontographer.

I tried this in Fontlab - it was OK blending some of the characters, but others could not be blended even though the outlines (or contours) are totally compatible with similar numbers and types of points (or nodes), path directions and starting points. In the resulting font extra nodes were inserted even when the ‘Do not interpolate compatible outlines’ box is checked. This apparently shouldn’t be the case.

Also - and this is very bad - there is no kerning in the blended font. Where is the kerning? it also should be a percentage blend between the kerning of the Thin and Extra Bold originals.

Any advice?

Thanks

Big Fleischmann Bake-Off Imminent

You have been requesting a comparative review of Mercury and Farnham in Bald Condensed, and I am happy to announce I will be able to offer twice the fun. Hoefler & Frere-Jones’ Mercury will be compared not only to Christian Schwartz’s Farnham, but to Matthew Carter’s Fenway – the type family he designed as a replacement for Times Roman in Sports Illustrated – and Erhard Kaiser’s DTL Fleischmann as well. Funny thing is that DTL Fleischmann – which is the odd one out as it is the most faithful (and quirky) digitisation – unexpectedly has a new competitor in Mário Feliciano’s as of yet unreleased Eudald. This is a Didone which is based on the work of Eudald Pradell, a Spanish imitator of Fleischmann. So five typefaces in total will contend for the coveted title of Most Totally Awesome Fleischmann Digitisation™.

Oh, one more thing: I would like to stress that I have no academic background whatsoever, so don’t expect any roaring theories nor historical contexts. I review type from a gut feeling and using common sense. So if I get it wrong, please feel free to laugh at my expense. Like, really hard.

Omine Type Launches

I am pleased to announce the opening of my personal foundry.
Three new typefaces are available for now, exclusively in the OpenType format.

The typefaces
Maest is an unusual script typeface constructed with straight lines. It features 4 styles of figures (tabular lining, tabular oldstyle, proportional lining, proportional oldstyle), swash capitals, ending forms, f-ligatures, and 3 weights (from regular to black).

Bunker is a versatile stencil type family with lowercase characters and a extensive character set that covers the Latin, Central European, Turkish and Baltic codepages. This type family is comprised of 4 interchangeable styles (a character occupies the same advance-width in all of the fonts) and features 4 styles of figures.

Gotica Lumina is a new blackletter. The soft version features gentle contours that contains no corners. The sharp version, more suitable for display usage, is more rigid and slightly lighter than the soft one. There is an alternate font* for each style, and all fonts have 4 styles of figures, standard ligatures and discretionary ligatures.

* The alternate characters are also available in the “main” font, through the “salt” feature, but considering the current general lack of support to this specific OpenType feature, alternate fonts have been made available.
