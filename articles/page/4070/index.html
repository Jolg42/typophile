BD4D Seattle

Hey all you Seattle people, I am curating a local event called BD4D - By Designers for Designers. BD4D seeks to create an atmosphere where designers can share their work in a fun and relaxed atmosphere. But what’s even more important, it’s FREE. Previous events have been held in London, Manchester, Sydney, Paris, LA, and New York, establishing BD4D as one of the world’s foremost designer gatherings.

BD4D is broken up into two segments. The first is 3MM (three minute madness) where a designer or studio is invited to present their work in around three minutes. Usually, this consists of focusing in on one specific piece or site. The Featured presenters have roughly 30 minutes on the stage to display and discuss a broad range of their work. Each presentations is followed by a QA session where audience members have the opportunity to question the designers on their presentations, or
work in general.

The Seattle event will be hosted at the Henry Art Gallery ( http://www.henryart.org) on Friday, October 3. Doors open at 6:30pm, but I encourage everyone to arrive early, as space is limited, and we have no idea how many people will be attending.

I want to invite you to come see/meet the great speakers we have lined up, as well as other creatives in attendance. The speakers are:

Studios ( http://www.secondstory.com)

( http://www.d-kitchen.com)

Fontsite/Softmaker: so what?

Hello Typophiles,

I’ve read the following statement in another
thread (Understanding the legal issues…):

Nathan Matteson:
_“I think James is hitting it right on the head
here. I’ve been trying to get my school to buy a
decent font collection - even if only for two or
three “type specific” labs - for nigh on three
years now. We just had a good instructor quit
over the lack of type and the beaurocratic hoops
you have to jump through to [not] get it.

And the closest we got was our bookstore selling
the dubious FontSite CD. :-/”_

Well, that statement came in good time. I’ve
been browsing the websites of many typefoundries
looking for a basic font CD collection to use
on everyday’s work at my office.

I’ve considered all my options, from Bitstream
to URW, and they range from U$ 120 to U$ 500…
That was until yesterday, when I found both
Softmaker (Megafont XXL) and Fontsite (CD 500)
collections, priced as low as U$ 39 and U$ 49…

So, what’s up here? I’ve recognized many fonts
under different names but I didn’t get if those
collections are legal or even “ethical”.

I’m planning to buy a font CD collection not
only to be “clean”, but also because I want
to make sure that the designers would receive
their proper royalties. I mean, what would
Adrian Frutiger receive if I buy FrontPage*
instead of Frutiger?

I want to make clear that I’m not distrusting
any company/person. I’m just curious why and how
those similar font collections can have so
different prices.

There’s a Brazilian proverb that says “o barato
sai caro”, wich means something like you cannot
have something for nothing… (In fact, I guess
it’s not even Brazilian, it’s an universal law).

Best regards,
hC

* This font equivalence was taken from Luc
Devroye’s website.

Dr. Peter Karow Award

Dr. Peter Karow Award for Font Technology & Digital Typography

At the third DTL FontMaster Conference on the 18th of November 2003 in Castle Maurick in the Netherlands, the first Dr. Peter Karow Award for Font Technology & Digital Typography will be presented. This prize will be awarded yearly to people with extraordinary and innovative achievements in the field of font-related technology and consists of a certificate, a facsimile of the 42-line Gutenberg bible and DTL FontMaster for Mac OS and Windows. The first award will be received by Dr. Peter Karow himself.
