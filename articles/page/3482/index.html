Stencil type fonts for a sign

Dear All,

I am an architect with a strong interest in all things type related and I am in the process of getting a sign made for my house/apartment here in Japan and for when we return to Australia. The sign will be made of laser cut steel plate (similar to the sign by Peter Verheul in the attached image http://www.farhill.nl/02_lettering/applettering.html).

I would like to use a calligraphic such as FF Quadraat (please forgive my poor grasp of the lexicon!) as I love their letterforms and especially those of the italics (and I have an existing license of the family).

The final sign may simply have our family name (Dunlop as you can probably guess!) or we may include an additional line above of ‘Grant & Yoko’ in a smaller point size. The obvious problem is with the counters in some of the characters (D, O, P, a, o, p) especially and the process of laser cutting them.

There seems to be two obvious approaches to solving this problem:

  1. Find an alternative ‘stencil’ type font which possesses the same calligraphic feel of FF Quadraat (or Fedra Serif for example) which would be suitable for a stencil type use.

  2. Modify the problematic characters of an existing font to make it suitable for stenciling.

A third less obvious solution is to design a character/font from scrarch which fits the purpose. This could be possible but I may then never end up with a sign!

I have taken a look at some of the other ‘stencil’ threads (Wallflower:) http://typophile.com/node/4933 & http://typophile.com/node/15078 here and they have provided some inspiration to tackle the third option. Peter Verheul’s letters and signs are also an inspiration and a spur.

I would be grateful if anybody could provide some advice for the first two options: any fonts which fit the first options and what the correct process would be for obtaining permission to alter those problem characters to make them suitable for the purpose.

I look forward to your responses.

Regards,

Grant Dunlop

P22 releases an addition to their Frank Lloyd Wright fonts.

For Release: June, 1st, 2006, Buffalo, NY

P22 type foundry announces an addition to its popular series of Frank Lloyd
Wright fonts with FLLW® Midway™ and releases a new font inspired by
Post-Impressionist artist Paul Gauguin.

FLLW® Midway™

In 1913, Edward C. Waller, Jr. commissioned Frank Lloyd Wright to expand an
Amusement Park complex and turn it into an upscale palatial beer garden.
The Midway Gardens was born. FLLW® Midway™ is based on the lettering found
on the Midway Garden’s working drawings. The lettering itself is in Mr.
Wright’s distinctive hand. This type of architectural lettering is a bit
more casual than standard lettering found on most blueprints. It evokes the
personality of Frank Lloyd Wright in a way that complements the other fonts
in the P22 FLLW font series. The set contains Midway One and Midway Two,
which can be used interchangeably to give a more naturalistic feeling of
hand lettering. Also included is Midway Ornaments, which features over 100
architectural and decorative elements that can be combined in many ways for
surprising and effective decorative motifs and borders. Designed for P22 by
Paul hunt.

http://www.p22.com/products/fllwMidway.html

P22 Gauguin Font Set™ -

P22 Gauguin™, designed by Colin Kahn, is based on the writings and sketches
of Post-Impressionist artist Paul Gauguin. This naturalistic writing font
is based on Gauguin’s notebooks from his travels to Tahiti and the South
Seas. This set presents two styles of script fonts (Regular & Brush) and a
set of decorative extras featuring Gauguin woodcuts, sketches and imagery
from his paintings.

P22 Gauguin™ is also available as a Pro font. The OpenType font
incorporates P22 Gauguin Regular™ plus over 350 additional characters
including: Stylistic alternates for all characters (a-z, A-Z), Central
European and Cyrillic character sets, ligatures, swash characters and many
other OpenType features.

http://www.p22.com/products/gauguin.html

Prices starting at $19.95 for single fonts. Both sets are available on
CD-ROM, contained in a DVD ThinPak case. All fonts can be test driven with
TypeCaster™.

Village: Galaxie Cassiopeia, Flama version 2, and an offer you can't refuse.

Submitted by Miss Tiffany : 31.May.2006 3.54pm

/ / / / /

Galaxie Cassiopeia from Village is published!

This fresh script face is the second release in the Galaxie family, following on its northern sky neighbour, Polaris. Cassiopeia is available as two bold fonts, one a basic non-joining font, the other a complex OpenType font using advanced feature programming for natural letter-to-letter joins.

Just turn on the OpenType Contextual Alternates feature and type; there are 11 versions of each lowercase letter and 5 versions of each uppercase letter. The “calt” feature will select the correct version of the letter for its context. The code was written by the OpenType pioneer Tal Leming, responsible for the “randomisation” feature in Orange Italic’s “Local Gothic”. (Unfortunately, the feature can’t be displayed by the mudTyper; you can see only the Basic version.)

An early beta version of the font was used for the Village logotype.

Galaxie Cassiopeia ships as cross-platform OpenType format for both fonts along with the Basic version of the font in legacy PostScript Type 1 format. The type supports dozens of languages, and a license sells for US$59.

http://vllg.com/Village/GalaxieCassiopeia/mudTyper+Weights/

/ / / / /

Flama Version 2 from Feliciano Type Foundry

Mario Feliciano presents an update to his popular Flama family, adding Thin and Ultralight weights at the skinny end of the gamut, and Extrabold filling a gap at the heavy end.

The family is now 9 weights in roman and italic, ratcheting the usability up a notch. You can see the family put through its paces in “Black Book” magazine.

Flama V2 ships as cross-platform OpenType format along with legacy PostScript Type 1 format fonts. The type supports the Latin 1 encoding, and a single user license sells for US$380.

http://vllg.com/Feliciano/Flama/mudTyper+Weights/
