Anonymous @
_Am I normal?_ i doubt it. there's no such thing as "normal." maybe i have it all wrong, but to me , the term _[humanist](http://web.archive.org/web/20100207003327/http:/www.typophile.com/wiki/humanist)_ refers to letters that are influenced by [ductal logic](http://web.archive.org/web/20100207003327/http:/www.typophile.com/wiki/ductal%2520logic), whether they're [sans](http://web.archive.org/web/20100207003327/http:/www.typophile.com/wiki/sans%2520serif) or [serifed type](http://web.archive.org/web/20100207003327/http:/www.typophile.com/wiki/serif). in [calligraphy](http://web.archive.org/web/20100207003327/http:/www.typophile.com/wiki/calligraphy), the [humanist minuscule](http://web.archive.org/web/20100207003327/http:/www.typophile.com/wiki/humanist%2520minuscule) is a descendant of the [carolingian minuscule](http://web.archive.org/web/20100207003327/http:/www.typophile.com/wiki/carolingian%2520minuscule). The humanist model of calligraphy is what influenced the earliest [Roman](http://web.archive.org/web/20100207003327/http:/www.typophile.com/wiki/Roman) ( [whiteletter](http://web.archive.org/web/20100207003327/http:/www.typophile.com/wiki/whiteletter)) types, particularly those of [Nicholas Jenson](http://web.archive.org/web/20100207003327/http:/www.typophile.com/wiki/Nicholas%2520Jenson). The [Vox-ATypI classification](http://web.archive.org/web/20100207003327/http:/www.typophile.com/wiki/Vox-ATypI%2520classification) system uses the term humanist to refer to the [Venetian](http://web.archive.org/web/20100207003327/http:/www.typophile.com/wiki/Venetian) model of type.

Doesn't sound wrong to me, Paul. But I rarely see "humanist" bandied about in regard to serifs (with the exception of typefaces inspired by Italian Renaissance type), maybe because many more serifs are "humanist" by your ductal-logic definition than are sans- and slab-serifs. I'd guess that this comes from the early slab-serifs and sans-serifs not being too concerned with ductus as far as I know until Johnston and Gill in the early 20th c. and Frutiger in 1968. So to say "humanist" doesn't separate a group out as much for serifs as it does for other type genres.
