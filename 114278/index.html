The style is generally called De Vinne, and was used in inexpensive rubber-stamp kits in the 1900s. Unfortunately, the /n doesn't match... Agree with Nick. Definitely [DeVinne](http://web.archive.org/web/20141014073921/http:/typophile.com/node/99936). For the /n issue, may it's a 180° rotated /u. More alternatives (some including a roughen effect): [Hand Stamped JNL](http://web.archive.org/web/20141014073921/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/jnlevine/hand-stamped), [Publication JNL](http://web.archive.org/web/20141014073921/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/jnlevine/publication), [Charter Stamp HF](http://web.archive.org/web/20141014073921/http:/www.fontbros.com/bundles/1608)
