I've seen them occasionally -- sorry can't remember the names of any typefaces off-hand -- but they are uncommon and, typically, of little use. The problem is that in an fi ligature it is typical for the overhang of the f to replace the dot of the i. The only way to have sufficient space for an accent above the i is to either move the i further from the f or to retract the overhang of the f; in either case, you usually cease to have a need for a ligature.

I've thought about such a ligature but never seen one. [Storm](http://web.archive.org/web/20050818112727/http:/www.stormtype.com/) sells at least [one package](http://web.archive.org/web/20050818112727/http:/www.pismolijna.cz/pdf/Walbaum.pdf) with f-ligatures for f next to an umlauted (dieresis) vowel. [Helzel's version of Wilhelm Klingspor-Schrift](http://web.archive.org/web/20050818112727/http:/www.romana-hamburg.de/fraktur7.htm) includes some long-s ligatures with the dieresis compressed and shifted over somewhat. They may have been part of Koch's original design. Andrea de Leeuw van Weenen [wrote](http://web.archive.org/web/20050818112727/http:/www.ntg.nl/texnl92may.html#Mon%2C%2025%20May%2092%2013:47:00%20CET) about an acute fi ligature to the TeX-NL list back in 1992, it would appear. That's all I can find about it. [Mrs Eaves Opentype](http://web.archive.org/web/20050818112727/http:/www.google.com.ni/search?sourceid=mozclient&ie=utf-8&oe=utf-8&q=%22accented%2Bligatures%22) contains accented quaint ligatures, including a capital acute fi.
