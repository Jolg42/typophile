Anonymous @ 11.Apr.2007 12.11pm
ah I’ve just wikipedia’d it... created in England by Robert Besley for the Fann Street Foundry in 1845. Besley went as far as trying to patent the typeface, though this failed. The original matrices and punches remained at Stephenson Blake and latterly at the Type Museum, London. They were marketed by Stephenson Blake as Consort, though some additional weights (a bold and italics) were cut in 1950s It was named after the Clarendon Press in Oxford. The typeface was reworked by the Monotype foundry in 1935. It was revised by Hermann Eidenbenz in 1953. could still be a proto version of the bold cut

We think it’s formica - multiple layers of white and kraft paper plasticized by laminating together in resin under huge heat and pressure. the top layers (maybe 20 sheets) would have the letter printed on them. this might become distorted under the huge pressures involved. we experimented with routed and laser etched plastics but couldn’t find one durable enough to withstand being repeatedly trodden on. we could easily be wrong though.
