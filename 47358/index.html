 The basic idea was one of these two designs:

I preferred the first, and the drafts below reflect that, but some folks have suggested the second version.
 Trouble I’m having is with the middle head of the large ש. I’ve tried for something abstract, but I’m worried that it still looks like something “belongs” there. I avoided the usual shape for the middle head for exactly that reason: it looked like the letter ז or ו was intended.

Anonymous @ 28 Jul 2008 — 9:32am
David: I see where the _ḥet_ (ח) & _heh_ (ה) might be confused (sorry simḥa!), but that's a bug in the Hebrew alphabet. If I were turning the surrounding text into a font I might try to differentiate them more, but in this context, with its well-known quote¹, I figured I could prefer ‘pretty’ over ‘more readable’.  That the _ḥet_ (ח) looks somewhat like the _mem_ (מ) from some fonts is harder for me to see; it certainly doesn't look like the _mem_ I'm using! Similarly with the _shin_ (ש) and the Rashi script _bet_ (ב): there's some similarity, but no real possibility of confusion.  The _vav_ (ו)/_yod_ (י) confusion is less excusable, so a bit of background: The style of the surrounding text came from an experiment I'd done in how wide I could extend Hebrew letters and have them recognizable. To distinguish the _vav_ (ו) from the _yod_ (י) from the _resh_ (ר) I chose to make the _yod_ as short as I could while keeping the basic shape and making the _vav_ the narrow exception to the extended style. The `bump' on the _shin_ (ש) and the awkwardness of the _ayin_ (ע) are both also results of this original design. I liked the _alef_ (א) I'd done, but it's only here in the tiny text. Eran/Simḥa: I vaguely see an _ayin_ (ע) in the right stem—where I’d intended _yod yod shin_ (ייש), but I cannot see how the _heh heh_ (הה) on the left looks like a _mem mem_ (םם) to you. I guess I should take it in account anyhow.  … But: Once you know what letters they're supposed to be, do you see them, or still what you saw first? It doesn't need to be obvious _a priori_; if it works _a posteriori_ it's good too.  (Modern bias?) —Joel 1. Jeremiah 33:10–11: “Yet again there shall be heard … in the cities of Judah, and in the streets of Jerusalem, … (11) the voice of joy and the voice of gladness, the voice of the bridegroom and the voice of the bride”.
