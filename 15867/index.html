Survey of Type Buyers by Graphic Design USA

Not sure if this has been brought to anyone’s attention on the Forum, but I just reviewed a survey in Oct. issue of Graphic Design USA on the Type Purchasing marketplace from the type specifier’s viewpoint.

Of note (by the readers surveyed):

Do you have purchasing influence with regard to type and fonts?
YES 88%, NO 12%

How many designer font users or specifiers are there in your department or firm?
1-2 38%, 3-5 21%
6-10 13%, 11-15 11%,
16-25 8%, 25+ 9%

Which operating system does your company use for those purposes?
macintosh 88%, window 38%, both 26%

Many of the comments by graphic designers are notable in terms of why and how the make decisions on buying fonts, but several comments just sound like “the smart thing to say.”

COMMENT BY GRAPHIC DESIGNER: Before computers changed the designer’s toolbox, type houses would provide large catalogues that displayed all manner of type style, size, specification tables (remember “specing type”?) and the like, for a wide variety of font families. We kept a huge library of these catalogues that we would “troll” for the appropriate font. The web has pretty much replaced this huge library, but nothing beats having the font resources right at hand.
Obviously we cannot purchase every available font on the planet so I would suggest to type designers and foundries that they send more mailers, promotions and reminders that they have the right “building block” at the “right time” for the design I am putting together. — Ron Kalina, Kalina Design, Exton, PA

The survey is followed by several pages Type Designers’ questions & answers.

Anonymous @ 27.Oct.2005 1.46am
» _Gotta sell quite a few $40 units to warrant production, printing, and mailing a mag of U&lc’s quality_ It’s true that printing and paper costs are higher now, but if you recall for the first year or two of publication U&lc was printed on low grade newsprint with black ink only. The “quality” of U&lc was in its layouts and content, plus a bullpen of some of the best designers of that time. Also, Aaron Burns initially focused on creating a mailing base of only type specifiers (built from mailing lists from PLINC, other type houses and subscription list from Graphic Design USA) and colleges with art programs. The ratio of type buyers to readership was extremely high. Eventually U&lc sold ad space as well. Developing a direct mail piece in _magazine-like_ format by a foundry or font distributor would be quite costly today. A better approach would be to consider it a true publication, maybe quarterly. The production and printing cost, like any other magazine, would be paid in part by the advertisers, subscribers (if you are charging a fee) and direct purchase (instead of “newsstand” sales, it would be single issues ordered via internet, like [étapes:international](http://web.archive.org/web/20081201111536/http:/www.artdesign.fr/boutique_us/liste_produits.cfm?type=23&num=0&code_lg=lg_us)). All in all, it would still be major undertaking. Hmmmm... guess we need to come up with another schema. ;-)
