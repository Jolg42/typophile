According to [wikipedia](http://web.archive.org/web/20150218044752/http:/en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snellen_chart), These aren't ordinary fonts (with only 9 characters), they appear to be custom designed so the black balances with the white (notan? :^P) and the body height and width is five times the width (or thickness) of a stroke. The sans-serif chart example appears to be [this](http://web.archive.org/web/20150218044752/http:/www.thomson-software-solutions.com/user_updates.html) font. The slab does bear a resemblance to Rockwell but I'm sure it is not.

Don't know the official typefaces. Judging by the vertical symmetry, lack of overshoots on the rounds (O,C), and tiny bowls (D,P) it seems clear that the slab version at least was made by scientists, doctors, or engineers -- not type designers. These are my closest matches: Serif: [Stymie Medium](http://web.archive.org/web/20150218044752/http:/www.fontshop.com/fonts/singles/linotype/stymie_com_medium/?sample_text=EFPTOZLD&sample_size=74) beats [Rockwell](http://web.archive.org/web/20150218044752/http:/www.fontshop.com/fonts/singles/monotype/rockwell_by_adobe_std_roman/?sample_text=EFPTOZLD&sample_size=74) mainly because of the width and it's shorter E,F crossbars. Sans: [Sackers Heavy Gothic](http://web.archive.org/web/20150218044752/http:/www.fontshop.com/fonts/singles/monotype/sackers_gothic_std_heavy/?sample_text=PNXCASLGRK)
