![](http://web.archive.org/web/20140822211318im_/http:/typophile.com/files/KCMasterpieceLabelTypophile112896SM_3664.jpg) U.S. federal trademark registration for this design was filed on September 20, 2011 [http://www.trademarkia.com/kc-masterpiece-85427596.html](http://web.archive.org/web/20140822211318/http:/www.trademarkia.com/kc-masterpiece-85427596.html "http://www.trademarkia.com/kc-masterpiece-85427596.html") I believe this is lettering/artwork and not based on a single font. In some ways the letters are similar to a sans with Clarendon style serifs added to the base and top of the vertical strokes. Check for example the letter-forms and terminals for C, c and e in Euro Sans Cond Bold [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/profonts/euro-sans-pro/](http://web.archive.org/web/20140822211318/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/profonts/euro-sans-pro/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/profonts/euro-sans-pro/") There are also similar features with some serif fonts, such as the lobe on r Jakob Erbar's Candida, as suggested by Fivos. Also, Walbaum or Sirba [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/type-together/sirba/](http://web.archive.org/web/20140822211318/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/type-together/sirba/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/type-together/sirba/") for the top terminal on the s. The diamond dot on the i is a feature of some Fraktur designs. Don
