In my opinion, in a perfect world, the First Amendment would be the law of the land in every nation. This would mitigate against penalties for inappropriate use of Comic Sans. Thus, the rule _de gustibus non disputandum_, usually translated as "there is no accounting for taste", should continue to hold. This, of course, does not change the fact that in a _perfect_ world, no one would be _tempted_ to misuse Comic Sans. Maybe Microsoft would license an appropriate typeface from Comicraft to include with Windows that would replace it for the uses to which it is not suited. Oh, wait a moment. In a **perfect** world, we wouldn't be _using_ Microsoft Windows on our computers. But I do think it's excessive to praise Helvetica as the world's "most beautiful" typeface. It _is_ a beautiful one that nears perfection. But it's still excessive to praise Helvetica, Times Roman, Baskerville, Garamond, or even Eusebius, Centaur, or Doves in that respect. There are too many typefaces out there that are beautiful and near perfection to single out just one as standing above all the others.

<cite>“it’s the most neutral typeface that doesn’t interfere with the conveyance of content”</cite> Depends on the content. I agree that this is "nonsense" in one way: Helvetica isn't even a particularly good text typeface. Univers is much better for that purpose if one must use a sans-serif. However, Helvetica Medium does get overused for signage for a reason. In that sphere, it is what people are used to, what they don't notice, what is invisible as a typeface. That, though, is clearly culturally based - a consequence of exposure to current fashions in typefaces. The biggest problem with such comments is that they're dated. Extravagant praise of Helvetica was warranted when it first came out, as it gave the typographer something that just wasn't provided by previous sans-serif typefaces, whether of American or European origin. Today, though, its lessons have been fully assimilated by designers of sans-serif faces, and so many alternatives exist.
