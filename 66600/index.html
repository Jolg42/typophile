Advice: Sans to pair with Brunel, the gorgeous English Modern

Greetings,
Would really appreciate your sage advice and tyopgraphical wisdom.

Working on a magazine redesign using Christian Schwartz’ delicious Brunel (see samples below), which he describes as an anthology of the late eighteenth and nineteenth century English foundries.

I need to find the perfect sans to pair … something that evokes the same era and feels like part of the family with that lovely period flavor. I’d also like to find a compatible slab … so basically the whole works.

What would YOU match up? And if you have the time … please tell me why.

[Figgins Sans](http://web.archive.org/web/20100127214150/http:/www.fontshop.com/fonts/downloads/shinntype/figgins_sans_family/) is based on the birth of the sans, though it's a very personal interpretation. See the PDF on [Shinntype](http://web.archive.org/web/20100127214150/http:/www.shinntype.com/figgins.html). [Akzidenz Grotesk](http://web.archive.org/web/20100127214150/http:/new.myfonts.com/fonts/berthold/akzidenz-grotesk-be/?refby=typographica) [Monotype Grotesque](http://web.archive.org/web/20100127214150/http:/www.fontshop.com/fonts/downloads/monotype/monotype_grotesque_std_complete_vp/) [Rhode](http://web.archive.org/web/20100127214150/http:/www.fontshop.com/fontlist/families/rhode/) For a slab, [Giza](http://web.archive.org/web/20100127214150/http:/www.fontshop.com/fontlist/families/giza/) or an [early Clarendon](http://web.archive.org/web/20100127214150/http:/www.fontshop.com/fonts/designer/the_fan_street_foundry/) would be your best bet. Possibly [Memphis](http://web.archive.org/web/20100127214150/http:/www.fontshop.com/fonts/downloads/linotype/memphis_std_complete_vp/) or [Rockwell](http://web.archive.org/web/20100127214150/http:/www.fontshop.com/fonts/downloads/monotype/rockwell_std_complete_vp/), though they are more 1930s.
