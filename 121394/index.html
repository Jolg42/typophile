Opinions about Greek character set

I’m currently working on a font, the origins and development of which can be found at http://typophile.com/node/120859.

During the course of that discussion, Vladimir commented that the font had a “dynamic quality… as if a breeze is blowing through the letters…”. Separately, I’ve been quietly following the conversation about Gill Sans Arabic ( http://typophile.com/node/92658), as well as other threads here, which touch upon the tendency by some designers to Latinise non-Latin scripts. Chris Lozos posted the following comment in a thread about the correct way to draw the Greek letter /Mu/:

My opinion is that Greek text should dance more than say Times Roman Latin. Many Latin faces have glyphs which appear as a single gestalt on the page and group together as words with a single gestalt. I feel Greek text swings together as a flowing, undulating tango rather than marching lockstep in very even time.

Everything I’d read made me wonder if it was possible to develop a worthy Greek character set for my new font, notwithstanding it’s more jitterbug than tango. This is the first draft; it includes only lower case characters at this stage and I’d welcome any feedback (please ignore the accented characters for now).

http://freepdfhosting.com/f949b115fe.pdf

For a comparison with the current Latin characters set, please view http://freepdfhosting.com/0ff63ba208.pdf

Thanks so much in advance.

Anonymous @ 24 Nov 2014 — 10:47am
I do not think that greek characters should dance tango on the page. Ancient greek inscriptional letters had very simple forms, similar to modern sans serif typefaces, before the roman alphabet even existed. Thus, I think it is a misconception that simple, rationalized forms are in any way unsuitable for a greek typeface. I really like your greek characters, and they match the roman characters nicely. However, I would remove the little top-right loops on zeta and xi, I think they are too much. I would also try less curved kappa and lambda forms. <cite>Most of these caps are copies of the Latin characters set - is this correct for Greek</cite> Yes.
