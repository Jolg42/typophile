Anonymous @ 22 Nov 2014 — 1:54am
Pre WW I spur serif design. Can't decide if it is lettering or was a font. I did not spot an obvious digital substitute. Scale is small but it may include an arrowhead termination on the goose-neck terminals of \_C\_, \_G\_ and \_S\_. The leg on \_R\_ reminds me of some Goudy fonts from the era, such as Goudy Oldstyle ![](http://web.archive.org/web/20150312164922im_/http:/typophile.com/files/GoudyOldstyleAsGreys_5824.gif) [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/lanston/ltc-goudy-oldstyle/](http://web.archive.org/web/20150312164922/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/lanston/ltc-goudy-oldstyle/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/lanston/ltc-goudy-oldstyle/") Some general similarity Vertrina ![](http://web.archive.org/web/20150312164922im_/http:/typophile.com/files/VertrinaAsGreys_5784.gif) [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/gatf/vertrina/](http://web.archive.org/web/20150312164922/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/gatf/vertrina/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/gatf/vertrina/") Possible substitute, which differs in many details, is a Mark Simonson recreation of a 1920s lettering style called Goldenbook ![](http://web.archive.org/web/20150312164922im_/http:/typophile.com/files/GoldenbookAsGreys_6338.gif) [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/marksimonson/goldenbook/](http://web.archive.org/web/20150312164922/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/marksimonson/goldenbook/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/marksimonson/goldenbook/") Don

For overall letter forms, I think it may have been based on something like Goudy Medium (just raise the crossbars on the |H|, |G|, and |E| and rein in the serifs): [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/redrooster/goudy-38-rr/](http://web.archive.org/web/20150312164922/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/redrooster/goudy-38-rr/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/redrooster/goudy-38-rr/") For general look and feel, but still missing some crucial details (such as the |R| and some serifs), these might also work for recreations: [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/linotype/aperto/](http://web.archive.org/web/20150312164922/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/linotype/aperto/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/linotype/aperto/") [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/fontbureau/grand-central/](http://web.archive.org/web/20150312164922/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/fontbureau/grand-central/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/fontbureau/grand-central/") [http://www.t26.com/search?q=Glosso](http://web.archive.org/web/20150312164922/http:/www.t26.com/search?q=Glosso "http://www.t26.com/search?q=Glosso") There’s also a passing resemblance to Korinna, which was designed in 1904 (the serifs are way off, but the overall form isn’t bad): [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/adobe/itc-korinna/](http://web.archive.org/web/20150312164922/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/adobe/itc-korinna/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/adobe/itc-korinna/") Nevada Serial captures some of the details (especially the |E|): [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/ef/nevada/](http://web.archive.org/web/20150312164922/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/ef/nevada/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/ef/nevada/")

The engraving or lithography used to make the lid design may have been made by an English craftsman who was familiar with a font sold in England as Wren, which has the sucking lemon \_G\_ with a tall bar and a small spur on the chin, and various similarities in other letters. ![](http://web.archive.org/web/20150312164922im_/http:/typophile.com/files/WrenLight@Klingspor@PrintingTypesP72SM_4803.jpg) ![](http://web.archive.org/web/20150312164922im_/http:/typophile.com/files/WrenBold@Klingspor@PrintingTypesP72SM_5452.jpg) Don
