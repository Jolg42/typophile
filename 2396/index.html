[Amplitude](http://web.archive.org/web/20070402033944/http:/www.fontbureau.com/fonts/font_frames.tpl?fontname=Amplitude) and [Whitman](http://web.archive.org/web/20070402033944/http:/www.fontbureau.com/fonts/font_frames.tpl?fontname=Whitman), both from FontBureau, are just wonderful. Amplitude, a sans with 'traps' used as decorative elements is very widely usable, I think, but has a fresh and unique look, fascinating to the eye. Whitman, a serifed text face, manages to be calm, graceful and crisp at the same time. Influenced by Dwiggins, but fresh and different. I hope it will turn out to be a favorite for books.

Re. Arabic Typesetting in response to Hrant: _1) There's some missing kerning? Like look at the big gap in "'amrihi" (4th word of 2nd line)._ Quite probably. This is the only text sample I had on hand, a PDF that was prepared by Mamoun and Paul for the TDC annual early this year, and the font was not quite finished. _2) Does the font fully support the vertical floating aliph? "'aLaahi" (second word) certainly shows it, but some words (like "rahmaan" - 3rd word - and "�aalika" - penultimate word on 3rd line) are supposed to use that instead of the normal (slanted) floating aliph, because it's pronounced like a long-aliph but is not fully marked._ I believe that's an encoding issue: the vertical alif mark used in _aLaahi_ is a different Unicode character from the fatha. I'm not sure whether Mamoun entered this text himself, or used an existing electronic text, in which case the encoding of marks is inherited. _3) I think this setting at least is underusing the kasheeda, which when applied well can greatly amplify the airy elegance of Arabic setting._ Yes, there is very little kasheeda use in this example. There would automatically be more if the text were justified, I believe, although I agree that justification is not the only reason to use kasheedas. Not obvious from the sample above, perhaps, are two of my favourite features of the font: contextual differentiation of similar letters by modifying tooth length, and the cursive connection feature that gradually raises preceding letters off the baseline: ![Arabic Typesetting contextual and cursive](http://web.archive.org/web/20070402033944im_/http:/www.typophile.com/forums/messages/30/22401.gif)
