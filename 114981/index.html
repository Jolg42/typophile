Anonymous @ 14 Mar 2014 — 2:48pm
In my opinion the fonts shown in the image are not old fonts, but rather are modern designs, which may or may not be digital fonts. No problem with the sans-serif for BOURBON as mentioned in my earlier posts. But "barrel STOUT" is a real Frankenstein. Notice the differences between these letters and classic italics like Garamond [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/paratype/original-garamond-bt/](http://web.archive.org/web/20140319224757/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/paratype/original-garamond-bt/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/paratype/original-garamond-bt/") and Fleischman [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/bitstream/fleischman-bt-pro/](http://web.archive.org/web/20140319224757/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/bitstream/fleischman-bt-pro/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/bitstream/fleischman-bt-pro/"). The truncated ascenders, the almost vertical slope, the strange tail at the top of b, etc. What's this with a ball serif capital S? And how did square serifs from Egyptian end up on the T and U? This is not to say that someone has not packaged this mish mash as a font. So if the Frankenfont does not turn up you could make reasonable facsimile of "barrel" by editing Fleishman. Other candidates for editing include Verdigris MVB Pro Text [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/mvbfonts/mbv-verdigris-pro/](http://web.archive.org/web/20140319224757/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/mvbfonts/mbv-verdigris-pro/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/mvbfonts/mbv-verdigris-pro/") ITC Cushing [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/itc/cushing/](http://web.archive.org/web/20140319224757/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/itc/cushing/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/itc/cushing/") and Kis [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/bitstream/kis/](http://web.archive.org/web/20140319224757/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/bitstream/kis/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/bitstream/kis/") Don
