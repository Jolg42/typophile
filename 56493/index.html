I don’t think that there was standard for this, it’s one of those things that was determined by manufacturers and would vary dramatically around the world based on the local language. Metal type was also sold by weight, and in the 1912 ATF catalog the scheme is broken down by the weight in pounds and ounces of each letter included in a 100 lb. font—which suggest that the number of each sort would vary based on individual sort weights.

There were / are several standard schemes for assembling fonts at foundries or Monotype houses. Unfortunately, I don't have any info on fonting schemes here at my office, so I'm no help. You might check with some current Monotype shops that still sell fonts. M&H Type in San Francisco or Quaker City Type Foundry in Honey Brook, Pennsylvania. Or Sterling Type in Indiana. Or Bell Type & Rule in Los Angeles. Or Hill & Dale Foundry in West Virginia. Some Monotype houses would also make custom fonting schemes. Or sell 6-inch lines of individual sorts. Depending on what a printer needed. Foundries and Monotype houses also sold "weight fonts," extensions of the standard fonting schemes in larger quantities. Let us know what you find out. powers
