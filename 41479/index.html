I would describe Freight, Stag, and Whitney as somewhere between [humanist](http://web.archive.org/web/20141018210220/http:/typophile.com/wiki/humanist) and [gothic](http://web.archive.org/web/20141018210220/http:/typophile.com/wiki/gothic) or [grotesque](http://web.archive.org/web/20141018210220/http:/typophile.com/wiki/grotesque). Whitney, for example, is based on the structure of a gothic (Trade Gothic) but its finish gives it a more humanist air. Helvetica is commonly categorized as a "neo-grotesque". The "Outside" font is not Gotham, though the caps are very similar. It's most likely something custom from [Font Bureau](http://web.archive.org/web/20141018210220/http:/typophile.com/wiki/Font%20Bureau), who often works with publishers like these. As for their retail fonts, you might dig their Antenna or Amplitude. In the vein of Whitney are [Lucas de Groot](http://web.archive.org/web/20141018210220/http:/typophile.com/wiki/Lucas%20de%20Groot)'s [Taz](http://web.archive.org/web/20141018210220/http:/www.fontshop.com/fonts/downloads/lucasfonts/taz_iii/), [Spiegel](http://web.archive.org/web/20141018210220/http:/www.fontshop.com/fonts/downloads/lucasfonts/spiegel/) and [Corpid](http://web.archive.org/web/20141018210220/http:/www.fontshop.com/search/?q=Corpid). New faces in the neo-grotesque realm include [Maquette](http://web.archive.org/web/20141018210220/http:/www.fontshop.com/fonts/downloads/arstype/maquette/), [Proxima Nova](http://web.archive.org/web/20141018210220/http:/www.fontshop.com/fonts/downloads/mark_simonson/proxima_nova_complete_ot/), [Akkurat](http://web.archive.org/web/20141018210220/http:/lineto.com/The+Fonts/Font+Categories/Text+Fonts/Akkurat/), and [Paralucent](http://web.archive.org/web/20141018210220/http:/www.fontshop.com/search/?q=Paralucent) (though this last one could be called a "neo-neo-grotesque" or "pseudo-neo-grot". If you're interested in typeface classifications I highly recommend getting a copy of [Bringhurst](http://web.archive.org/web/20141018210220/http:/typophile.com/wiki/Robert%20Bringhurst)'s "Elements of Typographic Style".
