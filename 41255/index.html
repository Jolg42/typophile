Groovin is noticeably "1960s". Seymour Chwast's Artone logo (1964) is iconic for this "fat bottom" style. The font is probably named after the Young Rascals song (1967). However, although bell-bottom trousers/pants were invented in the 60s, their heyday was the early 70s.
Anonymous @ 24 Jan 2008 — 9:55am
"Groovin" was a very 60s term that came out of the Haight hippie vernacular. The lettering was best seen in work known as Family Dog [http://www.wolfgangsvault.com/se/family-dog-series/FD.html](http://web.archive.org/web/20131020203502/http:/www.wolfgangsvault.com/se/family-dog-series/FD.html "http://www.wolfgangsvault.com/se/family-dog-series/FD.html") The early 70s was actually what most people remember as 60s look. The Beatles Rubber Soul album shows a glimpse of that fat bottom look. There was a great Art Nouveau influence as well. ChrisL
