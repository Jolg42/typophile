Take a walk through a light industrial area. That's where the true Japanese font wonders lie. You'll find bizarre interpretations our our alphabet, grungy kanji stencil fonts and clunky scripts with descenders chopped off. Bonus: slogans such as "We love wood and humans!"

_Bonus: slogans such as “We love wood and humans!”_ Be sure to visit a couple of 100 yen stores for those, too: ![](http://web.archive.org/web/20130820165924im_/http:/typophile.com/files/surely-use_4718.jpg) Apart from that, just walking around can already be total typographic input overload. I second the restaurants – [lettershapes everywhere](http://web.archive.org/web/20130820165924/http:/homepage.mac.com/altairas_eye/.Pictures/tokyo03/DSCF0021_1.JPG), some [beautiful calligraphy](http://web.archive.org/web/20130820165924/http:/homepage.mac.com/altairas_eye/.Pictures/tokyo06/P1010548.JPG) (and/or [funky stuff](http://web.archive.org/web/20130820165924/http:/homepage.mac.com/altairas_eye/.Pictures/tokyo06/P1010538.JPG)). But even just [stenciled signs](http://web.archive.org/web/20130820165924/http:/homepage.mac.com/altairas_eye/.Pictures/tokyo06/P1010515.JPG), [traffic directions painted on streets](http://web.archive.org/web/20130820165924/http:/homepage.mac.com/altairas_eye/.Pictures/tokyo06/P1010517.JPG), or LED signs are just… amazing. I found it especially funky how the different alphabets/scripts are combined and interchanged (like [here](http://web.archive.org/web/20130820165924/http:/homepage.mac.com/altairas_eye/.Pictures/tokyo07/P1010677.JPG)). BTW if you're in/near Tokyo, and interested in graffiti: while Tokyo doesn't seem to have any really, [Yokohama does](http://web.archive.org/web/20130820165924/http:/homepage.mac.com/altairas_eye/.Pictures/tokyo07/P1010752.JPG).
