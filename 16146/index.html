Anonymous @ 9 Nov 2005 — 7:27pm
Many of the artists and designers that produced posters for the psychedelic 60s were inspired by posters from the 1920s -- 1930s plus later nineteenth century "circus" types. The difference being that things become a little "liquidy" when you are _trippin'_, the result being the wonderful intertwined and melting letterforms. ![](http://web.archive.org/web/20141122183724im_/http:/typophile.com/files/filmore1.jpg) [Victor Moscoso](http://web.archive.org/web/20141122183724/http:/www.victormoscoso.com/) was one of the more prolific designers of the genre. ![](http://web.archive.org/web/20141122183724im_/http:/typophile.com/files/filmore2.jpg) The [The Bindweed Press](http://web.archive.org/web/20141122183724/http:/www.sixtiesposters.com/fd36.htm), San Francisco printed the bulk of the Avalon and Filmore concert posters. [SixtiesPosters.com](http://web.archive.org/web/20141122183724/http:/www.sixtiesposters.com/familydog1.htm) is a great source for info on designers and posters.
