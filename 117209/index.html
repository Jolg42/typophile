Late 19th or early 20th century artwork in the form of a trademark. The most interesting features are the median spurs, the terminals on \_S\_ and the leg on the \_R\_. Median spurs were usually used on Tuscan, i.e. split or fishtail terminal fonts, rather than ones with flat terminals. A font in the latter style was digitized by Dan X. Solo under the name Cavalcade. ![](http://web.archive.org/web/20150415062205im_/http:/typophile.com/files/CavalcadeSoloDoverCircusAlphabetsBookAsIvorySoap_4344.jpg) Unfortunately the only legitimate way to get this font is by purchase of a new or used copy of "24 Circus Display Fonts (Dover Electronic Display Fonts) (CD-ROM and Book)" Previous discussion of a median spur non-Tuscan font [http://typophile.com/node/116784](http://web.archive.org/web/20150415062205/http:/typophile.com/node/116784 "http://typophile.com/node/116784") The trademark could be expanded to make an attractive font, pehraps with plain and rimmed variations. Don
