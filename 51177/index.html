Energetic, elegant script

I’m looking to revitalize a client’s logo. Right now they’re using English 111 Vivace, but it needs new life. It’s currently a wedding florals company, but is expanding to include other florals, as well as event production design (lounge furniture rentals, etc). Each division will have its own logo, as well, but the new parent logo should be energetic, not strictly weddingy (you know what I mean…), but still be recognizable to her current clientele, as she is well established in the wedding industry already. House Industries’ revitalization of Agent Provocateur is a really good example of what I’m trying to do.

Any font suggestions? I love Burgues Script and Compendium Script, but not sure if they’re quite right. Plus my client thinks (on first viewing) that Burgues is too “slanty”…

Thanks!

How does the Agent Provocateur logo factor in? They like it? Have a look at [this thread](http://web.archive.org/web/20131018164826/http:/www.typophile.com/node/50595). Have a look also at two new scripts from [Stephen Rapp](http://web.archive.org/web/20131018164826/http:/typophile.com/wiki/Stephen%20Rapp) that are on the more handwritten end of the spectrum: [Memoir](http://web.archive.org/web/20131018164826/http:/www.typophile.com/node/50595) and [Montague](http://web.archive.org/web/20131018164826/http:/www.typophile.com/node/50595).'' If they are looking for something less "slanty" and more upright, consider then [Biscotti](http://web.archive.org/web/20131018164826/http:/www.fontshop.com/fonts/downloads/font_bureau/biscotti_ot/), [Fling](http://web.archive.org/web/20131018164826/http:/www.fontshop.com/fonts/singles/itc/fling_std_plain/) or [Kursivschrift](http://web.archive.org/web/20131018164826/http:/www.fontshop.com/fonts/downloads/linotype/kursivschrift_complete_vp/), big winners with the Martha Stewart crowd.

Anonymous @ 1 Nov 2008 — 5:56pm
Young Baroque is indeed delicate. It needs to be set at a large size to perform well, something you can't promise with a logo that will appear in various sizes. In that vein, [Ballantines](http://web.archive.org/web/20131018164826/http:/www.fontshop.com/fonts/downloads/elsner_flake/ballantines_script_1_ot/) might strike the right balance. In the end, this is probably a job for a professional letterer who can get you the precise style, weight, and tone you need. I can recommend fellow Typophiles [Michael Clark](http://web.archive.org/web/20131018164826/http:/typophile.com/wiki/Michael%20Clark) and [Mark Simonson](http://web.archive.org/web/20131018164826/http:/typophile.com/wiki/Mark%20Simonson) as well as [Doyald Young](http://web.archive.org/web/20131018164826/http:/typophile.com/wiki/Doyald%20Young), the designer of Young Baroque.
