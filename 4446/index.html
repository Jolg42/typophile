Perhaps I am looking at it backwards. I am seeing a type with idiosyncracies. To me, this is a type with quirks, a type with glyphs or parts of glyphs which break either the patterrn of form seemingly developed in the rest of the font or even different from the "norm" of all typefaces we know. It is like seeing a double-take in film�you are not quite sure that what you saw was "really" what you saw so you look again to confirm it. When I first saw text set in "The Mix," I had to look twice to see if had only partial serifs. On second viewing, it was clear to me that Luc had a very deliberate system going and today I would not say The Mix was idiosyncratic. Even joke fonts like "Ransomnote" (which has a different font for each character) is not idiosyncratic because it is consistent in its inconsistency. Rotis, on the other hand, drives me crazy with its lower case "e"�I would call Rotis idiosyncratic but most people wouldn't even notice. Chris L
