Things were lighter this year than last. Chalk it up to trimmed marketing budgets or perhaps people's hesitancy to travel on the anniversary of 11 Sept. Some highlights. **[STEP](http://web.archive.org/web/20131107164922/http:/www.dgusa.com/)** Got a free copy of their September/October issue. A great magazine. They've moved away from their former dumbed-down, plug-in tips and software tricks into a much more intelligent read. There's also a good piece on [Mark van Bronkhorst's](http://web.archive.org/web/20131107164922/http:/www.mvbfonts.com/) [ITC Conduit](http://web.archive.org/web/20131107164922/http:/www.fontshop.com/shop/view_main.cfm?fuseaction=listitems&searchby=package&searchterm=&searchfor=&searchletter=A&view=package&id=C.100316.0.0&designerid=0). **[Quark](http://web.archive.org/web/20131107164922/http:/www.quark.com/)** Quark was there, sort of. If you count one guy occupying a bar stool and laptop stand. "We don't do trade shows" was the rep's explanation of Quark's new policy. Asking about the upcoming Quark release, he said I'd be blown away by all the nifty features coming in Quark 6, due Q1, 2003. He quickly added that he'd have to kill me if he told me all of them. I think he was bluffing. Regarding OpenType support for the new version: of course. But don't look for full access to all OT features until 7.0. Where does that put us, 2006? How could I not feel bad for this guy? He's representing the market leader in layout software, sitting in the shadow of the buzzing Adobe pavillion across the aisle. What these guys need is a serious cash infusion (for product development and marketing). **[Font Reserve](http://web.archive.org/web/20131107164922/http:/www.fontreserve.com/)** This app looks much cooler than I expected. I've never tried it, but I'm downloading the [free trial](http://web.archive.org/web/20131107164922/http:/www.fontreserve.com/products/trial_mac.html) as I write this. There are the usual features, like creating sets and creating type sample books--stuff you could print for reference. But there are some handy features I wasn't familiar with, like searching or sorting fonts by genre and foundry. What I really miss (now that I'm using OS X) is the ATM Deluxe feature that auto-activates fonts from the background, even if ATM itself is not open, and regardless of whether you have a set 'active.' That's technology you can use.
