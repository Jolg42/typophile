_I would stick with a lowercase c. (with a period) especially if you have e.g. and i.e. in the document._ Tim, you're welcome to do it your way; however, _The Chicago Manual of Style_ recommends: "Common Latin abbreviations such as 'etc,' 'eg,' 'ie,' are set in roman type without punctuation." This includes **c** for **circa** ; **c.** is commonly used as an abbreviation for **century**. So, "circa 16th century" would translate as "c 16th c." Or not...

Anonymous @ 19 Dec 2005 — 10:49am
In CMS, possibly, however I would use punctuation for etc. e.g. i.e., the abbreviation for century commonly used in UK is C. The Oxford house style and Hart's Rules suggest any abbreviation that has the first and last letter needn't be punctuated whereas any other lowercase abbreviation should be. c. 16th C (or c. C16) is how I would set circa 16th century. {edit Bringhurst's quote is "birdshot and wormholes"} Tim
