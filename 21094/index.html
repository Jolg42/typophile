iTunes can be good and evil when it comes to arranging your music. Although, there really is no better way to do this, then have it automagically completed for you. I have a large collection, and I can’t even fathom having to sort them myself at this point. Just be aware that iTunes may not archive things in the most intuitive manner. Sometimes I wonder what the hell that program is thinking, and half of all my problem arise from incorrect information in the ID3 tags. It there are things that you are unclear about, as far as wether your information is correct or not, you might consider using [iEatBrainz](http://web.archive.org/web/20060705204712/http:/www.indyjt.com/software/). This tool attempts to match up a tracks acoustical fingerprint with another track that already exists in a large database. It’s not always right, so definitely exercise caution, but for the most part, I have found it useful. In my own experiences, it is most often the first match that will be correct, although on occasion it will produce several results. When this happens, I usually check a third source, such as Amazon.com to ensure that I am properly tagging the track with the appropriate information. If there is anything i hate more than having corrupt fonts, strange fonts, or just font crap on my machine, it has to be having incorrectly labeled tracks in my iTunes library.
