Anonymous @ 30 Jan 2015 — 3:48pm
The ampersand is the clue here: this is a variation of Copperplate, and as Don said, most likely hand-engraved: [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/linotype/copperplate-gothic/](http://web.archive.org/web/20150214073102/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/linotype/copperplate-gothic/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/linotype/copperplate-gothic/") Widen Engraver’s Gothic a bit, and it might also work (though it has a distinctly different ampersand): [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/paratype/engravers-gothic/](http://web.archive.org/web/20150214073102/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/paratype/engravers-gothic/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/paratype/engravers-gothic/")

Anonymous @ 1 Feb 2015 — 1:46pm
Copperplate Gothic was designed by Frederic W. Goudy for ATF in 1901. The condensed versions shown in my 1923 ATF type book are a good match for the digital ones now available from Linotype [https://www.myfonts.com/fonts/linotype/copperplate-gothic/](http://web.archive.org/web/20150214073102/https:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/linotype/copperplate-gothic/ "https://www.myfonts.com/fonts/linotype/copperplate-gothic/") I have not checked the earlier ATF books, but I expect that the condensed versions were offered before 1914 and would probably have been a familiar face to the French Engraver. Clip the mini serifs and Voilà! Don
