Released in 1948 in the era of great show-card lettering. Compare your first image with this style sample for lettering artists: ![](http://web.archive.org/web/20140803005736im_/http:/typophile.com/files/LoadedBrushScript2_4959.jpg) There are quite a few digitized brush script fonts but none is a perfect match. The closest I was able to find to the tone and general feel of your first image is Signalist by Mike Melvas: [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/mika-melvas/signalist/](http://web.archive.org/web/20140803005736/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/mika-melvas/signalist/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/mika-melvas/signalist/") There are also two fairly close fonts from Filmotype: Filmotype Lakeside: [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/filmotype/lakeside/](http://web.archive.org/web/20140803005736/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/filmotype/lakeside/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/filmotype/lakeside/") and Filmotype Lasalle: [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/filmotype/lasalle/](http://web.archive.org/web/20140803005736/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/filmotype/lasalle/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/filmotype/lasalle/") Lasalle is also available from Scangraphic and Elsner+Flake. BTW the lettering artists for Unfaithfully Yours were busy. They also produced a third version of the movie title: ![](http://web.archive.org/web/20140803005736im_/http:/typophile.com/files/UnfaithfullyYours1948_4_4669.jpg) Don
