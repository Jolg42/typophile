If you are meaning a Sans Serif that follows the same classic feel as Helvetica: A more “square” Sans Serif could be [Univers](http://web.archive.org/web/20081014051733/http:/www.fontshop.com/fonts/downloads/linotype/univers_lt_std_65_bold_ot/), but it is not exactly “squared”. It appears more “square” when compared to Futura or Helvetica. The “square” parts of it are the counters and the bowls, but still, like I said is only has the subtle nuance of “square-ness.” Also, [DIN](http://web.archive.org/web/20081014051733/http:/www.fontshop.com/fonts/downloads/fontfont/ff_din_ot_bold/) might fall into this as well. If you are looking for a square typeface might want to try: [Square Bold](http://web.archive.org/web/20081014051733/http:/www.fontshop.com/fonts/downloads/bitstream/square_721_bold/) [Eurostile](http://web.archive.org/web/20081014051733/http:/www.fontshop.com/fonts/downloads/linotype/eurostile_lt_std_bold_ot/) These faces are as far as you can get before moving into perfectly geometric characters or “techno” fonts (I hate using that term as a classification): [Agency](http://web.archive.org/web/20081014051733/http:/www.fontshop.com/fonts/downloads/font_bureau/agency_bold/) [Bank Gothic](http://web.archive.org/web/20081014051733/http:/www.fontshop.com/fonts/downloads/elsner_flake/bank_gothic_medium/) - small caps
