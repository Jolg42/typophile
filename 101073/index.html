ISPO MUNICH: [Good Times](http://web.archive.org/web/20131022085416/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/typodermic/good-times) with ISPO manually rounded poc: very " [Eurostile](http://web.archive.org/web/20131022085416/http:/www.myfonts.com/search/eurostile)". Looks "most probably custom" to me (possibly no optical correction on /p descender), everything redrawn starting from /o (/c = /o - cut ; /p = /o + vertical stroke). 2 solutions, the quick way [Saturn](http://web.archive.org/web/20131022085416/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/dsgnhaus/saturn) (but outlines don't perfectly match) and the (not so) long way [Reversal](http://web.archive.org/web/20131022085416/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/typodermic/reversal) (needing customization as described above but /o outlines looks to match)

Anonymous @ 6 Mar 2013 — 11:01pm
I'd say "techy geometric sans". First one is very "Sci-Fi lettering" (I can easily imagine it on spacecraft door). Second is simply "very Eurostile" to me. You can have a look to [Ray Larabie Typodermic](http://web.archive.org/web/20131022085416/http:/www.myfonts.com/foundry/Typodermic) fonts, [FontFabric Fonts](http://web.archive.org/web/20131022085416/http:/www.myfonts.com/foundry/Fontfabric) (some more including freebies [here](http://web.archive.org/web/20131022085416/http:/fontfabric.com/category/all-fonts)), [Test Pilot Collective](http://web.archive.org/web/20131022085416/http:/www.myfonts.com/foundry/Test_Pilot_Collective) and [Identikal](http://web.archive.org/web/20131022085416/http:/www.myfonts.com/foundry/Identikal_Collection). My favorite at this moment is [Carl Crossgrove Biome](http://web.archive.org/web/20131022085416/http:/www.myfonts.com/search/Biome). My 2 cent now. Using this kind of fonts is visually risky. They're surely cheesy and appealing, love them at first sight but they're far from being timeless, you can get tired of them very quickly.
