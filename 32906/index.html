ver sacrum

I am trying to find information on a Roman type used by the publisher Philip & Kramer for the Austrian Secessionist magazine “Ver Sacrum.” It may have been produced by Poppelbaum — at least Poppelbaum had a font called “ver sacrum”: it was either the same type or a close copy. Is anyone familiar with it? Does anyone make a digital version?

(The short tail of the ’Q’ also distinguishes your face from others. Edit: the ’G’ seems even more distinctive.)
Anonymous @ 9.Apr.2007 5.56pm
Tupper: thanks! Not an exact match, but LTC Jenson is pretty close — and it, I learn, is based on William Morris’s “Golden Type” of 1890, also a close match. This makes perfect sense, as Austria looked to England for much of its progressive design at the time. The Poppelbaum “ver sacrum” type was certainly just a local variation based on the Kelmscott original. I agree that the “G” is its distinguishing mark; I’m curious where that little spur came from — I can’t recall seeing anything like it.
