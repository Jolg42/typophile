Thank you. This is indeed an exact match. According to the note for the OPTI font it was "Originally a phototype by Filmotype." Unless there was a version with the same digital outlines by another fontmaker before OPTI produced OPTI Racer, this is presumably an original digital creation by OPTI. Does anyone know of a matching digital font before the OPTI font? I only ask because of of the notorious reputation of OPTI/Castcraft as a forger of unauthorized clones of digital fonts. A relatively small proportion of its fonts were original digital creations. Don
Anonymous @ 25 Jan 2015 — 5:08am
Not much help, but I can confirm from Filmotype catalog showings that this was originally Filmotype Racer. I know of no other digital versions. Stuart Sandler bought the rights to the [Filmotype collection](http://web.archive.org/web/20150317154702/http:/old.myfonts.com/foundry/Filmotype/) in 2006 and has since been having several fontmakers create digital versions [for sale](http://web.archive.org/web/20150317154702/http:/old.myfonts.com/browse/foundry/filmotype/?PREFS_SET%5Bipp%5D=96). No Racer yet, though.

Anonymous @ 25 Jan 2015 — 3:30pm
Mike, looking at the example of Racer, we have a design that Oxford University Press choose as the best way of expressing its title theme. I don't know whether OUP used the OPTI digital or a Filmotype analog source. I find the Racer typeface design superior to Fashion Didot for this purpose. Anyone trying something similar for a commercial project today would be stuck in the bind of using a digital font from OPTI -- an orphan foundry where there is no way of obtaining appropriate intellectual property permissions -- or making the font from scratch. I do hope that Stuart Sandler's digitization plan has Racer in the next batch. Cheers. Don
