Well, the archetypal face of this kind is Handel. But it's a little played out and might make people think of Pepsi. If it's just for a logo, you might consider taking a normal square sans and rounding it yourself.

Hej Petter, <cite>a font were the lettter E has round corners</cite> [Jocelyn](http://web.archive.org/web/20111214110211/http:/www.fontshop.com/fonts/downloads/creative_alliance/jocelyn/?sample_text=CONNECT&sample_size=60) by Creative Alliance [FF Droids Sans](http://web.archive.org/web/20111214110211/http:/www.fontshop.com/search/?q=FF+Droids+Sans&site=d&sample_text=CONNECT&sample_size=36)by Donald Beekman [Digitalis](http://web.archive.org/web/20111214110211/http:/www.identifont.com/show?4XY) by Nick Cooke I second Kevin, Handel Gothic is a classic in this field, and so is [Bauhaus](http://web.archive.org/web/20111214110211/http:/www.fontshop.com/search/?q=bauhaus&site=d&sample_text=CONNECT&sample_size=48). Though they both indeed feel dated, they still might provide a starting point for your logo design. <cite>a font that feels like software and safety at the same time</cite> I’m not sure about this requirement. [PTL Notes (Soft/Style)](http://web.archive.org/web/20111214110211/http:/www.primetype.com/faces_info.php?fid=34) has a technical and modern connotation, to me. F
