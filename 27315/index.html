And Brown Gothic and Walburn for the Globe and Mail -- although custom types designed for newspapers and magazines are a separate category, there are so many of them, Century, Times, etc. What foundries do is give the commissioning customer an exclusive licence for a limited time, and then release the face commercially. \> _fonts designed exclusively for a company’s branding_ Martin, are you asking about branding ('signature') typefaces, or the proprietary typefaces? Many popular printing types originated as proprietary designs. Among them are Century (1894), Cheltenham (1896), Times New Roman (1931), Bell Gothic (1937), Californian (1939). In my opinion, Romain du Roi is a fine example of a proprietary typeface... The typeface for the Prussian Railroads (1905) is probably the earliest proprietary design for railway car lettering and signage. It precedes Edward Johnston's typeface for London Underground by ten years. Packard (1913), Metropolitan Oldstyle (1914), Saks-Goudy (1934), CBS Sans and CBS Didot Bodoni (196?), Bloomingdale's Gothic (197?) are some of the long-forgotten American branding typefaces. There are two useful books on the subject: [_Branding with Type_](http://web.archive.org/web/20150221211834/http:/www.amazon.com/gp/product/1568302487/sr=1-1/qid=1154489617/ref=sr_1_1/103-4457932-5827061?ie=UTF8&s=books), and [_American Proprietary Typefaces_](http://web.archive.org/web/20150221211834/http:/www.printinghistory.org/htm/pubs/proprietary-typefaces.html).
