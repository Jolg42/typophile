Anonymous @ 15 December, 2003 - 11:10am
I am continually surprised at how often people assume that some old piece of lettering is type. Old advertising art like that is most likely not type, even if it was mass-produced, and even when it looks like it could be type (i.e., nothing particularly irregular about the letterforms or their treatment). Doing it by hand was the only way back then to have the kind of flexibility and control over the design that we take for granted today.

Of course Mark is right, and so I assumed that what you wanted to do was match the lettering, not find the 'font'. I looked at a number of sans serif fonts, and had trouble finding one that had everything. Futura has the right 's', but its 'u' has no stem; the 'b' from Quay Sans Black was fine, but the 'u' isn't quite right. If I were going to replicate this I would use the 3, o, l and b from Quay Sans Black (above), and use the s from Futura Heavy and modify the Futura u by adding a stem. (see attached version done that way, with some adjustments to size, tracking, and using the Futura'l' for the l and stem of the 'b'.) If that's too much work I'd stay with the Quay Sans version I did above. It would probably be close enough if you weren't comparing them side-by-side. ![Essolub Sim 2](http://web.archive.org/web/20051125084241im_/http:/www.typophile.com/forums/messages/83/22486.gif)
