The Hedera was one of the first punctuation marks of Greek and Romans (beside Interpuncts). In the Middle Ages this meaning was forgotten and it became a decoration for title pages and so on. In Germany we also call it Aldus-Blatt, because the famous italien printer Aldus Manutius used it very frequently. <cite>it would seem that the Hedera/Aldus-Blatt has been given more recognition in Germany than in other countries </cite> Yes, because Germany's most famous publisher of design and typography books ("Hermann Schmidt Verlag") uses it as their brand mark. But I don't think the ancient roots and the name Hedera are well known in Germany or elsewhere. You can find some more information in Parkes "Pause and Effect – an introduction to the history of puntuaction in the west" and in Paul Saenger's "Space between Words". I don't know which other typefaces include a Hedera. But if you find out more, please let me know. I would like to compare the different designs.
