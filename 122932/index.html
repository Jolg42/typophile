Anonymous @ 30 Jan 2015 — 7:16am
In my experience, the pink background indicates a problem with either the glyph name or its Unicode number. For any given character set, the gray character means that the glyph slot is "active" but there's no glyph in it, and the gray background means the glyph belongs to that character set, but there is no glyph present.
Anonymous @ 30 Jan 2015 — 7:28am
If you have Python installed and go to Macros and "Mark Problems" it shows up pink when points are not at the right place or when point should be inserted. @oldnick. Unicode problems show up in red, but only at the unicode value, not at the part were the glyph is previewed. Grey means that the glyph is part of the characterset, but is not active. If you would make an empty glyph, it would not show up grey. ...on the other hand..
