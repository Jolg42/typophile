It's calligraphic blackletter--the kind of writing found in manuscripts/books before the advent of movable type--and most likely hand-drawn. There are dozens of digital versions out there (Old English being one, but it is more ornamented than your sample). These get you close: [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/fontmesa/bella-rose/](http://web.archive.org/web/20150327015243/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/fontmesa/bella-rose/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/fontmesa/bella-rose/") [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/lamatas/liturgisch/](http://web.archive.org/web/20150327015243/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/lamatas/liturgisch/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/lamatas/liturgisch/")

This is indeed Old English, with 1. width compressed to about 65%, 2. made thicker by a parallel line technique, 3. the letters packed together so that they overlap 4. the \_s\_ simplified and edited, particularly so that the lower lhs would line up with the tail on \_a\_. ![](http://web.archive.org/web/20150327015243im_/http:/typophile.com/files/OldEnglishWidthAt65Percent_4194.jpg) [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/mti/old-english-text/](http://web.archive.org/web/20150327015243/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/mti/old-english-text/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/mti/old-english-text/") Don
