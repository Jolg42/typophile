That's a multiplication sign. There are other glyphs to indicate multiplication as well, but “×” apparently is also used in botanical taxonomy to indicate hybrids. Spacing and kerning probably should be handled as it would be as a math symbol. I reason this only by parsing your question and not any extensive knowledge of the relevant subject.
Anonymous @ 2 Oct 2009 — 9:29am
The × does indeed signify hybrid (cross-breeding). I believe that horticultural style guides vary on whether there should be space following the × or not. I think the current tendency is closed up, whereas previously it was open. In many typefaces, the design of the × has it raised above the baseline slightly, depending upon the default figure style. If this is the case and you're using a closed-up style (with no space after), then I suggest you try baseline shifting it down to the baseline (or center on the x-height is also an option).
