Engraving--note the way the tips come to sharp points. Can't do that with metal type. Well executed--differences between repeating letters are not obvious and treatment of serifs etc is very consistent. "Barthelemy Ferrer Fils." is in a style that probably looked a little old fashioned in 1911. Except for the shadow line it is not very different from a narrow 19th century "Latin." Here are a few -- mostly too wide and with differences in details -- Latin CT [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/castletype/latin-ct/](http://web.archive.org/web/20140724184738/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/castletype/latin-ct/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/castletype/latin-ct/") Spike [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/quadrat/spike/](http://web.archive.org/web/20140724184738/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/quadrat/spike/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/quadrat/spike/") Etienne [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/paratype/etienne/ITC](http://web.archive.org/web/20140724184738/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/paratype/etienne/ITC "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/paratype/etienne/ITC") Barcelona [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/itc/barcelona/](http://web.archive.org/web/20140724184738/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/itc/barcelona/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/itc/barcelona/") or More digging might find something closer. As for BF, it's again engraving of an idea to fit the two letters together in a monogram. The big slashing serif on the top stroke of the F is very 19th century. If you want more of this sort of exuberance, I suggest Ronaldson [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/canadatype/ronaldson/](http://web.archive.org/web/20140724184738/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/canadatype/ronaldson/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/canadatype/ronaldson/") or it's offspring Tiffany [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/itc/tiffany/](http://web.archive.org/web/20140724184738/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/itc/tiffany/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/itc/tiffany/") Don
Anonymous @ 15 Mar 2014 — 7:08pm
Don's suggestions are probably better, but I thought I'd mention two I thought might work nicely with an added shadow. They have fewer big latin serifs but, to me, match the upright, more squared-off appearance better. Both date from just before 1900. [Asteroid Primo Two](http://web.archive.org/web/20140724184738/http:/old.myfonts.com/fonts/spiecegraphics/asteroid-primo/) and [Estienne](http://web.archive.org/web/20140724184738/http:/old.myfonts.com/fonts/solotype/estienne/) Unfortunately, in both cases, the 'S' rather gives away the Art Nouveau roots, but Asteroid comes with a small caps version and Asteroid One has lightly ornamented caps for the F and T.

Dan X. Solo used the term Asteroid, but he was known for renaming old typefaces. May be in one of the old type specimen books.
