Time period: of all times — post WWII — digital era — recent? Geography/spread: world-wide — US — Europe — local? Style/Importance: avant-garde/outstanding — idiosyncratic/representative — anything? Which Garamond? General clues for finding typefaces in use: [Search](http://web.archive.org/web/20081011160748/http:/www.typophile.com/node/12344) typophile.com. Most of the faces you need have been asked for before. Like [Meta](http://web.archive.org/web/20081011160748/http:/www.typophile.com/node/42959). Linotype.com has a pretty neat [in use section](http://web.archive.org/web/20081011160748/http:/www.linotype.com/2106/byalphabet.html) Especially if you’re looking for images, [searching Flickr](http://web.archive.org/web/20081011160748/http:/www.flickr.com/search/?q=mrs.%20eaves&w=all) (or similar services) has proven helpful, to me. You might try adding ‘font’ or ‘type’ to your query, when searching for short/ambigous terms like ‘Franklin’, ‘Meta’ or ‘DIN’. F

oh boy, Gill Sans is EVERYWHERE. Twinning’s tea package has it (oh those british) the BBC’s logo (again, british) See Gill Sans was created in the mid-twenties by a british sculptor, Eric Gill. Once you know a little about the typefaces you’re looking for, you can start to find them where you’d expect. I know the font on the keys of my keyboard is VAG rounded. I don’t know where to find another example of it, but considering it was made for Volkswagen AG, I can imagine Volkswagen would be a good starting point for my research. Try to ’meet the type’ as though it’s a child of the creator. You know it’s name, but what’s it’s personality, what’s is nationality. Does it have other brothers and sisters? Cousins? What was it derived from. Best of luck on your little adventure! innovati!
