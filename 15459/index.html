Anonymous @
"But it’s too 70s." Exactly. If you really want flares with that, you might consider a couple of the old ITC faces - [ITC Novarese](http://web.archive.org/web/20110509085843/http:/www.itcfonts.com/fonts/detail.asp?sku=ITC9157) or [ITC Newtext](http://web.archive.org/web/20110509085843/http:/www.itcfonts.com/fonts/detail.asp?sid=VCCLSFKKHEKF8JR0GWD408PSKVFU89H4&sku=ITC9136) are both flared this way, but neither is unique, and the geometry of Variable makes this pairing a bit problematic to harmonise. Alternatively, if the types are not being used in close proximity on the page, you could go for a complete structural contrast in the headlines, captions etc. and try the grandaddy of flare serif types - Berthold Wolpe's [Albertus](http://web.archive.org/web/20110509085843/http:/itc.identifont.co.uk/partner?VCCLSFKKHEKF8JR0GWD408PSKVFU89H4+Albertus+255290+010082113), which is also available in an italic and a light weight these days. Having said all that, I much prefer Tim's idea of contrasting Variable with an Egyptian or Slabserif.

You need a serif typeface that isn't mousy and won't bow to the pressure of being paired with such a overtly modern typeface. Something quirky on its own, I think. What about something from [Fountain](http://web.archive.org/web/20110509085843/http:/www.fountain.nu/) such as Stefan Hattenbach's [Delicato](http://web.archive.org/web/20110509085843/http:/www.fountain.nu/catalogue/delicato.asp)? So the faces won't be used on the same page? OR The serif will be text and the sans will be head and sub-head? I think you will be updating regularly if you choose to use such a modern typeface as trends change so quickly. I suppose this can be seen as a positive as the art world changes its mind as to what is vogue from week to week as well.
