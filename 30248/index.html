First italic sans?

This may be a simple question, but I can’t find an answer anywhere, including at typophile.
Does anybody have a clue of what was the first metal italic (or in fact slanted sans serif font?
My guess from the material that I could look into would be something in a field where many different typefaces are needed in one composition: map printing or the like (on the other hand, maps were seldomly printed from lead letters apart from trials by Raffelsberger and others, but either engraved or later lithographically reproduced from ~1800 on).
So any clues that could point into the right direction? Akzidenz Grotesk?

Whatever it was, it probably wasn't AG as AG was cobbled together from other grotesks purchased by H. Berthold A.G.. The Berthold specimens from the 1960s on [Uli's site](http://web.archive.org/web/20131106143233/http:/www.sanskritweb.net/forgers/#BERTHOLD) has italics (kursiv), these weights are not mentioned in the list of weights and releases up to 1958 published in _100 Jahre Berthold_, which suggests to me that either they were added by G.G. Lange when he redesigned AG in the late 1950s or that they existed previously and nobody cared enough to list them in what writings and specimen books survive. Either way Lange would probably know.

Anonymous @ 31 Dec 2006 — 7:08am
James, in general the type founders were the last to get hold of a style, after signwriters, lettering artists, architects, stonemasons and calligraphers had had their way with it. "Phantom" was one of the few display styles that were "type first", from the 1830s, because type founders used engineering tools. ![](http://web.archive.org/web/20131106143233im_/http:/typophile.com/files/Phantom_5714.jpg) For letter genres which originate with type, one should look to text type, although even here engraving played a strong role.
