Be careful what you post (copyright related)

So a week or two ago I posted a logo for critique. I hadn’t yet registered the domain name and put up a business card design that contained the proposed domain.

http://typophile.com/node/60849

My client was supposed to register the domain last week and didn’t. This morning I ran across this:

http://stoneandwoodconcepts.com/

I’ve contacted the company, who does actually operate doing countertops in another geographic area, and they’re willing to sell the domain “as a reasonable price that covers their costs”. I also informed him they’re in violation of copyright law and he seemed to think it was something that their designer came up with (dates on this are easily provable).

If you do a google search for “granite countertop logo” my original thread comes up as the last result on the first page. You can put the pieces together to see what happened.

Moral of the story: watch your backs guys, and apparently trust no one.

Digital fonts are software products and the copyright laws that apply to software applies to fonts. Generally speaking concepts cannot be copyrighted but coding can. The U.S. Copyright Act provides a set of exclusive legal rights to the owner (or “author”) of a copyrightable work (which includes software and thus fonts): the right to reproduce the copyrighted works; the right to prepare “derivative works”; and the right to distribute (sell, license, etc.). This set of “exclusive” rights includes the right to block others from doing the same thing. In the case of fonts made using font-making software, your design, when published, will constitute unique code which has been generated by your exclusive design and it is this unique code that is copyrighted even though you did not directly write the code line by line. It is a manifestation of your concept - and is thus subject to copyright. The name - if unique - is a trademark issue.
