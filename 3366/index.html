Anonymous @ Tue, 2004-03-02 15:16
OK ... some dissenting points. 1) I certainly agree with the desire to have posters be honest about their intentions. However, requiring every poster to show a respect for type is just not realistic or even, IMO, reasonable. The vast majority of people out there just don't know enough about typography to have respect for it. As a result, we see plenty of posts from people who ignorantly believe that one can get a freeware/shareware font comparable to Verdigris. It seems to me that education would be more appropriate (and effective) than scolding. 1a) This type of font request is, IMO, very different from those occasionally seen on the MyFonts forum, where one poster actually stated, "I'm not going to pay money for fonts!". That type of post goes well beyond ignorance and reflects a relatively recent, largely generational attitude that it's not necessary to pay for fonts, music, software, etc. Those people (and they're legion) definitely need verbal spankings. 2) Mr. van Bronkhorst's creations are truly splendid and he does indeed deserve whatever price he asks. However, he rigidly offers his fonts as a package only. Certainly there are many situations in which paying $159 to obtain a single desired font is out of the question. I can think of two fairly recent posts here by well respected mainstays of this forum asking for alternatives in (I believe) similar situations. I can't find those posts just now, but I remember them verbally "walking on eggshells" for fear of condemnation. In the case of the home hobbyist, noncommercial user or even small firm, posters desiring a font they cannot reasonably afford have two choices: They can ask that someone e-mail them the font (in which case they need a verbal whacking) or they can seek a cheaper alternative. I see no reason why we cannot help out the latter poster, educating them along the way. Fonts bundled with applications (wihch the poster may even already have) can be a great option. And most of those fonts supplied with MS apps (mostly Monotype) are of very high quality.

Anonymous @ Wed, 2004-03-03 10:14
I might as well put in my two cents here too, since this is an issue I am pretty opinionated about, like many of you who have commented earlier. I find myself most in agreement with Mike Freiman, although in the right circumstances I feel like Yves too. The difference is in the purpose or intent of the user, and unfortunately we are usually only able to take their word for it about those things. If someone is willing to take a near-match because they can't afford the real thing for the purpose at hand, or if they are just looking for something with certain similar qualities, I think we can refer them to places where that can be found. If they want to get the real fonts for free they can go somewhere else, and I think we ought to refer them to a special FAQ page about why that sort of question is improper, unethical and unfair (and whatever other terms Yves used above). My 'gray area' happens when the font they want is only available as a 'clone' version, as far as we know. I will usually recommend those fonts. The FontBank fonts are a good example, because I think often the font the posters are trying to duplicate came from that collection in the first place. I am all for education, and promotion of original, quality fonts. That's why I like to credit the designers -- so people realize this was someone's creative work. However, I realize, as Mike said, that there are situations when that expense can't be justified. Those are times when we should suggest alternatives, but I don't mean rip-offs of the same font. We should explain that if you aren't willing to pay for it, then you must be willing to settle for something else, but here are some things that might be similar that come with applications you may have, etc. I think it's a challenge, because most of us don't spend much time looking for cheap approximations, but if we have ideas for them, that would be the place for it. The last thing we need to try to be doing is guessing at their motives, because there is no assurance that they will tell us the truth. You either want the original,and will pay for it, or you are willing to take something similar that we might suggest.
