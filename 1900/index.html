Looking for 1910's Resources

I am interested in design / type styles of the first two decades of the twentieth century: 1900-1919. What I’m looking for, specifically, are designs that are not Arts & Crafts or Art Nouveau, but more of a general commercial vernacular from this era (advertisements, logos, signage, package designs, etc.)

It seems that the design history books I have are pretty thin on this period, especially regarding things that don’t fit into a larger movement (such as Arts & Crafts, Deco–which I know came later, and so on).

I’ve done a search on MyFonts.com, and found some good type examples, but I’d also like to see more full-blown designs of type in use.

Does anyone have any suggestions of books or other resources I could try?

Thanks for any help,

I don't know where you are situated Troy, but here in Toronto (pop. 2 mil.) we have a number of stores that trade and sell old magazines, comic-books, paper ephemera. Anonymous @ Fri, 2003-10-31 14:06
Troy Probably the best place to look would be the various trade journals (on printing) that were available at about this time: Inland Printer, British Printer, The Graphic Arts, The American Printer, etc. Most major University library collections would carry these. These are very useful in seeing how things changed from decade to decade, especially in regard to typeface offerings. The late 20s and the early 30s are the heydays typographically,but some very interesting, and unsual, typeface entries can be found from the late 1890s up into the 20s. Gerald
