Computer Modern and Sabon?

Hi. Computer Modern may not be typographically perfect, but it’s a good font for displaying maths (and has the most extensive support for LaTeX, as far as I know). For text, it’s not the best choice, in my view. I think that Sabon (I’m using the Monotype version) is a good font to be used together with Computer Modern for math content. (Garamond is too “round”, in my opinion, although I would prefer it - Garamond Stempel - for text without math content. Note: For texts that need a more personal style, I’d use other fonts, however.) What do you think about the choice?

[cschroeppel](http://web.archive.org/web/20120416001947/http:/typophile.com/user/42308): <cite>I think that Sabon... is a good font to be used together with Computer Modern for math content.</cite> _Computer Modern_ is a Didone, and as such does not sit very well on a line of type with any other face, even another Didone such as _Didot_ or _Bulmer._ A Neoclassical face such as _Baskerville_ may work better, and there are some enticing similarities between Computer Modern and _[Plantagenet](http://web.archive.org/web/20120416001947/http:/www.tiro.com/Plantagenet/plantagenet_index3.html)._ The greatest difficulty you will have is matching the weight. If _Garamond_ does not work for you, how about pairing Computer Modern with a Garamond that is not a Garamond at all? Namely, a ‘Garamond’ that is based on the work of Jean Jannon, such as Monotype _‘Garamond’_ or Simonicini _‘Garamond’?_ ![](http://web.archive.org/web/20120416001947im_/http:/typophile.com/files/SimoniciniGaramond_ComputerModern_5803.png) Finally, have you considering using Hermann Zapf's _Euler_? That would allow you to select among Zapf's other designs, such as _Aldus_ or _Palatino,_ for a companion face. ![](http://web.archive.org/web/20120416001947im_/http:/typophile.com/files/Zaph-Aldus-Euler_specimen_5220.png) The glyph coverage of Euler might not be as large as Computer Modern, but it is an eminently more handsome face.

Monotype _Bulmer_ has a range of weights (regular, semi-bold, bold, display) and expert character sets (text figures, small caps, ligatures, fractions, etc.). <cite>(Are there any bold weights for that particular font.)</cite> Ask yourself, do you really need bold? In the case of _Bulmer_ the heavier weights are surely retroactive additions to the design. If you want a typeface that comes with a bold weight and is at all honest with itself, you should look to 20th century designs instead. ![](http://web.archive.org/web/20120416001947im_/http:/typophile.com/files/HermannZapf_Melior_4112.png) Hermann Zapf's _Melior_ dates from the 1950s and is suitable for setting extended text. ![](http://web.archive.org/web/20120416001947im_/http:/typophile.com/files/ITCZapfInternational_5811.png) _Zapf International_ dates from 1977 but is more suitable for titling.
