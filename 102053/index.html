Anonymous @ 6 Apr 2013 — 6:57am
For a technically-oriented manual I'd be inclined to go for something conservatively plain and unobtrusive, with well-disambiguated characters (e.g. no O/0 or 1/l/I confusion) that nonetheless reads fairly comfortably. If, as Hrant suggests, you don't want to spend money, there are some good free (open source) options. To name three: [Linux Libertine](http://web.archive.org/web/20150416052748/http:/www.linuxlibertine.org/index.php?id=1&L=1), [TeX Gyre Schola](http://web.archive.org/web/20150416052748/http:/www.gust.org.pl/projects/e-foundry/tex-gyre/schola/index_html) (a version of Century Schoolbook), or [Bitstream Charter](http://web.archive.org/web/20150416052748/http:/www.ctan.org/tex-archive/fonts/charter/). I imagine any one of those would do well. Georgia might work well too, although if your manual makes heavy use of tabular numbers you might find the old-style figures awkward. (Though there are ways around that, of course.)
