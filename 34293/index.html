Anonymous @ 7.Jun.2007 4.06am
Flash portfolios have to be REALLY elegantly simple for me to bother looking through them. Most of these seem to suffer from the same Flash issues: - loading bars everywhere - giant sweeping page transitions - mystery meat navigation - annoying sounds I’m a big fan of portfolios like this: [http://analog.sys.is/](http://web.archive.org/web/20070714143149/http:/analog.sys.is/ "http://analog.sys.is/") Yea, this is maybe extreme (everything on one page) but it makes it very easy for me to quickly scan the body of work. One of the few Flash-based portfolio sites that I’ve found nice is this one: [http://www.geredonovan.com/](http://web.archive.org/web/20070714143149/http:/www.geredonovan.com/ "http://www.geredonovan.com/") A little gratuitous/cliche with the scrolling text transitions, but, overall, lightweight and not crazy on the special effects. I also like Ty’s (haven’t seen him in here in a while) from a pure minimalist standpoint: [http://www.tywilkins.com/](http://web.archive.org/web/20070714143149/http:/www.tywilkins.com/ "http://www.tywilkins.com/")

The Manchester based magazine Rant! had a flash website a while ago which allowed the user to turn pages exactly as you do with a printed magazine (it reminded vaguely the [http://www.geredonovan.com/](http://web.archive.org/web/20070714143149/http:/www.geredonovan.com/ "http://www.geredonovan.com/") site except that it was much more literal, you had to turn the page with the cursor and it was a great feel. The page turned gradually from the bottom right corner until you had moved the cursor to the left side then the page would gently fall and the next page was gradually reveal. Brilliant. Not incredibly intuitive but extremely rewarding. The site doesn’t exist anymore, has anyone seen this effect somewhere else by any chance?
