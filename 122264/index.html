Anonymous @ 30 Dec 2014 — 5:14pm
Edited: The nameplate of prop newspaper in the Sherlock Holmes move is an authentic reproduction of the engraved nameplate of a British newspaper called \_The Penny Illustrated Paper and Illustrated News\_. Compare to a letterhead from Nov. 5. 1881 ![](http://web.archive.org/web/20150215061009im_/http:/typophile.com/files/ThePennyIllustrated18811105DetailSM_5252.jpg) I have not seen this particular lettering as a typeface. That is not to say that such a typeface was not made. BTW the British Library announced in 2010 that this newspaper, and many others, will be available in digital form for online reading. Press release: [http://blogit.realwire.com/british-library-and-brightsolid-partnership-t](http://web.archive.org/web/20150215061009/http:/blogit.realwire.com/british-library-and-brightsolid-partnership-t "http://blogit.realwire.com/british-library-and-brightsolid-partnership-t")... and [http://chnm.gmu.edu/worldhistorysources/w/276.html](http://web.archive.org/web/20150215061009/http:/chnm.gmu.edu/worldhistorysources/w/276.html "http://chnm.gmu.edu/worldhistorysources/w/276.html") Edited: Additional Information. Unfortunately the links do not actually lead to the digitized newspapers. By 1884 the idea of a concave lettering was made into a font by Marder, Luse & Co, which they called Concave. A version of concave was digitized as Show [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/juan-jose-lopez/show/](http://web.archive.org/web/20150215061009/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/juan-jose-lopez/show/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/juan-jose-lopez/show/") But there are considerable differences between Show and the nameplate lettering \_ILLUSTRATED PAPER\_. Don
