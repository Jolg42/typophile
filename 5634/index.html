Linotype has several extensive, highly affordable packages available. [http://www.linotype.com/6-1788-6/compilationvaluepackages.html](http://web.archive.org/web/20050516051627/http:/www.linotype.com/6-1788-6/compilationvaluepackages.html) I would recommend buying one of the following four packages (and then using the rest of your money to buy from some independent foundries, like Jim's, or maybe [www.typofonderie.com/](http://web.archive.org/web/20050516051627/http:/www.typofonderie.com/), or [www.fontbureau.com](http://web.archive.org/web/20050516051627/http:/www.fontbureau.com/), [www.fountain.nu](http://web.archive.org/web/20050516051627/http:/www.fountain.nu/), or [www.buymyfonts.com](http://web.archive.org/web/20050516051627/http:/www.buymyfonts.com/)): Linotype Essentials 1 cost 400 Euro (you can pay in dollars, of course� costs in little bit more because the dollar has deflated recently), and includes a great selection of established classic typefamilies from the Linotype Library. Includes Helvetica Neue and Bauer Bodoni. [http://www.linotype.com/6-1790-6/linotypeessentials1.html](http://web.archive.org/web/20050516051627/http:/www.linotype.com/6-1790-6/linotypeessentials1.html) Linotype Essentials 2 is 465 Euro, and has slightly more less common families, like Sabon and Zapfino. [http://www.linotype.com/6-1791-6/linotypeessentials2.html](http://web.archive.org/web/20050516051627/http:/www.linotype.com/6-1791-6/linotypeessentials2.html) Fresh Cream has Akira Kobayashi's Conrad, which is the best new typeface ever (imho), along with some other durable families. Gerard Unger's Praxis and Demos are here, too, which have recently been selected as the official typefaces of the German government. 365 Euro [http://www.linotype.com/6-1912-6/linotypefreshcream.html](http://web.archive.org/web/20050516051627/http:/www.linotype.com/6-1912-6/linotypefreshcream.html) Frutiger's Life has all of the typefaces designed by Adrian Frutiger that have as of yet been digitized. Sharon, if you want New England, you really should look more into Font Bureau ( [www.fontbureau.com](http://web.archive.org/web/20050516051627/http:/www.fontbureau.com/) Boston), or anything from Tobias Frere-Jones at Hoefler & Frere-Jones Typography ( [www.typography.com](http://web.archive.org/web/20050516051627/http:/www.typography.com/)). Bitstream is in New England, too... and MyFonts... at least they're _headquartered_ in Cambridge. After having lived in Providence for four years, I really get a kick out of the Providence Type Foundry ( [http://www.provtype.com/index2.html](http://web.archive.org/web/20050516051627/http:/www.provtype.com/index2.html)), too; or at least its font names. Unfortuantely, I can't say that Linotype has anything distinctly "New England." We do sell a lot of typefaces to New Englanders, though. Yves, I think that **all** of Linotype's products are reasonalbly priced now (no, I'm not saying this just because I work in the _marketing_ department). I mean, the gold edition will still set you back 10 grand, but it is ENORMOUS. Most of the font collections are very nicely priced. The Take Type Collections [http://www.linotype.com/6-1785-6/taketype.html](http://web.archive.org/web/20050516051627/http:/www.linotype.com/6-1785-6/taketype.html) are really broad, with a lot of one-off display faces, but they all have a few good text families (including the first appearance of Conrad, on Take Type 3!). Our new special packs sell five or six fonts apiece, at an average of 6 EUR a font, I think. [http://www.linotype.com/6-1788-6/compilationvaluepackages.html](http://web.archive.org/web/20050516051627/http:/www.linotype.com/6-1788-6/compilationvaluepackages.html) Plus, we will soon be offering student discounts (if we haven't had them before) And in the name of good, fair competition, I should add that the best of Unger's fonts are available from us, too ;)
