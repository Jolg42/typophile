I'm not an expert on medical positioning, but I've often admired the clean and crisp look of Stefan Hattenbach's [Stalemate](http://web.archive.org/web/20150316232840/http:/www.fountain.nu/catalogue/stalemate.asp), available from Fountain. According to the website, Stalemate "was originally constructed as a proprietary font for a German IT-company." Moreover, it "was designed to work both in print and on screen and experience shows that it performs well in both environments." It could be just what you're looking for. Here's a link to a [PDF sample](http://web.archive.org/web/20150316232840/http:/www.fountain.nu/catalogue/img/pdf/FTN_Stalemate.pdf) as well. Good luck!

Hi Diane, First off, I'm not quite a professional yet, I'm a student, so my suggestions may not be 100%, but here they are anyways :) If you're going to be using a sans for the body copy, I'd recommend a serif font, or a sans with some character for the display type. Here's just a few of my suggestions… Democratica if a quirky font that is modern/hi-tech, and I don't know it seems kinda 'medical' to me [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/emigre/democratica/](http://web.archive.org/web/20150316232840/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/emigre/democratica/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/emigre/democratica/") Modern 735 is more conservative, but certainly is professional, and modern. [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/bitstream/modern-735/](http://web.archive.org/web/20150316232840/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/bitstream/modern-735/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/bitstream/modern-735/") Kingsbury Conensed is interesting.
