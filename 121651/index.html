Anonymous @ 29 Nov 2014 — 3:36am
About the Instagram reworking, I understand the need to smooth the logo down and to make it more a logo and less a word set in a nice font. But I think the result took away too much of the liveliness from the original. In this regard, for the g\_r pair probably a more “twisted” curve would have helped (less tame loop for |g|, less wide counter for |r|, a bit like your Fertigo Script sample\*). As for Authentica, it’s not the first time someone is called out for ripping off Wisdom Script (see [http://typophile.com/node/93171](http://web.archive.org/web/20150428171556/http:/typophile.com/node/93171 "http://typophile.com/node/93171")). If you need to “improve” upon an existing script, at least do it with something which allows it (example: [https://www.behance.net/gallery/Blenda-Script-Free-Font/13832171](http://web.archive.org/web/20150428171556/https:/www.behance.net/gallery/Blenda-Script-Free-Font/13832171 "https://www.behance.net/gallery/Blenda-Script-Free-Font/13832171")). \* Although in Fertigo |g|’s loop could look a bit cramped, and the joining a bit thin.

Good point: liveliness vs. regularity in script font design. This seems to me to be perhaps the most difficult issue for a script font maker. The new \_Instagram\_ logo does indeed seem to emphasize regularity at the expense of liveliness. Blenda Script as a remake of Lobster -- one of the most successful font designs of recent years -- is not close to \_Instagram\_ because it is too crisp and regular for a match. Is it an improvement?
