Lowercase a,g opinions.

Anybody have strong opinions/terrifying childhood memories of lowercase a and g?

  1. Were you confused the first time you noticed that your schoolbooks used the “double-decker” type, while your teacher harped on you about “not worrying about it” and focusing on the single-story versions in your handwriting?

  2. In Sans faces: should the two forms ever be mixed (double-decker a with single-story g, or vice-versa)? (This one’s serious.)

  3. Do the Germans have it right? (Or are they just heavily laden with Besessenheitmitvollkommengeometrischgestalt?)

1. No. I have no memory at all of my first consciousness of the difference between the single- and double-storey a and g. I have no memory of ever having a problem with the distinction. However, I learned to read quite late (around seven years old), and I suspect most of the books I competently read first used the double-storey versions. By the way, there's something to be said for learning to read after one has developed a reasonable vocabulary: one gets to start with much more interesting books. 2. Yes. I think there are very few non-italic typefaces in which the single-storey a looks good, while the either version of the g can be used much more liberally. Frutiger is a good example of a typeface that very successfully matches the double-storey a with the single-storey g, but there are very many others.
