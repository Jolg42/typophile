I would suggest that you use not only an en-dash to separate a range, try a "thin" space (Command-Shift-Option-M in InDesign) instead of a full space. I would definitely turn off hyphenation in the paragraphs below "Experience". It might also help eliminate the "orphan" [http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Widows\_and\_orphans](http://web.archive.org/web/20100201031004/http:/en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Widows_and_orphans "http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Widows\_and\_orphans") in the second paragraph. Even though the rule of "never end a sentence with a preposition" is no longer accepted as hard grammar, I find that ending a line break with a preposition, that is, separating the preposition from its object, makes scanning more difficult. Try keeping prepositional phrases together if you can. With a résumé, you want it as scannable as possible. I might even consider (shudder) bulleted lists instead of the compounded sentence with all those semicolons.
Anonymous @
I suggest changing "information" to "Contact" (isn't the entire cv information?). Delete using the actual word "Address" and group all of your addresses under the bar, the same with your Education information. Your design makes sense with how you organized your employment, having the position in one column and the responsibilities under the gray bar, but to use that same treatment for contact info and listing your schools doesn't work. Or you could have the bars align with the left column and handle your name in a different manner. Your spacing is inconsistent, compare the white space from the bottom of the gray bar to the base line of "School Address" and the white space from the bottom of the gray bar to the baseline of "Saint Joseph" and same for third bar as well.
