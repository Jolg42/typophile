Anonymous @ 6 May 2009 — 6:06pm
Lettering and typography can share some usage in that they both attempt to present letterforms constructed as language and are meant to help convey content. One is a one-time use, the other is a repurposing of a modular system of movable elements. To create lettering, it takes a different kind of skill than it does to compose type. Part of this is technical in nature and part of this is a combination of skill, talent, and craft. The lettering artist can apply his skills to also be a typeface designer. A typographer can do the same but has more to learn about form creation to be successful(given he has the talent). I purposely omit the mention of tools since they have varied throughout the history of both disciplines (lettering being much older as well). Anyone has to learn the tools of his trade but learning the tools does not mean they will be applied to good end. ChrisL

Anonymous @ 6 May 2009 — 3:18pm
I'm all for permissive rather than prescriptive use of language. Most of us here know what we would call typical, unambiguous examples of typography or lettering. Then there are all sorts of borderline cases as beautifully illustrated above. The disciplines have influenced each other plenty since the invention of type. With technological advances type is more and more able to imitate lettering, and in turn lettering may be used as elements in a typographical treatment. As Miss Tiffany has pointed out, typography is itself a broad term, so there's more room for confusion. I think rather than try to rigorously define the terms for all situations, we should clarify what we mean by the different terms only when the need arises, and even then concede there are nebulous areas.
