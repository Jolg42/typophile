Anonymous @ 21 February, 2006 - 7:43am
I could be wrong but I’m not sure if there’s such a thing as calibrating your monitor for WEB design other than ensuring your monitor is displaying the broadest range of colours by correctly setting brightness/contrast and gamma levels. This can be done with the Display Calibrator Assistant (OS X) or the software enclosed with your monitor. As for the preset ICC profiles, I’ve never had much luck with them. Another thing to consider is the fact PC and Macs display on-screen colours differently by default. Mac’s use a brightness gamma of 1.8 while PC’s are usually darker at 2.0 or 2.2. Keep this in mind as the colour that appears on your monitor will not appear the same on all monitors. For web design, use sRGB colour space! You know that already. :) However I work predominantly in print so I use [Spyder](http://web.archive.org/web/20060525031724/http:/www.colorvision.com/profis/profis_view.jsp?id=101) to ensure my monitor colours represent Pantone colours as accurately as possible. Hope this helps.

Ok. By using the Spyder to create a monitor specific profile, I can cause all my apps to display the images using the custom profile. Note, this isn’t converting the image. I have a third party RIP that also has good profiles so I’m able to A.) fix color casts in photography and B.) give a color correct proof from my own system. This allows me to get good results with marginal quality digital photos that come from different sources, and screen correct them so they match. By giving accurate color proofs myself I can show a client what they should expect, and keep the approval process on press more streamlined. More detail? Color profiles and the ICC have really been a subject of much dispute ever since the notion of a “streamlined workflow” came about. I work at a large print shop as a pre-press operator, and I prepare files for our offset presses, both being Komori Lithrone 40 models, one six color, and the other eight. All of our equipment is no calibrated, lol. Do not ask me why, but it just is not. It seems that even when things are calibrated correctly, human factors take over, and it all has to do with what the customer wants. Regardless of wether the monitors are calibrated to the press, or the proofing systems, if at the time of press check, the customer is not happy, it will be adjusted on press, or worse yet, re-platted. Most all the files that we get, go through numerous phases of retouching, proofing, and re-proofing. Nothing is ever right, until the client says it is, no matter if the print matches whats on the screen.
