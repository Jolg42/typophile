Anonymous @ 12 December, 2003 - 2:23pm
Mark & Dan, you might be interested in this quote from an e-mail I recently received from a long-time graphic designer: "Yes, old engraving companies in Washington DC still use the pantograph for engraving. The process is very simple: the alpahabet was machined on heavy steel plates: upper and lowercase; numbers and all the other stuff (ampersands, etc.). I wrote out the copy backwards in pencil on a long continuous paper tape. For each letter of each word and punctuation I would trace it from the steel plate. There was a little gauge where I set the leading. To find the middle of the plate I used dividers (which is the reason I don't measure for anything -- I just find the middle and divide in half) -- okay so while I'm tracing the letters and moving the steel plate to each letter another thing, the stylus is etching the letters lightly on coated steel or copper plate. There was something I used to size the letters to, probably another lever. It was a contraption for sure. After the plate was etched it was dropped briefly into a vat of acid. The brown stuff wash away and there was a light line for the engraver to follow. So the engraver, a very old man, popped the loop onto his eye, grabbed his engraving tools and carved the steel or copper plate. Then the plates would go downstairs to the plate stamping machine. They would strap the plate to the bottom of this huge shaft that probably weighed a ton, and let it mash down on the paper. Voila: engraving. These men I worked with were absolutely great titans of a long lost age: skilled, patient, accurate beyond the beyond. They taught me my work ethic." She also worked for years with Compugraphic film type setting equipment. I was amazed to learn we both attended the same high school in Queens NY for at least one year at the same time (1961), so I guess that tells you neither of us is a 'Spring chicken'.
