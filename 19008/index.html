IN PERSON: There were very few rounded fonts used then. That's probably the bleeding ink of something like [Futura Extra Bold](http://web.archive.org/web/20120610171354/http:/www.fontshop.com/index.cfm?fuseaction=catalog.fontdetail&displayfontid=C.45.0.3&attributes.sampleSize=48&sampleText=IN+PERSON&sampleSize=48). Futura or [Tempo](http://web.archive.org/web/20120610171354/http:/www.fontshop.com/index.cfm?fuseaction=catalog.fontdetail&displayfontid=LH.121017.0.1&attributes.sampleSize=48&sampleText=CARNEGIE+HALL&sampleSize=48) is used for the other small type. The big wide stuff can be emulated with Font Bureau's newly released [Titling Gothic](http://web.archive.org/web/20120610171354/http:/www.fontshop.com/index.cfm?fuseaction=catalog.fontpackage&startrow=1&records=&currpage=1&totalrecords=7&displayfontid=FB.122839.0.0&fontRendererText=JUDY+GARLAND) or [Rhode Extended](http://web.archive.org/web/20120610171354/http:/www.fontshop.com/index.cfm?fuseaction=catalog.fontpackage&startrow=1&records=&currpage=1&totalrecords=4&displayfontid=FB.100197.0.0&fontRendererText=JUDY+GARLAND), or [ITC Blair](http://web.archive.org/web/20120610171354/http:/www.fontshop.com/index.cfm?fuseaction=catalog.fontdetail&displayfontid=ITC.103137.0.3&attributes.sampleSize=48&sampleText=JUDY+GARLAND&sampleSize=24).
