Are you sure that space around the numeral 1 isn’t due to the font metrics themselves? Fonts with [tabular figures](http://web.archive.org/web/20081206132756/http:/typophile.com/wiki/tabular%252Bfigures) always have more space around the 1 because the numerals are monospaced. To set text and headlines I would use a font with [proportional figures](http://web.archive.org/web/20081206132756/http:/typophile.com/wiki/proportional%252Bfigures).

I think the vast space around the ’1’s indicates that Highlander’s figures are indeed tabular, as is the case with most fonts in the ITC range. Not sure what is happening in the ’2’ and ’5’ rows. Here are two alternatives to consider that have proportional figs: [ITC Legacy Sans](http://web.archive.org/web/20081206132756/http:/www.fontshop.com/fonts/singles/itc/itc_legacy_sans_bold_italic_osf/?sample_text=Legacy%201234567890&sample_size=48) [Sauna](http://web.archive.org/web/20081206132756/http:/www.fontshop.com/fonts/singles/underware/sauna_bold_italic/?sample_text=Sauna%200123456789&sample_size=48) You’ll note that these are _oldstyle_ or text figures, which means they descend and ascend like lowercase letters. Your options of fonts with proportional _lining_ figures (numerals of the same height) will increase substantially if Freetype can take advantage of the figure options in OpenType fonts. Can it?
