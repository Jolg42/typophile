Binding Options

Hello,

I am looking for options for binding systems for short printed documents. Ten to 60 page count, and copy and card stock paper will be used with the possibility of screen-printed covers.

No hard covers and preferably lay flat (I am open to other options). Coil and ring binding are too assembly line; I am looking for something a little more classy.

I would love to hear your ideas.

Never done this with with cardstock covers (only board and wood). And never with a single signature. But coptic binding might be worth a shot. It'll lay totally flat, and it looks pretty classy. For a single signature you could use a (what we call a) cahier stitch. Which is basicly a simplified version of the above mentioned coptic stich, which you can use when you need to bind multiple signatures together. see: [http://www.ambachthuys.nl/plaatjes/handboekbinden15.jpg](http://web.archive.org/web/20130820040904/http:/www.ambachthuys.nl/plaatjes/handboekbinden15.jpg "http://www.ambachthuys.nl/plaatjes/handboekbinden15.jpg")

Anonymous @ 22 Dec 2009 — 6:07pm
It's hard to find a cheap binding method for small quantities that beats the comb, velo, or thermal binding. There are somewhat expensive ($5K+) desktop perfect binding machines that will do a fine job, but the upfront cost may be prohibitive. I have a friend that loves these [Circa](http://web.archive.org/web/20130820040904/http:/www.levenger.com/PAGETEMPLATES/NAVIGATION/Products.asp?Params=category=326%7Clevel=2%7Cpageid=1749) things for binding things around his office. I've seen [Treasury tags](http://web.archive.org/) used for documents like you describe, but it may or may not be the look you're after.
Anonymous @ 22 Dec 2009 — 6:18pm
Ah, coptic and pamphlet bindings are probably too involved for something like that. I have seen small desktop systems for poor man's perfect binding. You have to use specific covers that have a channel of glue pre-applied to the spine. Arguably slicker than comb and coil, and probably adequate for small corporate jobs.
Anonymous @ 22 Dec 2009 — 8:07pm

Have you thought about staples? Also it's possible to have your cover go over your comb/sprial spine, to make it visually more attractive.
