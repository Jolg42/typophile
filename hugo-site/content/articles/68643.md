For example, in the English word 'sheath' the pairs 'sh', 'ea', and 'th' are all digraphs. Digraphs, trigraphs, etc. are strategies used by orthographies to represent more sounds and distinctions than are possible using a limited inventory of single letters. Digraphs may sometimes be considered single characters, like the Dutch ij, but most are considered a sequence of two letters. However, visually, digraphs are usually indistinguishable from a normal sequence of the component glyphs.  When a pair of glyphs are actually modified in their forms as to be distinguished from a normal sequence (so that they touch, for example), then we have a _ligature_. This criterion has nothing to do with aesthetics.
