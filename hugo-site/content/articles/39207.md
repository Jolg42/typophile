Typeface design involves a good deal of philosophy. So-called \\\"humanist\\\" philosophy accomodates and encourages anomalies, aberrations, inconsistencies attributable to a human effort eschewing modularity, matching angles and widths, and dogmatic abeyance to the grid. So-called \\\"modernist\\\" philosophy favors the opposite. The changes I've made can easily be construed as reductive modernism aimed at modularization. While I dislike modularity and have moved away from it in my own type designs, modularization is to some extent an inescapable function of the desire for harmony.   ![](http://web.archive.org/web/20150417093902im_/http:/typophile.com/files/evenwidth_4209.gif)  Which phiilosophy, if either, is appropriate for this font?
