It's the printed size of the band occupied by x etc. that determines type's apparent size, so the smaller the x-height, the larger the point size required.  On Windows, most people use [FontLab Studio](http://web.archive.org/web/20150416211928/http:/www.fontlab.com/) (or, for beginners, the same company's TypeTool) to design fonts. On Macs, [Glyphs](http://web.archive.org/web/20150416211928/http:/www.glyphsapp.com/) has become popular. There's also the free and capable but slightly forbidding [FontForge](http://web.archive.org/web/20150416211928/http:/fontforge.github.io/en-US).  By the way, \\\"slab serif\\\" usually implies serifs that are particularly thick, not just rectangular, as in Linden Hill (which is really Fred Goudy's Deepdene, renamed probably for legal reasons).\"}]"
