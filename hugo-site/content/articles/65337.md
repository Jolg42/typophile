Vierling is an old South German weight unit which equals to 8 Loth or, nowadays, 140 grams.  [Wikipedia](http://web.archive.org/web/20131021002101/http:/en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurrent) has an alphabet and a few links.   Like with other unfamiliar letterforms (Textura, for instance), it’s a matter of [practice](http://web.archive.org/web/20131021002101/http:/www.houseind.com/showandtell/2009/03/31/swiss-pre-modernism). The ‘h’ has a descender aswell, and looks almost the same as a ‘long s’ with loops, or an ‘f’.  The letter ‘c’ is a mere downstroke (like a dotless ‘i’), while ‘e’ is a double downstroke. Latter can easily be confused with an ‘n’. On top of that, ‘u’ is identical with ‘n’, were it not for the (curled) [stroke above the ‘u’](http://web.archive.org/web/20131021002101/http:/www.flickr.com/photos/tags/udistinguisher/). Not to confuse with doubled letters (like the ‘n’ in ‘dann’): they were often marked with an overbar.  These similarities consequently lead to a zigzag overkill.
