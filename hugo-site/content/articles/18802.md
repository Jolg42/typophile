So along the journey on the eternal quest of making a font  
look good (but to me at least never “the same”) both in print  
and on screen, I have a question:

What’s the relative effort involved in getting this  
outline “g” (slightly modified from Patria) to render  
out this bitmap “g”, preferably robustly on as many  
platforms and applications as possible.

 ![](http://web.archive.org/web/20090814014720im_/http:/typophile.com/files/render_g.gif)

One way is to make two separate fonts, one outline, one pixelfont\*.  
Another is to superhint, like the previous generation of MS core fonts,  
but with the difference that grayscaling is enabled. A third is to -come  
close, and in some ways exceed- using CT, using some level of hinting  
(which I’m not clear on). For the purposes of this discussion I will add  
that -if it makes a big difference- you can assume the top of the lower  
bowl of that outline “g” to be flat -and even uniform in weight- instead.

\* Or a raw bitmap font, in special situations.

So I’m wondering what are the relative efforts, as well as  
the relative technical impediments in terms of delivery,  
of the various methods. Thank you.
