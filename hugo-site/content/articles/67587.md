It’s not unlike Syntax, but fresher and more versatile. You can judge it on paper: [the printable specimen pdfs](http://web.archive.org/web/20100223015936/http:/www.stonetypefoundry.com/specimenbookspag.html) that are available on the Stone Type Foundry’s website are very helpful.  Note that there is an economic ‘Compact’ family in between the standard and the Condensed.   Furthermore, Sumner Stone has extended the superfamily (which originally started out with [Basalt](http://web.archive.org/web/20100223015936/http:/www.stonetypefoundry.com/basaltoverview.html) – two sets of signage caps incl. arrows) with slightly heavier [Halo grades](http://web.archive.org/web/20100223015936/http:/www.stonetypefoundry.com/halofontspage.html), light [Titling weights](http://web.archive.org/web/20100223015936/http:/www.stonetypefoundry.com/numamagmathinspe.html) plus matching archaic Roman forms ( [Numa](http://web.archive.org/web/20100223015936/http:/www.stonetypefoundry.com/numaoverview.html)), an accompanying uncial ( [Munc](http://web.archive.org/web/20100223015936/http:/www.stonetypefoundry.com/muncoverview.html)), and a softened spin-off, [Tuff](http://web.archive.org/web/20100223015936/http:/www.stonetypefoundry.com/tuffoverview.html).  I haven’t combined Magma with Cycles, but they should work together quite well.
