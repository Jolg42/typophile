My 12-year-old won his middle school science fair with a font [legibility](http://web.archive.org/web/20100728075744/http:/typophile.com/wiki/legibility) project. He evaluated viewers’ responses to eight fonts – four [sans](http://web.archive.org/web/20100728075744/http:/typophile.com/wiki/sans%2520serif) and four [serifs](http://web.archive.org/web/20100728075744/http:/typophile.com/wiki/serif) – at simulated road sign distances. The font size was chosen to be just legible for the average person at ten feet, the test distance.

The results – from most to least legible: [Highway Gothic](http://web.archive.org/web/20100728075744/http:/typophile.com/wiki/Highway%2520Gothic), [P22 Underground](http://web.archive.org/web/20100728075744/http:/typophile.com/wiki/P22%2520Underground), [Verdana](http://web.archive.org/web/20100728075744/http:/typophile.com/wiki/Verdana), then [Arial](http://web.archive.org/web/20100728075744/http:/typophile.com/wiki/Arial), [Times New Roman](http://web.archive.org/web/20100728075744/http:/typophile.com/wiki/Times%2520New%2520Roman), and [Georgia](http://web.archive.org/web/20100728075744/http:/typophile.com/wiki/Georgia) - all roughly equal, then [Palatino](http://web.archive.org/web/20100728075744/http:/typophile.com/wiki/Palatino), and last was [Goudy Oldstyle](http://web.archive.org/web/20100728075744/http:/typophile.com/wiki/Goudy%2520Oldstyle).

[Point sizes](http://web.archive.org/web/20100728075744/http:/typophile.com/wiki/point%2520size) were adjusted so that [cap heights](http://web.archive.org/web/20100728075744/http:/typophile.com/wiki/cap%2520height) of the different faces were equal. Test text (half all caps, half sentence case) was a shortened version of the “Jaded zombies” [pangram](http://web.archive.org/web/20100728075744/http:/typophile.com/wiki/pangram) with some numbers added.

His conclusions – sign makers know what they are doing, sans is best for signage, [x-height](http://web.archive.org/web/20100728075744/http:/typophile.com/wiki/x-height) is important. Hardly groundbreaking, but his methods apparently were sound. Yeah, I helped.
