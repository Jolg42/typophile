It creates unnatural level of gray and unexisting \\\"flows\\\".  My native language is polish, so I use this as an opportunity to test just a few accented characters which are the bridge to (from my point of view) the real \\\"extended character set\\\".  The method is raw. First I work with InDesign file (since I don't trust MS) containing 12 pt lines with all possible combinations for polish, plus germandbls and ccedilla - 11 regional characters (aaaąabacać... ). Such set needs only one sheet of paper and gives an overall view of an \\\"extended\\\" typeface.  After this I test another set, containing eth, dcroat, hbar and accents: umlaut, hungarian umlaut, macron, tilde.  Then I know moreless, how will all the accents behave in context of default character set, and I can go to the next level, described by oldnick (I collect strange looking words).\"}]"

I was wondering how other designers proof extended character sets. Are you running proofs that show every accented letter positioned against every accented letter? Are there techniques for narrowing down what gets proofed that don’t take less time than just testing every combination and ignoring the irrelevant stuff?
