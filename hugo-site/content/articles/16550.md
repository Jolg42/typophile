The serifed [Cyrillic](http://web.archive.org/web/20140701122915/http:/www.typophile.com/wiki/Cyrillic) alphabet uses many Latin forms, several of which are the same in both upper and lower case, but styled differently, with different kinds of serif -- for instance c has a straight serif in upper case, a ball in the lower.

However, Cyrillic-only characters are generally "serifed" the same way in both upper and lower case.

There have been some departures from this orthodoxy, in particular [Vladimir Yefimov](http://web.archive.org/web/20140701122915/http:/www.typophile.com/wiki/Vladimir%20Yefimov)'s " [Kis](http://web.archive.org/web/20140701122915/http:/www.typophile.com/wiki/Kis)" typeface, where the upper case K form has a straight top right arm and serif, but a curved-with-ball form in the lower case.

So I'm wondering, if I were to design a Cyrillic extension in a traditional (Western) genre, and introduce subtle differences between upper and lower case serif treatment, would indigenous readers think:

- damn foreigner doesn't know what he's doing  
- that's cool, just like Yefimov  
- didn't notice, as long as it reads smoothly, not an issue

Also, what has been the reaction to Yefimov's Kis? Is it considered a quaint anomaly, or as a provocative new direction?
