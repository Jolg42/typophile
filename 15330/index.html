Anonymous @ 8 Mar 2006 — 11:21am
_Personally, I believe that LWFN stands for LineWork FoNt, since linework is a common term in the printing industry for vector graphics._ That's an interesting theory. You have to remember, though, that the people who came up with these things were not printing industry people, they were programmers. For nearly a year, the only device commercially available that could output PostScript fonts was Apple's LaserWriter. PostScript fonts were commonly called LaserWriter fonts for a while. When the Linotronic and other devices came out, the usage faded, but even the Linotronic was accessed via the _LaserWriter_ driver for years after, even when the LaserWriter was discontinued. Note also that Apple's icon for a PostScript font was a picture of a LaserWriter printer.
Anonymous @ 8 Mar 2006 — 5:16pm
At the time the "creater" LWFN was adopted, the Apple LaserWriter (PS level 23.0) was the only PostScript printer in existence. LWFN is an Apple name for LaserWriter [outline] FoNt. The screen fonts, which were bitmaps, were in separate font "suitcases". The outline fonts were in Type 1 format, with each face appearing separately. Times Roman had four outline files. Others had eight or more.
