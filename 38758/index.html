Anonymous @ 4 Nov 2007 — 5:00am
For really nice quirky numerals, it's always a good idea to take a look at typefaces that were specifically designed with the numerals in mind, like [Bell Gothic](http://web.archive.org/web/20141124153111/http:/www.fontshop.com/fonts/downloads/linotype/bell_gothic_virtual/?sample_text=0%201%202%203%204%205%206%207%208%209&sample_size=36) and [Bell Centennial](http://web.archive.org/web/20141124153111/http:/www.fontshop.com/fonts/downloads/linotype/bell_centennial_virtual/?sample_text=0%201%202%203%204%205%206%207%208%209&sample_size=36) which were created for telephone books. Also [Garage Gothic](http://web.archive.org/web/20141124153111/http:/www.fontshop.com/fonts/downloads/font_bureau/garage_gothic/?sample_text=0%201%202%203%204%205%206%207%208%209&sample_size=36) is worth a look, as it _"was derived from numbered tickets given at city parking garages"_. Any typeface designed for use in really small sizes is bound to have nifty numerals as well, like [Doric](http://web.archive.org/web/20141124153111/http:/www.fontshop.com/fonts/downloads/linotype/doric_std_ot_virtual/?sample_text=0%201%202%203%204%205%206%207%208%209&sample_size=24) for example.
