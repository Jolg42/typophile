**_[According to Linotype:](http://web.archive.org/web/20150217121307/http:/www.linotype.com/13446/helveticaroman-font.html)_** Many customers ask us what good non-Latin typefaces can be mixed with Helvetica. Fortunately, Helvetica already has Greek and Cyrillic versions, and [Helvetica™ World](http://web.archive.org/) includes a specially-designed Hebrew Helvetica in its OpenType character set. But Linotype also offers a number of CJK fonts that can be matched with Helvetica. **Chinese fonts that pair well with Helvetica:** [DF Hei](http://web.archive.org/web/20150217121307/http:/www.linotype.com/110191/dfhei-family.html) _(Simplified Chinese)_ [DF Hei](http://web.archive.org/web/20150217121307/http:/www.linotype.com/110207/dfhei-family.html) _(Traditional Chinese)_ [DF Li Hei](http://web.archive.org/web/20150217121307/http:/www.linotype.com/110368/dflihei-family.html) _(Traditional Chinese)_ [DFP Hei](http://web.archive.org/web/20150217121307/http:/www.linotype.com/110533/dfphei-family.html) _(Simplified Chinese)_ **Japanese fonts that pair well with Helvetica:** [DF Gothic](http://web.archive.org/web/20150217121307/http:/www.linotype.com/110162/dfgothic-family.html?DSESSID=&PHPSESSID=a04b0657aaf1e74cf0314ee8b3f82f6c) [DF Gothic P](http://web.archive.org/web/20150217121307/http:/www.linotype.com/110152/dfgothicp-family.html?DSESSID=&PHPSESSID=a04b0657aaf1e74cf0314ee8b3f82f6c) [DFHS Gothic](http://web.archive.org/web/20150217121307/http:/www.linotype.com/110223/dfhsgothic-family.html?DSESSID=&PHPSESSID=a04b0657aaf1e74cf0314ee8b3f82f6c) **Korean fonts that pair well with Helvetica:** [DFK Gothic](http://web.archive.org/web/20150217121307/http:/www.linotype.com/110320/dfkgothic-family.html?DSESSID=&PHPSESSID=a04b0657aaf1e74cf0314ee8b3f82f6c)

I realize I'm coming into this very late, but for future reference, buying the fonts directly from Dynacomware is _much_ cheaper than from Linotype, at $25 a pop. They're one of the cheapest type foundries for East Asian stuff (if not _the_ cheapest), and they offer packs of fonts at a further huge discount as well. [http://www.dynalab.com/Products/Wheretobuy/DynaComwareGlobal/tabid/645/l...](http://web.archive.org/web/20150217121307/http:/www.dynalab.com/Products/Wheretobuy/DynaComwareGlobal/tabid/645/language/en-US/Default.aspx "http://www.dynalab.com/Products/Wheretobuy/DynaComwareGlobal/tabid/645/language/en-US/Default.aspx") Note that for Japanese, Morisawa's Medium Gothic BBB was actually packaged with Helvetica for a long time. They have since replaced it with a proprietary face in the OpenType version of the font (Helvetica was seen as having glyphs that were a wee bit too small and heavy), but it's still very close to Helvetica aside from the _a_ (has a cut-off tail like in Arial or the heavier weights of Helvetica, but otherwise it resembles Helvetica's _a_ more than Arial's), the _g_ (double-story), the _r_ (the top is a little shorter), and the _y_ (the tail curves a little differently). Here's a comparison of the two versions: [http://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/06/Gothic\_BBB\_sample.png](http://web.archive.org/web/20150217121307/http:/upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/06/Gothic_BBB_sample.png "http://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/06/Gothic\_BBB\_sample.png")

Adobe Acrobat now includes a sans serif Simplified Chinese font (AdobeHeitiStd-Regular) that can be used for authoring purposes, and we also bundle this font with many of our other applications (when it is localized for China). It's a very good design, and covers GB 18030, meaning that all of the hanzi in the URO and Extension A are included, to the tune of 30K glyphs. We purchased this design from Changzhou SinoType.
