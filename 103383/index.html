Are you sure the newspaper’s name isn’t [Kvällsp **o** sten](http://web.archive.org/web/20140829055309/http:/www.expressen.se/kvp/dagens-kvallsposten/)? The typeface looks like some home-brewn Futura to me – probably custom(ized). If you are looking for a similar retail font with such compact diacritics, see Scangraphic’s Supertypes Series, labeled with SH – e.g. [Futura SH](http://web.archive.org/web/20140829055309/http:/myfonts.us/td-zwprBg). This series started in the 1980, i.e. before OpenType. The compact alternates are still not properly encoded as alternates, but can be accessed via ~ (tilde) for Å and { for Ö. In the early 1990s, URW released its Poster series. In [Futura P Extrabold](http://web.archive.org/web/20140829055309/http:/myfonts.us/td-0X8lia), such integrated accents are the default. Albert-Jan Pool kindly provided me with this information for my recently published [article on unusual umlauts](http://web.archive.org/web/20140829055309/http:/www.flickr.com/groups/fancy-diacritics/discuss/72157633790076349/) for TypoJournal.
