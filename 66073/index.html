Hi Steffen, your sample is [Old English](http://web.archive.org/web/20131014144316/http:/www.fontshop.com/fonts/singles/itc/old_english_std_plain/?&sample_text=Ungl%C3%BCcklicher%20bayerischer%20Kronprinz&sample_size=32) by ITC (or [Letraset](http://web.archive.org/web/20131014144316/http:/new.myfonts.com/fonts/letraset/old-english/?testdrive=dock%3Dfalse%26size%3D28%26w%3D720%26src%3Dcustom%26text%3DUngl%25C3%25BCcklicher%2520bayerischer%2520Kronprinz%2520%26fg%3D000000%26bg%3Dffffff%26goodies%3Dfit%26seed%3D28) respectively – these versions seem to be more or less identical. Old English is based on a [blackletter](http://web.archive.org/web/20131014144316/http:/typophile.com/wiki/blackletter) by [William Caslon](http://web.archive.org/web/20131014144316/http:/typophile.com/wiki/William%20Caslon) (England, ~ 1760). Other foundries had their own versions of this style, like the Manuskript Gotisch that Georg has shown above. At the beginning of the 20th century, [Morris Fuller Benton](http://web.archive.org/web/20131014144316/http:/typophile.com/wiki/Morris%20Fuller%20Benton) designed a version for ATF. More information about the history of Old English typefaces can be found in this [Linotype Font Feature](http://web.archive.org/web/20131014144316/http:/www.linotype.com/2195/oldenglishfonts.html). Today, there is a number of digital ‘Old English’ fonts: [Monotype’s](http://web.archive.org/web/20131014144316/http:/www.fontshop.com/fonts/singles/monotype/monotype_old_english_text_std_plain/) (and [Mecanorma’s](http://web.archive.org/web/20131014144316/http:/new.myfonts.com/fonts/itfmecanorma/old-english/?testdrive=dock%3Dfalse%26size%3D28%26w%3D720%26src%3Dcustom%26text%3DUngl%25C3%25BCcklicher%2520bayerischer%2520Kronprinz%2520%26fg%3D000000%26bg%3Dffffff%26goodies%3Dfit%26seed%3D32)) is pretty wide and loose. The [URW version](http://web.archive.org/web/20131014144316/http:/www.fontshop.com/fonts/singles/urw/old_english/?&sample_text=Ungl%C3%BCcklicher%20bayerischer%20Kronprinz&sample_size=32) is lighter, with the ‘i’ dots shifted a bit to the right. It has the long s (ſ) as default. [Bitstream’s version](http://web.archive.org/web/20131014144316/http:/www.fontshop.com/fonts/singles/bitstream/cloister_black_regular_ot/?&sample_text=Ungl%C3%BCcklicher%20bayerischer%20Kronprinz&sample_size=32) is called ‘Cloister Black’ and can be easily distinguished by its sporns on ‘b’, ‘h’, ‘k’. Furthermore, there are numerous ‘spin-offs’, e.g. [Avebury Black](http://web.archive.org/web/20131014144316/http:/www.typedesign.com/fonts/aveblk.html), an ultra-black and wide display version by [Jim Parkinson](http://web.archive.org/web/20131014144316/http:/typophile.com/wiki/Jim%20Parkinson). Anonymous @ 9 Jan 2010 — 5:44am
For alternative suggestions: well, it depends what you’re aimng at, of course … [Wilhelm-Klingspor-Gotisch](http://web.archive.org/web/20131014144316/http:/www.linotype.com/1608/wilhelmklingsporgotisch-family.html) by [Rudolf Koch](http://web.archive.org/web/20131014144316/http:/typophile.com/wiki/Rudolf%20Koch) is a classic [Textura](http://web.archive.org/web/20131014144316/http:/typophile.com/wiki/Textura) beauty. So is [Goudy Text](http://web.archive.org/web/20131014144316/http:/typophile.com/wiki/Goudy%20Text), although it is more normalised – see its ‘d’, ‘h’, ‘z’, ‘A’ etc. [Fleischmann Gotisch](http://web.archive.org/web/20131014144316/http:/new.myfonts.com/fonts/preusstype/fleischmann-gotisch-pt/) is a festive baroque rendition. [Sinkwitz Gotisch](http://web.archive.org/web/20131014144316/http:/new.myfonts.com/fonts/preusstype/sinkwitz-gotisch/) is rather dynamic and lively – both are revivals by [Ingo Preuss](http://web.archive.org/web/20131014144316/http:/typophile.com/wiki/Ingo%20Preuss). [František Štorm](http://web.archive.org/web/20131014144316/http:/typophile.com/wiki/Franti%C5%A1ek%20%C5%A0torm)’s [Plagwitz](http://web.archive.org/web/20131014144316/http:/www.stormtype.com/typefaces-fonts-shop/families-37-plagwitz) with its strong vertical stress or [Karlgeorg Hoefer](http://web.archive.org/web/20131014144316/http:/typophile.com/wiki/Karlgeorg%20Hoefer)’s economic [Notre Dame](http://web.archive.org/web/20131014144316/http:/www.linotype.com/1279/notredame-family.html) are also charming.
