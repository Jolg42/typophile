You would be well-served to research not only examples of good justification, but also technique, everything from abbreviation to glyph scaling, from Johannes Gutenberg to Hàn Thế Thành. There are some useful links in this thread: [http://typophile.com/node/64375](http://web.archive.org/web/20131014093031/http:/typophile.com/node/64375 "http://typophile.com/node/64375") Your research would also not be complete without noting Eric Gill's dissension as to the wisdom of justified text, which can be found in §6 of _An Essay on Typography._ If you want to see what a modern justification engine is capable of, you can read the documentation for the `microtype` package in LaTeX: [microtype.pdf](http://web.archive.org/web/20131014093031/http:/www.tex.ac.uk/tex-archive/macros/latex/contrib/microtype/microtype.pdf). Even if you read the section headings and nothing else, it will give you the vocabulary to describe what such software is capable of. The following figure illustrates what can be accomplished with tracking alone (for good or ill). ![](http://web.archive.org/web/20131014093031im_/http:/typophile.com/files/microtype_package_4254.png)
