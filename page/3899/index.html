Archive through May 26, 2004

I was expressing a gut feeling in another thread concerning congenial typographic interpretations.

Thomas Phinney wrote Indeed, if the typeface choice doesn’t need to resonate with its content in any way, we wouldn’t need more than a handful of functional typefaces in the world.
Yes indeed.

I’ve found that any typeface of worth, like Berthold’s Garamond for example, would serve a detective story, or a historical text just as well as a science fiction novel or pretty much anything.

The trick is to use that one typeface as sensitive and sensible as possible, not choose between hundreds and hundreds. That’s skill. That’s what I think a good typographer should do.
And that has been my experience.

My comment in the other thread may have sounded severe, but please note that this mainly concerns book typography. I have nothing against more expressive typography per se.

And if you have some good examples where crazy typefaces enhance crazy content in sweet harmony, please allow me to make some exceptions to my rule.

Rated "R" Font

It’s very Melior-like, but it’s not Melior. Renault is also similar. I suspect they were custom designed for the ratings board when they were introduced in the late 1960s. This poster, which shows all of them together, seems to support this. The ratings that were added later–NC-17 and the 13 in PG-13–don’t match the style. In the early years of the system, there was also X, and, for a short time, M and GP.
