St Bride Library Letterpress Exhibit

Submitted by Miss Tiffany : 7.Oct.2009 11.24am

Late letterpress: the work of Desmond Jeffery

Exhibition at St Bride Library,
Tuesday 27 October – Friday 13 November 2009:

  • In the Exhibition Room, St Bride Foundation
  • Open 12.30 to 6.00pm Mondays, Tuesdays and Fridays,
  • 12.30 to 9.00pm on Wednesdays,
    and from 2.30 to 6.00pm on Thursdays
  • Admission free

Evening talk:

  • Tuesday 27 October 2009 at 7pm (exhibition preview from 5.30pm)
  • In the Bridewell Hall, St Bride Foundation
  • Admission £7 · concessions £5 · Friends of St Bride £3
  • Pay on the door

As a direct and elegant means of putting words on paper, letterpress remained vigorous until the end of its useful life about forty years ago. In 1950 the power of this unmediated route from original text to printed sheet caught the imagination of a young returning serviceman, Desmond Jeffery. He saw in the work of Anthony Froshaug what could be done with hand-set letterpress. Unlike Froshaug, for whom it was a matrix upon which to develop a design programme, for Desmond the practice was the programme. He equipped himself with an Adana, an Albion and a collection of foundry types, most of them imported, then in 1956 took over a jobbing letterpress workshop in Marylebone, where he installed a Heidelberg platen. Customers ranged from the Stevens Shanks foundry to Mayfair galleries, the Goldsmiths’ Company to the Partisan coffee house. This is the first public exhibition of his work.

In a talk on 27 October, Paul Stiff, James Mosley and Ian McLaren will contribute personal views of Desmond and his work, to be followed by discussion with other speakers.

Type for redesign of Siddhartha

Hello,

I am a student and working on a redesign for Siddhartha by Herman Hesse. For the assignment, we are putting together the book start to finish, including full illustrations.

From what I’ve seen, most of the design approaches for Siddhartha revolve around a really surface level understanding of the book; i.e. a cover with some sort of Buddha/Eastern philosophy/religion-related image on the front. Since Siddhartha is really a Western interpretation of Eastern philosophy/religion, I really want to get away from this. It’s overdone, I think, and inaccurate, and it doesn’t really do the book justice. It also doesn’t appeal to a contemporary demographic. I’m hoping to convey a new sense of this book’s relevancy to a modern reader. Past designs/treatments have been escapist - about traveling into a psuedo-historical time through a fable. I want the design and illustrations to direct the reader more to the hear and now.

The illustrations I’ve created will accompany each chapter beginning and play upon the main themes of the book: the idea that text-book, logic-based knowledge (in this case, represented by a collage of type) by itself cannot lead to enlightenment, but rather, intuitive, “wisdom” founded on experience leads to an ultimate understanding of “truth” (with experience-based knowledge represented by a collage of bright, images - some pop-culture images, others natural forms, others references to history, others consumerism…). These image collages also represent the second major theme in Siddhartha: that everything in the world is connected and has inherent value and that life, and experience, should not be avoided in an ascetic manner. As Siddhartha progresses in the narrative, he finds himself closer or farther from enlightenment based on his participation in these to modes of understanding; the illustrations -simple silhouettes that are either letter or image based - represent these states.

A link to some of my illustrations can be found here:

http://erinkendig.blogspot.com/2009/06/illustration-word-image.html

I am looking for typeface in which to set body copy of the book. I am thinking a serifed, old-style font might be best, but I’d like to do something other than the ubiquitous Garamond. I’m not too particularly familiar with the various typefaces that are out there.

I would like the type to be somewhat calm feeling, understated - I feel like setting the type with generous (though not insane amounts) of leading might be appropriate. I don’t want it to feel too overly whimsical - but rather, elegant. And traditional, in a way. Nothing too zanny.

The format of the book will be 8 x 8 square.

Any suggestions are much appreciated!

Thank you!

Theatre show logo

Hi all – hoping for a critique on this logo for an upcoming play. I don’t have a lot of experience creating swashes, but I really felt that was the right way to go with this logo, because it’s a whimsical family-oriented show that will play around the holiday season (though there’s no holiday element to the show).

The first attached image is simply the logo above; the second shows the logo in use.

A note: I’m not interested in any critiques on the choice of typeface that I started from (LHF Ross). The show itself has a late-19th/early-20th century vibe, so I’m pretty comfortable with the type choice there. I would, however, like critique on any or all of the following questions:

  1. At first I was going for more circular curves on the large outer loops, but they were really too large, so I went for the curves they are now to kind of pick up the trapezoidal shape of the logo in general. Are the curves too extreme? Also, is it a problem if they’re not mirrored exactly left to right? (Where the curves originate and end don’t match, so it seems impossible for them to be mirrors, but I’m wondering if it’s a problem to be so similar and yet not exact.)

  2. I know there are a couple of problem spots where one of the tails loops through and creates the top second A crossbar. If anyone has any comments about those spots I’d be happy to hear them. Even better, if you have an idea where you think the points should be to create more fluid curves in those spots, I’d love it if you’d take the jpg, circle those spots, and reupload it. I’ve had a devil of a time picking where the points should be in that section.

  3. I know it’s not really an accurate use of a swash, but I love the top R tail’s creating the end of the L. From a typographer’s standpoint, however, is doing that a major crime against swashes? Just curious.

  4. The diamonds in the O’s: too precious?

  5. Anything else?

Thanks,
Cheshire
