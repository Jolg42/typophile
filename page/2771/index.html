20th Century Design Periods

This piece by Matisse is a well known example of Fauvism. It perfectly exemplifies the non-realistic use of color. Although it was not widely accepted in its early years, the use of vivid, non-naturalistic, and exuberant color is what makes this time period unique. This was the first period in which color ruled supreme. I rather like the unnatural use color used to spice things up a bit.

Invented by Pablo Picasso and Georges Braque, Cubism to me is the most radical and most innovative time in art’s history. These guys took spicing things up to a whole other level. I don’t necessarily like all of it, but the concept behind the work is pretty solid. They distorted images, wanting to break away from the conventional. The works are broken and abstracted, meant to demonstrate the mind’s ability to abstract the natural world.

The exaggerated imagery displayed in the Expressionist period really speaks to its audience. This time period used symbolic colors within ordinary, everyday scenes. These works reflect the artist’s state of mind rather than the reality of the external world. Expressionism was meant to give the viewer an emotional.

Design Periods

Art Nouveau: Absinthe Robette by Livemont Privat
I did a project on art nouveau last semester, and so Ive become very familiar with many designers, and font faces from that period. This is a great poster, done by one of the designers regaurded as one of the masters of the poster industry at that time. Art Nouveau posters tend to have very curvilinear shapes (as you can see in this poster) and make use of many lines and motifs found in nature. There is alot of use of flowers and vines, along with the female figure. Fonts used in this time period include Avant Guarde, Benguait, and Arnold Boecklin.

Succession by Von Stuck Franz
The Vienna Succession was also known as the Union of Austrian Artists. The movement was a protest of the younger generation against traditional art. It was a separation from the past towards the future(like most periods, right?). The first chairman was Gustav Klimt. I like the type used in this period. It’s very long and narrow, and much like art nouveau in terms of stylized type. It has influences from the arts and crafts movement.

Marilyn Monroe by Fischer-Nosbisch, Dorothea
Pop art is a design movement that emerged in the late 1960s in the United States. One of the most memorable designers from this period is Andy Warhol. I chose this poster to protray pop art, because I feel like everyone has already seen Andy Warhol’s works. So here is Marilyn Monroe. In German. I love how the type fills the dress. The type is a san serif font and it is a little distorted in order to follow the curves in the dress.

Perpetua

Perpetua and I fell in love last year in Mrs. Bernardo’s class. :) She is an elegant and graceful typeface, meant to flow across the page effortlessly.

Created by Eric Gill and released in 1932, Perpetua has a long story to tell. Along the way Gill didn’t know for sure if this would be the typeface he would release. He had another offer from another company for her sister font, Felicity. The time, scheduling, and mismanagement worked out in Perpetua’s favor.

Gill named her after Saint Perpetua who was one of the first martyrs to die for Christianity. It took nearly 8 years of meticulous work to to be able to produce such a beautiful typeface to suit such an honorable woman. In the end he produced a masterpiece that is still unique and timeless today.

Knowing Saint Perpetua’s story as well as the sketchy business related background that went into this typeface, I had to share its story as well as choose it as my favorite.
