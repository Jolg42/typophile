I am currently working on a stationery system redesign, and obsessing on a typographic detail.
In setting type on a main single column, it makes sense to divide it in logical blocks with vertical space, like this:

address block
(more than one line)

phone
fax number

e-mail address
website URL

I am wondering if the best solution is to separate the paragraphs with hard returns but keeping the leading constant, or opt for vertical space lines with reduced leading. For example: let’s say my text blocks are set at 8/10 pt, but my space lines have only a leading of 6pt.
Is the asymmetrical leading a faux pas in this case? I wouldn’t think so, since it’s not running text, and the column certainly looks more compact than with the other solution. Is there a rationale in deciding how much to reduce leading, like dividing it by half maybe?

A new typeface family for the Baltimore Sun

The Mencken family was created by Jean François Porchez for The Baltimore Sun redesign, launched on 19th September 2005. The Mencken family includes various members: Text, Subhead, Head, Head Narrow, Head Compressed, Nameplates, each of them specially designed for their own function. Focus groups conducted during the redesign period liked the Mencken family; 75 percent preferred Mencken over the previous Sun typefaces. The family’s name Mencken is a tribute to H.L. Mencken’s journalistic contributions to The Sun.

"True" Old-Style Numerals

In the design of the (Latin) UC there are two general schemes: the “classical” (old-style) one where the widths diverge a lot (like “B” is narrow while “C” is wide); and the “modern” style where the widths converge a lot. When it comes to numerals, it just hit me that even in the old-style scheme there’s a lot of convergence in width (except for the one). Now, it can be said that numbers are inherently “rationalist” so they should converge. But there’s always the option of making monowidth numerals, and when it comes the old-style (AKA “text”) numerals it seems to makes sense that their geometric divergence shouldn’t be confined to the vertical.

This idea came to me upon returning to admire Perrin’s Augustaux:
http://www.themicrofoundry.com/other/Elzevir/PerrinNums.gif _
(See this old thread: http://typophile.com/node/7596)

The main question -assuming you agree that width divergence has a place- I think is this: what might be a good scheme here? I mean, which numerals should be towards the narrow end and which the wide? And does this relate to the given numeral being x-height, ascending or descending? Also, can we greatly diverge the widths even in the design of lining numerals?

It’s not impossible that we might end up with a new numeral scheme that works better in running text… at which point we might start calling what we currently call “old-style”… “transitional”? :-)
