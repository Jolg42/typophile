Two More Releases From Filmotype: Alice and Zanzibar

Submitted by Miss Tiffany : 14.Mar.2008 5.24pm

Greetings Friends!

We’re pleased to announce the re-release of two breathtaking scripts from the Filmotype Font Library.

Filmotype Alice marks the beginnings of the casual handwritten script aesthetic. Introduced by Filmotype in the late 1950s, it perfectly captures the mid-century playfulness of hand lettering while providing comfortable readability.

You can look for Alice’s cousin Brooklyn to be released in the coming months. Brooklyn was a derivative weight of the Alice face that is slightly bolder and wider than the original.

Strikingly poised and almost as exotic as it sounds, Filmotype Zanzibar is among Filmotype’s most amazing and beautiful formal scripts released. This hidden gem from the early 1950s has been meticulously redrawn from the original font filmstrips and has been expanded to include a full international character compliment, automatic fractionals, ordinals, and a host of alternate characters in dynamic OpenType format.

We’re pleased to share the re-release of these amazing original designs from the 1950s and hope you’ll find them to be a perfect compliment to your design toolbox.

You can purchase, preview and discover more about these faces online exclusivly at Font Bros!

See ’em here: http://www.fontbros.com/filmotype.php

Warmly,
Stuart

Type families

Do you agree that a good designer really only needs five or six families of types to explore - for me it is Eric Gill’s Sans in its mnay forms - plus his father-in-law Edward Johnston’s charismatic and ground-breaking Sans for the London Underground (still very much in use and full of beans and new applications today) not to mention Joanna who, as well as being a fine fnt somewhat akin to his Perpetua, was also incidentally Johnston’s daughter, hence the font name.

Top that list of three font families off with Bembo or Goudy etc or whatever you like working with and you have enough type variations and weights etc to keep you busy for a year or three ringing the changes and possibilities.

By the way, this is my first post - just stumbled on this lovely site while idly Googling for a beautiful alphabet series of lithos by The Beggarstaff Brothers (which I did not find, any help anyone can give will be very gratefully received). I have owned an original for the letter “Y is for Yokel” for fifteen years and want to find out the rest.

But back to my question: Do you find that, except for specific briefs of course, you come back to certain type families - rather like a musician having a favourite instrument which you get better at playing the more varied ways you use it?
