Small Type recommendations for a topographic map re-design

First time posting on this list but I have been lurking for months. I am also mostly a typography novice, hence my questions.

I am a cartographer working on a re-design of a large scale topographic map (1:50k). This is a large sheet 28" x 40" with a 4 color CMYK tiff with type and line work printing over that in black and two spot colors: a blue and a greyish brown. Large quantities of single line small type blocks in the 5 or 6pt range and usually 4 to 6 letters long need to be placed. But some may be longer and two to three lines long. Some text may also be 8pt to 10pt in size. In the past this was set in Arial. I am looking to change this. I want to use a sans serif family for most of it, with a well matched serif or semi-sans for some place names and water bodies. I realise this may be a bit off-topic for this list but I really enjoy the tone of the discussion here and though I would solicit your informed opinions.

Legibility is very important, and there is a good chance text will need to be converted to paths at some point in the printing process.

BTW I am loathe to use Bell Centennial.

I’ve been recommended Frutiger and Amasis. Anyone else with some suggestions?

Stability and dazzle in letters

Here is an illustration from Letterletter by Gerrit Noordzij.

I may not have the proportions of the white squares to the black exact.

The idea is that when you look at this your brain does a ‘gestalt switch’ between seeing white squares against black one moment, and a framed cross the next.

Noordzij says “Real characters have built-in stabilizers that keep black shapes in the forground”. In other words, they avoid the dazzle in this illustration.

I am interested in what you experienced type designers think of the problem of avoiding dazzle, particularly in text faces.

First of all, do you think Noordzij has it right? Is getting type to rest on the page a matter of balancing white and black? And what are your thoughts on doing it?

What would make me question whether the black/white balance is all of the story is the to me undesirable dazzle in many of the ‘didone’ class of type in small sizes. It seems like part of the story is just the thinness of the lines.

I was recently struck by how well Kent Lew’s new face, Whitman, ‘rests’ on the page, at least judged by the samples on the internet. I am interested in how he and others have approached this problem.

Switching between PostScript and OpenType fonts

Since I started using the OpenType version of the
Neutraface family in InDesign (this is what digital
typography always was meant to be, although the
OpenType features of Neutraface are VERY basic
unfortunately) I constantly need to switch back to
the PostScript version when I want to work in
other software.

For example for a congress book I’m currently
designing, the cover is done in Adobe Illustrator 10
(no support for OpenType), but the book itself is
in Adobe InDesign 2 (OpenType supported). I
can activate only one version at the time in ATM,
because it sees them as conflicting duplicates
(quite logical IMO). So when I want to switch
software, I have to deactivate and remove one
version of the fonts and then import the other
version. This is a bit tedious.

Does anyone know a more elegant way for doing
this? (And please don’t give me any “try designing
your cover in InDesign too” nonse, smartypants!)
