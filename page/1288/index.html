Victorian era Letterforms

Good evening.

I am a first semester Design student from Mexico looking for visual reference for late 19th century types and letterform designs.

I was fortunate enough to find some good listings (and samples of the most popular ones like Kismet and Nymphic) by John F. Cumming, Hermann Ihlenburg and from the Mackellar Smiths & Jordan foundry. On the latter I was particularly pleased to find this web site ( http://cgm.cs.mcgill.ca/~luc/mackellar/ ) which list a good number of types featured in the “The Compact Book of Specimens”.

Now my problem is that I have yet to find any sort of on-line visual reference for a lot of these, as some don’t seem to have been digitalized yet (to my knowledge). I found quite a few ’variations’ though but not much examples of the originals.

Seeing as this is a good place to ask for assistance I humbly request any sort of URL with data or images of letterforms and typefaces from that period, as I will be trying to list them for part of a presentation by image example, name, year of patent and designer.

Any help would be appreciated.

Thank you very much.

Village announce two newest members: Sibylle Hagmann and Hugo d'Alte.

Hi!
it is with great pride and pleasure that we announce two new members of our family: Sibylle Hagmann of Kontour, and Hugo d’Alte, whose work is now being published by Thirstype.

/
Sibylle Hagmann is the tenth member of the Village co-op; she is a graphic and type designer and design educator born in Switzerland, educated in California, and living in Houston Texas with her husband and children. Sibylle is best known to the world for her Cholla typeface; she was also asked to participate in the University of Minnesota’s “Twin Cities” type competition.

Odile will be the first type family released under the Kontour label, and it is an amazing and beautiful piece of work; a contemporary serif design with intriguing interior lines and angles. You can see a preview of the type at the Kontour page of vllg.com: http://vllg.com/Kontour/Odile/Example+About/

/
Hugo d’Alte has joined Thirstype’s ranks with his fantastic Kaas typeface, a geometric blackletter covering extended Latin, Cyrillic, and Hebrew. The face blends post-modern irony with genuine historical dilligence. The huge OpenType glyph table contains titling capitals, “latin” and geometric numerals, and a pan-European collection of accents. http://vllg.com/Thirstype/Kaas/mudTyper+Weights/

Hugo is even more peripatetic than Sibylle: Born in Portugal, educated at KABK in the Netherlands, and living with his wife and baby boy Gustavo in Helsinki Finland. (This makes Hugo the travel champion of Village.)

/
Welcome to Sibylle and Hugo.

Village Type: Three New Releases

/ / / / / / / / / / / / /

Hi!
Happy autumn from New York City. It’s now eight weeks since the launch of Village, and we’re happy to present three new releases, all debuts of one kind or another.

/
First up is “Local Gothic” from Orange Italic. This is Christian Schwartz’s first ever release on his own label. (You probably know Christian for his many typefaces published with the world’s best large foundries: “Amplitude”, “Los Feliz”, “Neutraface”, and “Unit” to name a few.) “Local Gothic” was used extensively in materials for TypeCon 2005, which took place in the city in July. The face includes some brilliant OpenType randomization programming by Tal Leming of Type Supply. We’re very excited to present “Local Gothic”.
http://vllg.com/OrangeItalic/LocalGothic/mudTyper+Weights/

//
Our second debut is Thirstype’s “Kaas” typeface, the first face published by Hugo d’Alte. Hugo was born in Portugal, educated in the Netherlands, and lives in Finland, making him Village’s hands-down pan-European champion. “Kaas” is a modern geometric/constructed blackletter with a historically-accurate set of titling capitals, a huge collection of accents, and Cyrillic and Hebrew alphabets.
http://vllg.com/Thirstype/Kaas/mudTyper+Weights/

///
Number three on our list is Joshua Darden’s debut face on his own label: “Omnes”. (You might be aware of Joshua’s super-family “Freight” or his Headline version of “Meta”.) “Omnes” is an exquisite contemporary mostly-rounded sans serif whose details become more apparent as the weight increases. The face is unique and versatile, friendly and clean. We’re extremely proud to bring you “Omnes”.
http://vllg.com/JoshuaDarden/Omnes/mudTyper+Weights/

All three typefaces are reasonably priced: You can pick up all three new releases for less than $200.

Thanks for reading.
