You’re welcome. When compiling this list, I didn’t pay regard to your original request, sorry. That is, I won’t recommend most of those typefaces for setting a children’s book (including URW Grotesk, for the reason Jens mentioned). In my opinion, the significance of a single-storey ‘a’ for children is, at least, debatable. Such a typeface might come in handy for teachers, when it is both used for reading and (as a model for) writing. Apart from that, the benefit of avoiding confusion of ‘_a_’ and ‘a’ is soon outweighed by the potential –&nbsp;and likely – confusion of ‘_a_’ and ‘_o_’. Children are not stupid, they can handle more than one representational form per character. Their world doesn’t end at the edges of their primer. They do spot and effortlessly read a lot of different ‘a’ glyphs every day: caps, italics, serifs, sans-serifs, scripts, fat letters, condensed letters, single-storey and double-storey … <cite>Sassoon Primary looks a bit too young for our purposes</cite> Note that the [Sassoon family](http://web.archive.org/web/20150331013554/http:/www.clubtype.co.uk/fonts/sas/sasslist_com.html) is an elaborate system that consists of a number of members, each designed for a special purpose.
