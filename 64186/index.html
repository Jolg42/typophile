"Jenson" will always be significant, because it is the definitive roman type. A great many type designers do their version (mine is Goodchild/Nicholas), presumably because engaging the _fons et origo_ provides insight into why the typographic letter is shaped the way it is. Do art directors and graphic designers feel similarly inclined? What has prompted you to use it (or similar Venetian), Tiffany?
Anonymous @
Not only is Jenson the root of our Latin type, it’s the typeface used in the roots of great printed book design. Jenson’s books were exquisite; his paper, proportion, and typesetting are simply fabulous. So as long as there are books, there will be book designers who have sought out Jenson’s work and let it influence theirs.
