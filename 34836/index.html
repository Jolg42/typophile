The most established such tool is FontForge. It started out as something of a Fontographer clone for Type1 fonts only, but has grown considerable sophistication for TT and OpenType as well. My own fonts, including Inconsolata, are developed using my [Spiro](http://web.archive.org/web/20091107124024/http:/levien.com/spiro/) tools. The underlying math is quite sophisticated, but the polish and packaging to make it a smooth, easy-to-use app is absolutely not there yet. I will continue to chip at the code in support of those goals, but I expect progress to be very slow unless considerable new energy comes into the project from other collaborators. See also Dave Crossland’s talk at [LGM](http://web.archive.org/web/20091107124024/http:/www.libregraphicsmeeting.org/2007/program.html) (audio and video links of the presentation inside that link). There is a bit of information about less mainstream tools on the [OFLib Wiki](http://web.archive.org/web/20091107124024/http:/openfontlibrary.org/wiki/index.php/Tools) as well. Best of luck!

FontForge [http://fontforge.sourceforge.net/](http://web.archive.org/web/20091107124024/http:/fontforge.sourceforge.net/) (that what Raph mentioned) is a tool for developing fonts, free to use and modify under “essentially the revised BSD license”. It is a UNIX type of program, but has builds that work under X11 on MacOSX and Cygwin on Windows.
