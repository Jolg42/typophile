\> _I would like to learn how to emphasize its expressive power via the use of an extraodinarily well-chosen typeface._ Hello Anton, As I understand it, your intent is to express the (non-)word “EXPANTIUM” without the use of any graphical elements and without any modification or modulation of the selected typeface. Why are you limiting yourself? I can understand if you have given yourself a personal challenge to find what seems like the “right typeface,” but I fear that even AFTER you have made the decision, you may be haunted with the notion that you are “not sure if that’s absolutely the right one.” What do I mean? There will always be a typeface that you have missed (digitized or not), and even if you have seen them all there are NEW ones created all the time. It may even be that a contrast of typeface weights or a combination of more than one type family may provide the best solution. Selecting typefaces to be used in graphic design, and creating a unique logotype are completely different challenges. You may not be aware of this, but even logos that appear to be using only a typeface, may be in fact MODIFIED, even slightly. Even after a logo has been created, it may be necessary to modify or update the look when appropriate. Sample: Resurection of the [**ATARI brand**](http://web.archive.org/web/20060522215945/http:/www.brandequity.com/frame_communications1.htm) (look just at the letterforms). Ask yourself if your personal challenge of using only ONE UNMODIFIED typeface is more important than finding an exceptional expression of “EXPANTIUM.” Even the sound of the word begs a dynamic and not static use of letterforms. Please understand that I am not diminishing your intent. I just want you and other readers to understand that creating a unique identity is NOT solely the task of finding the “right typeface.”

I like the aesthetic as it stands, particularly if classic, high-end, and somewhat conservative is your goal.

The Netherlands is one of the best countries in the world for both quality and innovation in type design, so you’re in the right place to find great type and type designers. The aesthetic you’re seeking may not be part of the culture there, as you noted, but you could easily find a very skilled foundry to create some custom type for a logotype or even an entire custom typeface for you. This is, for many, the ultimate signifier of a completly distinct brand identity. Here are a few lists: http://www.dutchtype.org/foundries.html [http://www.myfonts.com/country/nl/](http://web.archive.org/web/20060522215945/http:/www.myfonts.com/country/nl/) Your concept of “gold-tech” is very interesting. You seem to have a clear image of what you want and the ability to articulate it, which could feed a strong creative brief for a type project. Though your appreciation of the new UPS logo is likely to draw some criticism from designers, who tend to disdain it. : ) If you go with the original you posted, Norbert’s suggestion about letterspacing is, as usual, a good one. The current spacing is slightly uneven across the word. Fortunately there are no major sins, but it should be immaculate, particularly for something like this. Also, size it down to about 25% (not an uncommon size for on-screen use) and determine whether it’s still working for you. The thinner strokes begin to close up, which might call for a custom on-screen drawing for small sizes or a typeface with a heavier light stroke.
