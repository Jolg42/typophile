Anonymous @ 28 February, 2007 - 7:45pm
I think that if you’re going for “clean,” this doesn’t quite work as a logo. The tightness of the letterspacing causes a number of extremely dark, frankly ugly spots; for example, the m and i and the a and t. Also, using what appears to be Helvetica isn’t often a spectacular way to distinguish yourself in a logo, nor is it especially considered modern anymore (although it can certainly be if used in the right way). I’d try working on your logo concept some more; maybe just do some free associative sketches for a while. What would you like to convey to someone with your logo? How do you see yourself as a photographer? I’d experiment with both typographic and largely graphic logos; either can work as long as it’s inventive and memorable.
