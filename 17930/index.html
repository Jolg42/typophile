Rather than start on the computer you should really get out a pencil and paper. Think about the many different variations of style that the letterforms can take. Look at other lettering artists and ambigram artists to see how they do things. In the end you may have to pay someone to do this if it’s really what you want. What you have right now is rather ’mish-mashy’, keep trying...don’t be content until it’s perfect.

I think your basic approach is sound and some of the letter combinations are working well, as you say patience and perserverance are going to be required, you could look at [this thread](http://web.archive.org/web/20080920053351/http:/typophile.com/node/1516?from=0&comments_per_page=50) for some ideas. I think you’re onto something with your ’k’ becoming an ’is’. Just needs a little straightening out to make it work with the other characters. I think you could eliminate the stroke leading into the a in Francois and bend the bowl of the a back to the right a bit to make the a more obviously an a without compromising the n. I believe the o in Francois could also get rounder as long as you keep that incoming stroke that makes it an a. Finally, the F might read better both as F and E if the top horizontal was not detached from the stem. It’s very cool to see sketches in progress - save these when you’ve arrived at a result, they’re great. And don’t give up hope, you’re almost there.
