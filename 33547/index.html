Anonymous @ 5.May.2007 7.52pm
The small type in the 3qs example is [Cheltenham Old Style](http://web.archive.org/web/20090904015208/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/bitstream/cheltenham/) and Cheltenham Wide (don’t know of a digital version). The large type is a typical wood type grotesque. The word “CANADIAN” is [Copperplate Gothic](http://web.archive.org/web/20090904015208/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/linotype/copperplate-gothic/). Some of the small type near the top in the 3DU sample is DeVinne. The only digital version I could find is [this one](http://web.archive.org/web/20090904015208/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/woodentypefonts/de-vinne/), but it’s apparently based on a large wood type cut, not the small metal cut in your sample. I’m pretty sure the rest of the small type is an old metal font called Grasset. “A.R.R.L.” is [Copperplate Gothic](http://web.archive.org/web/20090904015208/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/linotype/copperplate-gothic/). Regarding the last one, I’m also the one who identified it for you on the [T-D] list. This is about as close as you’re going to get to the original metal Parsons. There doesn’t appear to be any ITC-style design modernization.
Anonymous @ 5.May.2007 8.06pm
Regarding the metal typeface Grasset (E Grasset, 1898), the two digital versions I’m aware of are [Paul Lloyd’s](http://web.archive.org/web/20090904015208/http:/www.moorstation.org/typoasis/designers/lloyd/index.htm) [Grantham](http://web.archive.org/web/20090904015208/http:/www.moorstation.org/typoasis/designers/lloyd/grantham.htm) and David Nalle’s [Octavian](http://web.archive.org/web/20090904015208/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/scriptorium/octavian/).
