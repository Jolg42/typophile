The main objections to derivative fonts is the dilution of ownership rights that such fonts may contribute to. In general I think most type designers and foundries would find it acceptable to modify a character or set of characters in Illustrator, that's how most logos get made after all. The practice is so common I doubt many EULAs even talk about it. But having said that some EULAs are tricky and the best advice would be to check.

Anonymous @ 14 Jul 2006 — 9:40pm
Modifying a letterform (or even several letterforms) that has been converted to outlines is **not** modifying the font. If anyone has a EULA that prohibits this very common practice, he/she/they ought to give some serious consideration to changing that EULA to conform to real-world practice. After following several threads in this forum, I changed my EULA to allow modification of my fonts for personal/corporate use: as long as the end product is not resold or redistributed, the end user ought to have the option of making changes that make the product work best for them.
