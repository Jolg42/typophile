Anonymous @ 3.Jan.2006 4.49pm
A quick glance through Rookledge shows that Venetian (sloping e-bar), Angled Stress/Oblique Serifs, Vertical Stress/Oblique Serifs and (confusingly) Vertical Stress/Straight Serifs _generally_ have angled serifs, while Abrupt Contrast/Straight Serifs (Didone etc) and Slab Serif have straight serifs. Of course there are exceptions and there are some that have different angles, different sizes/types (full and half) of serif and some have a chirographic lefthand serif. My general preference is angled followed by less angled (or flat) serif. I suppose it really depends on the type of serif and stress. Tim
Anonymous @ 4.Jan.2006 5.00am
The trick with the u is that the serif on the righthand stem is the only letter-internal x-height serif off a straight stem. If your design has strong triangular serifs at the x-height, you’ll find that, if the basic serif model established by the lowercase i is applied to the righthand stem of the u it will dominate the interior space of the letter too much. I believe this is why the u often has more horizontal serifs than other letters: you adjust the righthand serif so that it leaves the counter nice and open, and then you have to adjust the lefthand serif to harmonise, otherwise the letter looks really weird.
