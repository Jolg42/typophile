Typesetting TMs with periods

Gang,

Way back as a junior lackey, I was taught to typeset trademark symbols (hereafter referred to as TM) at the end of a sentence thusly:

“…so make your voice heard: call Acme.TM” (then kern the TM close to the period, rather than:

“…so make your voice heard: call AcmeTM.”

Was I duped? Comments? Is there a reference guide that may discuss this vexing issue?

Thanks in advance.

CP

Anonymous @ 5 Jun 2003 — 4:29pm
I think it is incorrect to separate the  from the Acme with a period intervening. No matter what. The implication in your approach (both 1 and 3 in your example) is that the entire sentence (or at least the clause "call Acme") is the trademark. I would set the sentence order as Acme., then kern the period under the , but not so far as your #3 example. This way, also, your text is not "broken" when repurposed into an environment without kerning. That's my opinion as a designer, typographer, and editor. _\>Crazy dedicated typographers do the same with quotes over periods._ Crazy dedicated typographers who use well crafted fonts shouldn't have to, as the quote-period kern pair should be present in a good font. I also include kerns for period-quote in my fonts. I have not, however, heretofore built a kern pair for trademark-period. But perhaps I will include it from here on out. (And, I suppose, period-trademark as well, just in case.) -- K.
Anonymous @ 5 Jun 2003 — 5:06pm
I am much less concerned with implying that a period or an entire sentence is part of the trademark than I am with that lonely period floating off by itself! In general, I would pull the TM much closer to the word Acme than you have in any of the examples shown, CP. Then I would tuck the period in so that it would match any other e. combination. I'm more concerned with the flow of the sentence as it is read. The TM is going to be ignored by the reader, so I try to make it as inconspicuous as possible while still meeting the legal requirements of the corporation. JMHO
