Anonymous @ 14.Feb.2007 9.26am
Hrant, that last one is indeed easily readable. (It says, literally, “salt doll”. I was intrigued by this title, which I haven’t seen before, and a quick bit of googling tells me it’s the name of a TV drama series.) Informal lettering written with a brush is all the rage these days in South Korea, appearing on everything from food packaging, signs on restaurants, and TV titles. It’s fascinating for me to imagine how Korean lettering and fonts must look to those who don’t read Korean. Being a native reader, it’s not something I have had a chance to think about very often. I wouldn’t have stopped to think that yes, it makes sense that someone who doesn’t read Korean would wonder whether an example like the one above would be easily readable. I can’t be completely objective in judging how my own familiarity affects how I read different scripts, but I think it’s a credit to Hangul (and good news for Korean lettering artists) that the glyphs can be so playful and informal without hurting readability. By the way, ugh on that first example with all the pink and all the hearts. Though I just possibly may be bitter about having to do an all-night shift on St. Valentine’s Day.
