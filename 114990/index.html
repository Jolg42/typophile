Might help if you gave some hint as to the nature of the image, logo, menu, old book etc and an approximate date. At first glance this looks like a "Latin" type such as Etienne [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/paratype/etienne/](http://web.archive.org/web/20141011012350/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/paratype/etienne/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/paratype/etienne/") Latin CT [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/castletype/latin-ct/](http://web.archive.org/web/20141011012350/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/castletype/latin-ct/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/castletype/latin-ct/") ITC Barcelona [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/itc/barcelona/](http://web.archive.org/web/20141011012350/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/itc/barcelona/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/itc/barcelona/") or Spike [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/quadrat/spike/](http://web.archive.org/web/20141011012350/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/quadrat/spike/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/quadrat/spike/") with a shadow line and coat-hook ornaments pasted amidships. This is not to say that someone has not packaged this mish mash as a font. Could have been done by a Victorian typemaker with little imagination or a similarly changed modern designer. Compare it with the somewhat similar, but much better designed Algerian [http://www.myfonts.com/search/algerian/fonts/](http://web.archive.org/web/20141011012350/http:/www.myfonts.com/search/algerian/fonts/ "http://www.myfonts.com/search/algerian/fonts/") Don
