If you’re setting tables of financial data and blocks of text, a face with lining and oldstyle numerals is probably a good bet. That’s more common in serif faces, but is becoming more common in contemporary sans. Without knowing more, that’s as specific as I can get.

I would think [Giacomo](http://web.archive.org/web/20050825013538/http:/terminaldesign.com/retail_specimen.php?ID=1176&viewFam=1), a sweet sans by Terminal Design, might be ideal. It has tabular lining figures as well as proportional oldstyle figures, and looks great in a magazine. Edit: I just missed your second post. In that light, here are two more that might fit the bill: [Apex Sans](http://web.archive.org/web/20050825013538/http:/thirstype.com/fonts.php?font=ApexSans) from Thirstype, and [Section](http://web.archive.org/web/20050825013538/http:/lux.thirstype.com/fonts.php?font=Section) from Lux Typographics. To me, each conveys strength but with a modern flair.
