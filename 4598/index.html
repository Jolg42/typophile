A Nick's Font adaptation is [Saturday Morning Toast](http://web.archive.org/web/20111216182725/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/nicksfonts/saturday-morning-toast), though it's not italicized and it doesn't contain swashes. By the by, according to Nick, the original face was called Curtis Post Shaded.

I think Scriptorium has a version of Blanchard they call [Ruffian](http://web.archive.org/web/20111216182725/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/scriptorium/ruffian/). It's not italic either. The problem is that often if people do digitize a classic typeface, they are cautious about using the name, and so you need to know it when you see it. I have tried to 'decode' the Scriptorium collection because David Nalle does a great job of recapturing some of the old designs, but he almost always changes the names. So I guess, unless someone knows of some more interpretations, the answer seems to be that they are mostly in books right now.
