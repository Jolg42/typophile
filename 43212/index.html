I spent around ten minutes in Photoshop transforming [this stock photo](http://web.archive.org/web/20080422060618/http:/www.photoshopstar.com/tutorials/35/stock.jpg) (found via Google Image Search) into [this](http://web.archive.org/web/20080422060618/http:/typophile.com/files/stock_grainy_4557.jpg). Is that sort of what you had in mind? Here’s a quick summary of how I did it: I converted the image to CYMK, then copied each CMYK channel into a separate new grayscale document. For each of the four new documents, I added two 50%-gray layers and set them both to Overlay mode. Then I used Add Noise on each of the 50%-gray layers followed by Gaussian Blur. I used a different amount of Gaussian Blur on each of the two layers: a 1.5-pixel radius for the first one and 0.75 pixel for the second. The idea was to get two levels of grain: one coarse, and one finer. Then I flattened all four of the CYMK gray documents and copied each one back into the appropriate channel in the original document. I converted back the image to RGB, then added a Hue/Saturation layer to bring down the saturation. Finally, I added a Curves layer to boost the center of the red channel and depress the center of the blue channel.
