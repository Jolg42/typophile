Vera is a renamed open source version of Bitstream Prima. Doesn’t look like there was a medium... [http://new.myfonts.com/fonts/bitstream/prima-sans/](http://web.archive.org/web/20100103023537/http:/new.myfonts.com/fonts/bitstream/prima-sans/ "http://new.myfonts.com/fonts/bitstream/prima-sans/")
Anonymous @ 29.Dec.2009 6.48am
“closely based on Bitstream’s Prima”: [http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitstream\_Vera](http://web.archive.org/web/20100103023537/http:/en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitstream_Vera "http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitstream\_Vera") AFAIK you are allowed, according to the Vera font license, to make your own medium weight, as long as you don’t call it Vera $SOMETHING. The Fontographer “Blend Fonts” feature comes in handy for that (use normal and bold weight). Check your Vera license to see exactly what you are allowed to (IANAL). Andreas

Better yet, you could do it with Deja Vu Sans (another Vera derivative [http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DejaVu\_fonts](http://web.archive.org/web/20100103023537/http:/en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DejaVu_fonts "http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DejaVu\_fonts")) and share it back with the world! :-) That said, you can try to obtain a “medium” look just adding a thin stroke to the regular weight using the desktop publishing app of your choice.
