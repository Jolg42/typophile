Anonymous @ 20.Oct.2008 3.23pm
I understand your thinking behind this. There are two problems for me. Firstly I think the red and white and the cross symbol represent first aid rather than a wide range of medicine and health. Also the “computer, arcade type font” is from a much earlier age of computers than blogging, and its fuzzy edges don’t help. Look for something bolder, clearer and more up-to-date. I would get rid of the cross but keep the binocular form. Try a warm, vibrant green or orange rather than red to look healthy and lively, and make it smaller if you are going to keep it to the side of the lettering.
Anonymous @ 20.Oct.2008 3.44pm
In my opinion, you’re approaching this design way too literally. The concept for the blog isn’t visually congruent and to try and create a visual image to go along with the blog is honestly, out of your skill range. Not to offend. I think you’d have better luck creating a clean logotype and matching it with health related images (stock photography perhaps) in the banner of the site. Also, to reiterate what Jay said earlier, avoid the “computer/arcade” type. It’s a poor concept to assume that if you’re creating a logotype for a website it has to look “techy or futuristic”. Personally, I’d use a friendly slab serif typeface like Soho, Archer, Zine Serif or Officina Serif. Any of these fonts would bring a confident look without seeming too cold or tiresome.

Anonymous @ 21.Oct.2008 7.50pm
A ballpark would START at around AU$2000 - that doesn’t take into account relative factors for legitimate NFPs or NGOs. And continuing about relativity... - i.e, how much value is placed on the project, it’s an image and identity - a face - for the organisation. Then you need to factor in things like; research (there always should be if the project is of any value to the business - the designer needs to get up to speed with the company, service/s, structure, audience & visual language, and competition etc. to understand it.) then ... project timing; specific requirements; approval processes; project management etc. You’re paying for the creative’s/designer’s time as a creative/visual thinker and designer - if you think that time is worth $100 you’ll get a $100 result - because you will get A result. No doubt about it. Having said that, if you value a designers time at $100 ... you won’t have any choice but to be happy with whatever the results are. Clipart with a font might work ... who knows. I’ll refer you to the triangle ... you can have any two sides included in your project. Which two are most important to you? ![](http://web.archive.org/web/20081023045829im_/http:/typophile.com/files/fast-good-cheap_5922.gif) You’re paying for the creative’s/designer’s TIME of finding an effective and appropriate solution to your requirements and their EXPERIENCE in finding that effective and appropriate solution. It’d be great if it were a fixed price industry ... but it’s not. There are too many variables involved. At the end of the day, one thing never changes. You get what you pay for. ————— **Paul Ducco** [Logo Design Melbourne](http://web.archive.org/web/20081023045829/http:/www.solid-creative.com/identity.htm)
