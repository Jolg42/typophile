According to Bringhurst, yes. Solidus is a more angled slash used for fractions (the term derived from a roman coin).

True solidus from Roman. England has it because the Romans came here and ’/’ is a shilling. // two slashes with a bar through it is a libra, a measure of weight or curreny (looks similar to # that the americans call ’pound sign’). Hence the £ is the current form of the ’pound sterling’. And in typography the stacked fractions were called ’nut’ fractions since they were often on the ’nut’ or ’en space’ and the lining ones often called ’shilling fractions’.
