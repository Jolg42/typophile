Slab Serif Defined

Could someone please point me in the direction of some literature (or a thread) where someone tries to nail down the criteria for a face being a slab-serif?

A true formula would be extra nice. Something like “the thickness of the serif on a lower-case h, from the baseline to the point at which the slop of the bracket is equal to zero, must constitute at least X percentage of the stroke width of the h’s stem.”

This was a great thread: [http://www.typophile.com/node/30127](http://web.archive.org/web/20150115070213/http:/www.typophile.com/node/30127 "http://www.typophile.com/node/30127") One of my posts to it seems relevant: _slab serif and egyptian are synonyms,_ That’s always beeen my understanding. I named my slab-serif take on Bodoni “Egyptian” partly for that reason, as well as others, but most logically because the serifs don’t look at all like slabs in the Light and Thin weights. But for encompassing a variety of typefaces, rather than weights, “Slab” is more practical, which is no doubt why the FontBook uses it as a category, to include many hefty-serifed faces (eg for news text) that are not at all “Egyptian”.
Anonymous @ 25 Oct 2007 — 12:15pm
The official rule for slab serifs (which I am about to make up) is that they have thick serifs, at least 50% as thick as the narrowest part of stem and other strokes, and no bracketting. Some people will call a Clarendon a slab serif (it fits, but has brackets), but they are the type of people who would steal sheep.
