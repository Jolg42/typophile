I have been searching Garalde typefaces looking for similar k structure (ignoring the flip leg), and it seems that Zapf and Goudy have used similar leg placement in some of their typefaces. The lower leg of the k connects close to the stem, but on the upper leg. Zapf’s Aldus and Zapf Renaissance look pretty close to the k used in the logo. The initial f looks very customized, and it surprises me that a hair salon would have a custom logo, unless this is a large chain of shops. - Mike Yanega
Anonymous @ 28.May.2009 12.08pm
I guess my conclusion is that this is certainly a custom logo. If I had to pick a starting point, I would use [Zapf Renaissance Antiqua](http://web.archive.org/web/20090607101728/http:/new.myfonts.com/fonts/efscangraphic/zapf-renaissance-antiqua-sh/), and I would shorten the l so that the serifs on top of the l and k are stepped, such that the top edges of the serifs form a straight line. Then I would clip off the leg of the italic k and transplant it on the Roman k (and then probably tweak it a lot). The f would be a challenge for me, because I have no skill at this (obviously), but I know this would be relatively simple work for any one of the font designers who hang out here. Your original question was whether this was a font or custom, and I vote the latter. - Mike Yanega
