Hi!

Any information would be appreciated. I could’nt find anything useful about this topic here in Europe.

This may just be where I grew up but in Pittsburgh during the 50s, we called car nameplates "chrome script." An older guy from Detroit (even older than me) several years ago explained that the joined nature of the heavy script was just due to the technical manufacturing aspect. It was just one piece to cast and only needed 2 rivots to mount on the car. Today, they can do anything so there is no need for that style. It is fun to look at though! ChrisL

Ralf, you'll find more about this in the book called ['Streamline: American Art Deco'](http://web.archive.org/web/20131115035738/http:/www.amazon.com/Streamline-American-Deco-S-Heller/dp/0811806626) by Steven Heller and Louise Fili. I think it says that 'streamline' referred to mid-century American Modernism and included architectural and product design. Chronicle Books may have incorporated it as a chapter into a newer book called Euro Deco, but I'm not sure. The original is out of print, but good reference. You're right about the relationship to neon signs; motel signs in particular.
