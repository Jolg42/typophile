Anonymous @ 4.May.2007 5.25am
Here’s how I do it. Start from your Italic outlines. Since the point placement and structure will be pretty much the same, you’ll be in a much better starting position. I put a copy of the original Italic in the template/mask/background (different terms, same thing, depending upon your software) of the Bold Italic, to compare with. I also keep open a glyph window from my Bold, with a copy of the Regular in the template/mask/background. Using visual comparison, the relationships between your Bold Italic and Italic should be similar to the relationships between your Bold and Regular. So, for example, you might center a Regular ’o’ behind the Bold ’o’, and then you’ll have a guide for how much weight to add to your Italic ’o’ and how much you should add inside and how much outside. You can keep shifting the relationship to check different aspects. For instance, I will usually position the ’n’ centered on the counter and compare, then aligned on the right side of left stem and compare again. This is how I use the Bold for visual reference. I believe it is essentially the same kind of thing that David is talking about. — K.
