Anonymous @ 22 Dec 2005 — 6:34pm
If anything, you should consider the Typowiki as a growing resource for all things typographic and type related, and as a resource it should be accessible and understandable to visitors. Personally, I see no need for many sub-classifications, since for the most part, people involved with type are often multi-disciplined. How many time would you enter Hermann Zapf? Type designer, type educator, typographer, calligrapher, graphic designer (he has done it), husband, etc.? What I do see lacking in our "Designers" section are people who have made significant contributions to the futherment of type in aesthetics, education, scholarly-research, marketing, etc., yet have never designed an actual typeface. The majority of individuals listed in the "Designers" category are actual type designers, a few punchcutters, and a scattering of "typophiles" such as [Roger Black](http://web.archive.org/web/20130903083945/http:/www.fontbureau.com/people/RogerBlack), who I don't recall ever actually designed a typeface despite his incredible contribution to the world of type. Likewise, a person like Aaron Burns (I "Typowikied" him but can't find it) should be listed, but not under "Designers." So if anything, I see two basic and clear catergories: - **Type Designers** Individuals who have a typeface(s) attributed to their design, along with any other of their type related activities. and - **Typophiles** (for lack of a better all inclusive definition) Which is for individuals who have NOT actually designed a typeface, but have made significant contributions to the world of type. Just my humble opinion.
