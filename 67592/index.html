Lineto's **Le Corbusier** is indeed drawn in the style of a »Didone« or Modernist/Romantic letter. <dl> <dt>For a <strong>Bodoni</strong> that is suitable for extended text, you have three good choices:</dt> <dd><a class="freelinking external" href="http://web.archive.org/web/20130718080729/http:/www.stonetypefoundry.com/itcbodoniovervie.html" rel="nofollow">ITC Bodoni</a></dd> <dd><a class="freelinking external" href="http://web.archive.org/web/20130718080729/http:/www.linotype.com/118/bauerbodoni-family.html" rel="nofollow">Bauer Bodoni</a></dd> <dd><a class="freelinking external" href="http://web.archive.org/web/20130718080729/http:/www.bertholdtypes.com/bq_library/290005.html" rel="nofollow">Berthold Bodoni</a></dd> </dl> The last two date from 1924–26 and 1930, respectively; a period when Le Corbusier was active. Other Romantic faces would include **Didot** and **Walbaum**. However, you specifically mentioned that you are looking for a sans serif. Here the task is more difficult, but it is not inconceivable to pair a Romantic Modernist serif with a Geometric Modernist sans serif such as **Futura** (c. 1925) or even **[Memphis](http://web.archive.org/web/20130718080729/http:/new.myfonts.com/fonts/adobe/memphis/)** (c. 1929). Better still, **[Akzidenz Grotesk](http://web.archive.org/web/20130718080729/http:/www.bertholdtypes.com/bq_library/AkzidenzGrotesk.html)** traces its geneology to the letterforms of Walbaum, and you may have an easier time harmonizing it with Le Corbusier.
