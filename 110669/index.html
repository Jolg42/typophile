Probably lettering in a popular style of the early 20th century, rather than a specific font. Note the tilde shape for the center stroke on E. Here is a somewhat similar digital font called Jonquin [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/gatf/jonquin/](http://web.archive.org/web/20140802204320/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/gatf/jonquin/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/gatf/jonquin/") by a British foundry, Greater Albion Typefounders, which is "inspired by some hand lettering seen on a World -War One recruiting poster." Move the tilde stroke to mid level and do some other relatively minor tinkering and you are in the ballpark for your lettering. Other digital fonts with the E tilde include Tenebra by František Štorm [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/storm/tenebra/](http://web.archive.org/web/20140802204320/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/storm/tenebra/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/storm/tenebra/") The E tilde is in the lowercase. For more information on fonts with the tilde see: [http://typophile.com/node/103815](http://web.archive.org/web/20140802204320/http:/typophile.com/node/103815 "http://typophile.com/node/103815") Don

You're welcome Matthew. There are some digital fonts with the flavor of the lettering of the era shown in your image. They lack the tilde feature and would need to be expanded in width. 1. Two independent digitizations of the another old font, name unknown: Milton by Mecanorma [demibold] [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/itfmecanorma/milton/](http://web.archive.org/web/20140802204320/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/itfmecanorma/milton/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/itfmecanorma/milton/") Musketeer by Tony Geddes [demibold, light & regular] [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/mti/musketeer/](http://web.archive.org/web/20140802204320/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/mti/musketeer/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/mti/musketeer/") 2. Kleukens Antiqua typeface designed by Friedrich Wilhelm Kleukens in 1910 for Bauer [http://typophile.com/node/52222](http://web.archive.org/web/20140802204320/http:/typophile.com/node/52222 "http://typophile.com/node/52222") Sold under the name Trieste EF by Eisner & Flake. Veronika Elsner & Günther Flake did bold, medium & light weights [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/ef/trieste/](http://web.archive.org/web/20140802204320/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/ef/trieste/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/ef/trieste/") Also Trieste SB by Scangraphic, bold, medium & light weights [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/efscangraphic/trieste-sb/](http://web.archive.org/web/20140802204320/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/efscangraphic/trieste-sb/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/efscangraphic/trieste-sb/") Nick Curtis has a medium weight sold under the name Kleukens Antiqua NF. [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/nicksfonts/kleukens-antiqua-nf/](http://web.archive.org/web/20140802204320/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/nicksfonts/kleukens-antiqua-nf/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/nicksfonts/kleukens-antiqua-nf/") 3. Other fonts with some similarity and based on sources from the era include Windsor, P22 PanAm, and Edwardian. Don
