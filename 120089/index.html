Anonymous @ 2 Oct 2014 — 1:09am
Yes, in 1984 some books were typed on an IBM Selectric typewriter, or a similar good quality typewriter from another source and the results were photographed for printing. Note the handwritten Greek phrase. We can check the design against some of the popular typefaces used on IBM "elements" -- the thing that looks like a golfball -- and see if it is digitized. They had names like \_courier\_, \_prestige\_, \_elite\_ etc. Here is a selection of these typefaces compiled by Luc Devroye [http://luc.devroye.org/fonts-44934.html](http://web.archive.org/web/20150317120026/http:/luc.devroye.org/fonts-44934.html "http://luc.devroye.org/fonts-44934.html") If you can find some of the distinctive letters in the book, like \_Q\_, \_R\_, \_g\_ and \_3\_ it makes it easier to decide on a match. Don

Thanks Guillaume for the link to the Delegate specimen. I have fond memories of Delegate. It produced a presentation quality document with a subtle variation in stroke weight that made it much more attractive for publication than most typewriter typefaces.
