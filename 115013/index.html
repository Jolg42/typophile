The Concept of Stripping Down the Black Terminals?

Previously, I started a thread about The Concept of Stripping Down the Serifs.
Now, let’s go back to time, back to the Middle Age.
I still wonder who first conceives sans serif typefaces based on blackletter calligraphy? Should I say Blackletter Sans , in other words, blackletter without terminals.
William Morris, Rudolf Koch or Tom Carnase and his Honda?
Some recent designers work and exploit geometric moduls combined with a heavy weight which give the flavor of blackletter: see Trigot or Griffensee.
Fell free to give your interpretation. Thanks for your input.

William Morris' [_Troy_](http://web.archive.org/web/20140804024923/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/p22/morris/troy/) fits the actual concept of **Neo-Blackletter** or **Blackletter Sans**. Tom Carnase's [_ITC Honda_](http://web.archive.org/web/20140804024923/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/linotype/honda/) (1970) has not only the **Blackletter Sans** leaning but combined with the weight of a slab serif/fat face if you catch my meaning. ![](http://web.archive.org/web/20140804024923im_/http:/typophile.com/files/honda_6736.gif) I found another lead concerning the concept through Eric Gill's [_Jubilee_](http://web.archive.org/web/20140804024923/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/redrooster/jubilee-rr/).
