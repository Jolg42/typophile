This looks like an embroidery font that does not seem to match any retail font. It seems to have been based on 19th century fonts styles ranging from "Antique" [Egyptian] to Clarendon. The serif treatment on \_S\_ seems to be the key to the source fonts. Perhaps the closest source might be Kinghorn 205 Med ![](http://web.archive.org/web/20150201021400im_/http:/typophile.com/files/Kinghorn205MedAsHMNZS_4685.gif) [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/talbot/kinghorn-205/](http://web.archive.org/web/20150201021400/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/talbot/kinghorn-205/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/talbot/kinghorn-205/") which has strokes and serifs all of a roughly equal weight similar to the lettering in the Cap Tallie. There is also a condensed version. The reverse contrast -- top and bottom thicker than sides -- shown in the top of \_S\_ in the Cap Tallie might be a quirk of that the digital instructions for that letter. Also consider Antique Central Heavy ![](http://web.archive.org/web/20150201021400im_/http:/typophile.com/files/AntiqueCentralHeavyAsHMNZS_5180.gif) [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/aboutype/antique-central/](http://web.archive.org/web/20150201021400/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/aboutype/antique-central/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/aboutype/antique-central/") if you want reverse contrast you could consider ![](http://web.archive.org/web/20150201021400im_/http:/typophile.com/files/PTBarnumAsHMNZS_5815.gif) [http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/bitstream/p-t-barnum/](http://web.archive.org/web/20150201021400/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/bitstream/p-t-barnum/ "http://www.myfonts.com/fonts/bitstream/p-t-barnum/") which suggests the \_S\_ but the vertical strokes are too thin.
