Have a look at [OurType](http://web.archive.org/web/20080409024943/http:/www.ourtype.be/)’s Parry. It comes in normal, semibold, bold and even black, each with accompanying italics. The proper term would be a [slab serif](http://web.archive.org/web/20080409024943/http:/www.typophile.com/wiki/slab%252Bserif) or Egyptienne.

Why not go in a slightly different direction if you want contemporary? [Fedra bold](http://web.archive.org/web/20080409024943/http:/www.typotheque.com/fedra_serif_a_bold/) by Typotheque is about as contemporary as you can get. I’m just not sure that slabs based on the Clarendon model feel particularly contemporary, though that would also depend on the usage. Of course, if you want to get crazy with a slab, try Storm’s [Farao](http://web.archive.org/web/20080409024943/http:/www.stormtype.com/typefaces-fonts-shop/typefaces-199-farao-black).
