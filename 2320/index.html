First of all, I found your **g** William! Read on ... forgive the rant. I've got to speak up here. If somebody who knew no better were to read this thread as it stands, they'd leave thinking that URW is a scumbag font pirate company. I'm _really_ not trying to be contentious here, but I'd like to clarify some things. Yves, you lumped URW into the same paragraph (next sentence, in fact) with "those quick-steal-jobbies" and then "all they did was change the most distinctive characters into something else, slant the roman instead of providing true italics, digitally extrapolate some extra weights, maybe add an outline or rounded version, and bingo: 1000 fonts for $24.95 whattadeal!". This effectively equates them with Southern Software or Bay Animation or any of those other crooks who renamed fonts and sold CD's full of them at a pittance. Now surely you weren't speaking of URW here. If so, I'd honestly like to learn what it's based upon. Specifically, [URW Agenda](http://web.archive.org/web/20071210203807/http:/www.urw.de/cgi-bin1/dalcgi/english/schnellsuche.htd?searchchar=agenda) has no italic, much less a false one or outline or rounded variants. I'm not aware of them doing this with other typefaces either. Secondly, Clearface Gothic is attributed to Morris Fuller Benton. URW also offers [Clearface Gothic](http://web.archive.org/web/20071210203807/http:/www.urw.de/cgi-bin1/dalcgi/english/schnellsuche.htd?searchchar=clearface) and gives proper attribution to Benton, 1906. So why offer Agenda as well, if it's meant as a thinly veiled clone? Agenda is attributed to [Phil Martin](http://web.archive.org/web/20071210203807/http:/www.urw.de/english/schrift/wahl7-set.htm), who, prior reading has led me to believe, was no slouch when it came to designing at least a few well known and well used fonts in an earlier era. [See here especially](http://web.archive.org/web/20071210203807/http:/mm2000s.net/index2.shtml). First hit the Font History button and be ready to hold your cursor over the Stop button in this awkward presentation (at least Agenda is the 2nd font shown). Mr. Martin makes a statement much like Mike's about the letter 'g'. Now hit the Font Graphic button and behold the very 'g' thought to be "homemade". So, Martin takes a 1906 typeface and, in 1976, makes several significant modifications to it. Absolutely he ought to have credited Benton. Two years later, Victor Caruso takes that same old typeface and digitizes it. My beef here is that, while I detest the folks like Southern Software who steal, rename and sell, there are so many gray areas to this topic. What occurred with Agenda has occurred to varying degrees with many, many font foundries, some of them large and well respected. An existing typeface was modified, sometimes precious little and called something new. Even worse, in my opinion, has been perpetrated: [1](http://web.archive.org/web/20071210203807/http:/www.ms-studio.com/articles.html) & [2](http://web.archive.org/web/20071210203807/http:/www.ms-studio.com/articlesarialsia.html) (Thanks for these articles, Mark. I'm sorry if you didn't intend them to be used this way). My intent is definitely not to paint Monotype black, but to show lurkers who don't know better that ethical gray appears in lots of type foundries and to call, say, ITC and Monotype the good guys and, say, URW and Bitstream the bad doesn't do justice to any of them. I've seen, recently, Bitstream, Corel and, now, URW labelled flatly as fiends, as if it's a known fact

The point being, just as in Marks tale of the Arial's of Monotype, that foundries are just as determined to keep abreast of their competition as any other business is.
