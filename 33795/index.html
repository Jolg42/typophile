Microscale particles shaped like letters

http://news.com.com/2300-1008_3-6169351.html

“UCLA professor Thomas G. Mason and chemistry graduate student Carlos J. Hernandez produced particles that are just microns wide and shaped like each letter of the alphabet. Graduate student James Wilking used “laser tweezers” to pick up the letters ’U, C, L, A’ and move them in order.”

Using nanomanipulation to create logos dates back to 1990, when Eigler and Schweizer used a scanning tunneling microscope (STM) to etch the IBM logo onto a nickel substrate using just 35 atoms of Xenon. That and other images can be found at: [http://www.almaden.ibm.com/vis/stm/catalogue.html](http://web.archive.org/web/20071202182121/http:/www.almaden.ibm.com/vis/stm/catalogue.html "http://www.almaden.ibm.com/vis/stm/catalogue.html").
