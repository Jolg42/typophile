Anonymous @ 31 Oct 2008 — 4:06pm
The typeface looks quite interesting. The important omitted glyphs are as follows: Idotaccent (uni0130) and dotlessi (uni0131) (Turkish, discussed [here](http://web.archive.org/web/20150420101453/http:/typophile.com/node/13570)) Tcommaaccent and tcommaaccent (without Unicode reference, needed for Romanian) Eng (uni014A) and eng (uni014B) (needed for rare languages like Sami and some African languages) If becoming punctual, there are also 4 optional glyphs you should add, although in many typefaces they are not present - **[Aringacute/aringacute](http://web.archive.org/web/20150420101453/http:/typophile.com/node/35221)** and **Oslashacute/oslashacute** (although I'm too lazy to check the Unicode points for them). Also from WGL4 maybe you should add kgreenlandic (uni0131) and napostrophe (uni0149), although I haven't seen them in real use. The matter of adding CE charset is discussed in more detail [here](http://web.archive.org/web/20150420101453/http:/www.typophile.com/node/2764). Now about the quirks: I have some doubts regarding the ogonek placement in iogonek (Roman). Otherwise the diacritics placement looks ok to me.

Anonymous @ 5 Nov 2008 — 12:21pm
Aringacute and Oslashacute are almost obsolete, although still are used in some Danish grammars, dictionaries and other linguistic texts, perhaps also in some poetry (quoting John Hudson from **[here](http://web.archive.org/web/20150420101453/http:/typophile.com/node/35221)**). kgreenlandic (aka **kra** )and napostrophe is almost extinct, but I still see them often implemented in many typefaces. They are described in this **[PDF](http://web.archive.org/web/20150420101453/http:/www.evertype.com/alphabets/greenlandic.pdf)** by Michael Everson. Implementing it possibly would require to add also a specific Greenlandic uppercase K, as described **[here](http://web.archive.org/web/20150420101453/http:/typophile.com/node/35971)**. About the lack of Unicode reference of Tcommaaccent the mistake is mine (in my old FontLab codepages I found only the glyph name, not Unicode values). Should have checked more thoroughly. None of these glyphs are in heavy use; I mention them only because they still exist in [WGL4 specifications](http://web.archive.org/web/20150420101453/http:/www.microsoft.com/typography/otspec/WGL4.htm) (i.e. Microsoft cosider them still not totally extinct).
