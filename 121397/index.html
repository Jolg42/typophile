Deutsche Grammophon Script Logo

Here is the famous Deutsche Grammophon yellow logo with the crown of tulips, designed by Siemens advertising consultant Hans Domizlaff in 1949 or shortly thereafter, who perhaps also did the script.

The complete logo is attached.
I know it’s hand-lettered and not produced directly from a font. The repeating _e_ and _o_ are not identical.
But what font or fonts might have inspired the lettering?
And what fonts since then have this distinctive angular formal style?
Very interested in your ideas. Thanks.
Don

It occurred to me that this type of cursive lettering has some similarity to the angular cursive style of handwriting that was taught in German schools as Kurrentschrift [http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurrent](http://web.archive.org/web/20150317102614/http:/en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurrent "http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurrent") But Kurrent lacks the thick and thin character and is less regular. I wonder if there are any more fonts in the Cabarga Cursiva genre? Don
