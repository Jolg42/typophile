The most peculiar character is the |G|, most unusual in a style which tends to enclose all letter in a boxy shape. One of its inspirations could be the lettering of the old WPA posters. See, as references: [http://fontstruct.com/fontstructions/show/wpa\_gothic](http://web.archive.org/web/20150423013642/http:/fontstruct.com/fontstructions/show/wpa_gothic "http://fontstruct.com/fontstructions/show/wpa\_gothic") [http://fontstruct.com/fontstructions/show/wpa\_gothic\_deco](http://web.archive.org/web/20150423013642/http:/fontstruct.com/fontstructions/show/wpa_gothic_deco "http://fontstruct.com/fontstructions/show/wpa\_gothic\_deco")
Anonymous @ 23 Mar 2015 — 11:58am
I forgot to mention that this style is sometimes called gas-pipe. The link posted by Riccardo reminded me of this. As for the letters in the image this could be lettering rather than a font as such. It would not be out of place as a US WPA poster from the 1930s. Also note from the Chank Diesel interview that this style was popular among signmakers because it was so easy to execute. I recall seeing many such signs in my rambles across North America years ago that had these general features. Most of these are now gone, replaced by chain brands of little artistic character. IMO Liquorstore is a good choice for a substitute. Don

If I have to recreate it, I'd probably start with [Industry](http://web.archive.org/web/20150423013642/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/fort-foundry/industry) Black, [Purista](http://web.archive.org/web/20150423013642/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/suitcase/purista) or [Oxide](http://web.archive.org/web/20150423013642/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/fontfont/oxide-pro) mainly because of closer x-width and even if it means more work to do: recreating /A (upside-down /U) and /Y (/U bottom half + vertical stroke), chopping off some terminals from /S (+ making spine horizontal or completely redrawing it starting a /O quarter) and /G (+ double sided crossbar).
