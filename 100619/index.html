I've tried to focus my search on slab serifs with curved transition ( [Egyptienne](http://web.archive.org/web/20131018074051/http:/www.myfonts.com/search/egyptienne/)/ [Clarendon](http://web.archive.org/web/20131018074051/http:/www.myfonts.com/search/clarendon/fonts/)) with a varsity/college feeling. Here are some, matching only /P specifications, hoping there'd be at least one that could meet your expectations: [Egyptienne](http://web.archive.org/web/20131018074051/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/urw/egyptienne-2/), [Clarendon](http://web.archive.org/web/20131018074051/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/urw/clarendon/) (MT version [here](http://web.archive.org/web/20131018074051/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/mti/clarendon/)), [Xenia](http://web.archive.org/web/20131018074051/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/paratype/xenia/) (the only one with straight-legged /R, more "varsity" to me), [Gold Fever](http://web.archive.org/web/20131018074051/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/fontmesa/gold-fever/) ( [Bookman](http://web.archive.org/web/20131018074051/http:/www.myfonts.com/search/bookman) track I've decided not to follow because too far from varsity style to me) The other option would be to start with any geometric slab serif of your choice and rework transitions for each letters you need. Here is what I've done starting from [Rockwell](http://web.archive.org/web/20131018074051/http:/www.myfonts.com/fonts/mti/rockwell), reworking serif transition and recreating /O and /C (may be /R leg would have need some tweaking). ![](http://web.archive.org/web/20131018074051im_/http:/typophile.com/files/princeton_4921.png)
