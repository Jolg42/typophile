embedding

Hello all,

I just completed a large document (288 pgs) in In-Design 6 and tried to publish it to PDF. I know you heard this before. Adobe refused to embed the font, which of course is mandatory. The font is a TTF created in 2001.

I have written permission to use the font commercially and need to find a way to legally remove the do-not-embed restriction. I contacted the creator of the font, and he did not realize he even put in the restriction and does not know how to remove it. He does not even have the program he used to create the font. Yikes!

Changing the font would take weeks even though I only use it in chapter heads and drop caps. I am also afraid outlining would put things out of whack. It needs to work in print and in multiple electronic forms.

I have scoured the web and have tried all sorts of apps. Some did not work, and I could not even figure out how to use a few others.

Any ideas on how I can get myself out of this fix? I get addicted to fonts and will resist changing it since it so perfectly relates to the subject matter of the text.

Help.

Anonymous @ 15 Feb 2015 — 4:01am
Anthony, in general converting text to outlines is regarded as _circumventing_ the EULA. I believe the intention of most EULAs is not to forbid embedding the font _an sich_, but instead to forbid using the font at all inside a PDF. Therefore, you are violating perhaps not the letter of the EULA, but certainly its spirit. EULAs tend to lag behind technical possibilities -- that is, if 'just outline the fonts' becomes common practice, it'll surely get mentioned that is allowed/not allowed next time.

Here is what H & Co says about outlining: [http://www.typography.com/faq/question.php?faqID=100](http://web.archive.org/web/20150322050255/http:/www.typography.com/faq/question.php?faqID=100 "http://www.typography.com/faq/question.php?faqID=100") > The simplest way to supply a logo to a client is to convert it to outlines in a drawing program. This saves the client from needing to license and install fonts, and it guarantees that the logo will appear exactly as you designed it. > > If your client needs to install and use the fonts themselves, they’ll need to license the fonts for their own use, which they can do through this site. The problem with embedding the font is that it can be extracted from pdf or postscript files. However, with current technology, fonts need the opentype tables to produce a decent output and those tables are not embedded in printouts. Maybe I am wrong but I would expect that with current technology, trying to extract a font from a pdf file is outdated and that the problems with technological advances come more from web fonts and fonts for e-pub than from those for desktop publication.
