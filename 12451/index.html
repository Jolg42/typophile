Chad, Futura is a very accommodating typeface for that clean-modern look however I find myself using Officina or Frutiger often too. The trick I find is to get 2 typefaces that are a nice complement of each other and go from there. I know it is bad to limit yourself to 2 and by no means is that what I am saying, but for somebody in school it is a very good thing to see what the limitations of said face are. My 2 during school were Futura Family (regular & condensed) and Minion Pro, a very nice face however used quite often can create the same drone feeling that Times has created for itself. Anonymous @ 15 May 2005 — 11:01pm
Hve you looked at [Avenir Next](http://web.archive.org/web/20150405123227/http:/www.typophile.com/wiki/Avenir%20Next)? [Avenir](http://web.archive.org/web/20150405123227/http:/www.typophile.com/wiki/Avenir) is a typeface based on [Futura](http://web.archive.org/web/20150405123227/http:/www.typophile.com/wiki/Futura) that [Adrian Frutiger](http://web.archive.org/web/20150405123227/http:/www.typophile.com/wiki/Adrian%20Frutiger) designed in 1988. He had never been bappy with the cold, modern appearance of Futura, so he humanized it. In 2003, he and [Akira Kobayashi](http://web.archive.org/web/20150405123227/http:/www.typophile.com/wiki/Akira%20Kobayashi) redrew Avenir, creating Avenir Next, which has better distribution of weight across the family, small caps, old style figures, and condensed styles. No [true italics](http://web.archive.org/web/20150405123227/http:/www.typophile.com/wiki/true%20italics), though. I don't think that Frutiger was ever a fan of those. Here is the [URL to Avenir Next](http://web.archive.org/web/20150405123227/http:/www.linotype.com/90672/avenirnext-family.html), and here is one to the old [Avenir](http://web.archive.org/web/20150405123227/http:/www.linotype.com/72/avenir-family.html). \_\_ [www.typeoff.de](http://web.archive.org/web/20150405123227/http:/www.typeoff.de/ "www.typeoff.de")

Personally I think there is no excuse whatsoever for using Futura Condensed, as I truly think that face is an abomination, sorry for ranting. :-) If you want a clean-_truly_ modern-elegant look there are a number of contemporary alternatives that blow Futura, Frutiger and the lot right out of the water. I don't have time right now to give you a complete run-down, but for starters have a look at the very-forward-thinking-but-subtly-so sans serifs of every graphic designer's best kept secret [Psy/Ops](http://web.archive.org/web/20150405123227/http:/www.psyops.com/html/specimens.html), [MacRhino](http://web.archive.org/web/20150405123227/http:/www.macrhino.com/)'s quite narrow [Stalemate](http://web.archive.org/web/20150405123227/http:/www.fountain.nu/catalogue/stalemate.asp), [Eric Olson](http://web.archive.org/web/20150405123227/http:/www.processtypefoundry.com/)'s technical-yet-warm [Klavika](http://web.archive.org/web/20150405123227/http:/www.processtypefoundry.com/typefaces/klavika/index.html), [Chester](http://web.archive.org/web/20150405123227/http:/www.thirstype.com/designers.php?designer=chester)'s [Galaxie Polaris](http://web.archive.org/web/20150405123227/http:/www.thirstype.com/fonts.php?font=GalaxiePolaris), [Infinity](http://web.archive.org/web/20150405123227/http:/www.thirstype.com/fonts.php?font=Infinity) and [Apex Sans](http://web.archive.org/web/20150405123227/http:/www.thirstype.com/fonts.php?font=ApexSans), and [Luc(as) de Groot](http://web.archive.org/web/20150405123227/http:/www.lucasfonts.com/)'s complete output.

If you want a more conventional-looking alternative, particularly like Futura's small x-height _and_ need a condensed companion as well, Christian Schwartz's [Neutraface](http://web.archive.org/web/20150405123227/http:/www.houseindustries.com/index.php?page=showfont&id=18) and [Neutraface Condensed](http://web.archive.org/web/20150405123227/http:/www.houseindustries.com/index.php?page=showfont&id=94) are the perfect choice. It's got true italics, and can be used both in Futura- (Neutraface Alternate) and Avenir (Neutraface "regular")-mode -- two type families in one! :-)

Anonymous @ 16 May 2005 — 8:16am
Although I do still like Futura - the regular width, not the condensed - my current two favorite sans for a sense of modern elegance are [Auto 1](http://web.archive.org/web/20150405123227/http:/www.underware.nl/site2/index.php3?id1=auto&id2=animated&id3=allanimations) from Underware and [Magma](http://web.archive.org/web/20150405123227/http:/www.stonetypefoundry.com/html_pages/MagmaOverview.html) from StoneType. More daring yet still elegant are [Sophisto](http://web.archive.org/web/20150405123227/http:/www.psyops.com/html/spec_sophisto_ss.html) from MAC Rhino & PSY/OPS, [Faceplate Sans](http://web.archive.org/web/20150405123227/http:/www.psyops.com/html/spec_faceplate.html) from PSY/OPS and [Reykjavik One](http://web.archive.org/web/20150405123227/http:/www.psyops.com/html/spec_reykjavik1.html) also from PSY/OPS. All these faces are fairly narrow, especially Magma and Sophisto. chester's stuff, recommended by Yves below, is simply incredible. In particular, I reach for [GalaxiePolaris](http://web.archive.org/web/20150405123227/http:/thirstype.com/fonts.php?font=GalaxiePolaris) whenever I need a superbly stylish grotesk. [Stalemate](http://web.archive.org/web/20150405123227/http:/www.fountain.nu/catalogue/stalemate.asp) would have been on my list as well if Yves hadn't mentioned it already. [Vista Sans](http://web.archive.org/web/20150405123227/http:/www.emigre.com/EFfeature.php?di=200) is wonderful too, but beware. Even though designer Xavier Dupré says "the text weights are loosely spaced" I was shocked to discover just how loosely spaced they are - so much so that Vista Sans is not, in my view, a realistic choice for extended text at a bookish 9.5 points, for example. Magically, the text weights start to work and really are quite lovely starting at around 11 points - but they still seem better suited to shorter than to longer texts at this size as well. One final caveat on Vista: it is a very, very dark typeface. I usually have issues with the fact that many digital fonts are too light for my limited office toolset, but Vista is a little dark even for me. The book weight might be considered a regular in other fonts, the regular a medium, and the medium a bold. Regardless, I find Dupré's work amazing and am now saving my pennies for his FF Absara Sans, [reportedly](http://web.archive.org/web/20150405123227/http:/typographi.com/001000.php) due out from FontShop later this year.
